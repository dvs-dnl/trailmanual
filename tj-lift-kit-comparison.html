39 kits from 18 brands, with price, the fitment each seller names, what owners report after the install and what went wrong. Listed alphabetically, not ranked. Most of this log is what sellers claim, and we found no independent before-and-after test of a lifted TJ, so check each label before you trust a number.

Stock baselines

Start from your own rig. These are the factory numbers and the stock findings in the log.

  • Jeep launch press kit (marked preliminary) lists Rubicon running ground clearance 259 mm (10.2 in), wheelbase 93.4 in, 31-inch P245/75R16 tires, Dana 44 axles at 4.10 with selectable lockers and NV241OR 4.0:1 transfer case. 10.2 in running clearance Official record Stellantis Media (Rubicon press kit), 2002-2003
  • Same press kit: approach 45.1, ramp breakover 25.8 and departure 34.4 degrees. These are preliminary pre-production figures. 45.1 / 25.8 / 34.4 deg Official record Stellantis Media (Rubicon press kit), 2002-2003
  • Quadratec's 2003 spec page lists a single ground clearance of 8.2 in for all variants and a 93.4 in wheelbase; curb weights SE 3,235, X 3,407, Sport 3,450, Sahara 3,575, Rubicon 3,716 lb. 8.2 in ground clearance Retailer claim Quadratec 2003 TJ specs, 2026-10-30
  • Quadratec's 2005-06 page (citing a DaimlerChrysler release) lists axle clearance front 8.7 min to 9.2 max in, rear 8.6 to 9.1 in, running clearance 9.0 to 10.0 in, approach 42.2 and departure 31.5 degrees. The breakover value reads 226 (garbled) and the mm figures do not match the inch figures, so only the inch numbers are used. Running clearance 9.0-10.0 in Retailer claim Quadratec 2005-2006 TJ specs, 2026-10-30
  • Quadratec 2005-06 page lists Rubicon curb weight 3,832 lb (Sport 3,539, X 3,438, SE 3,200) and Rubicon stock tire as the Goodyear MT/R in a 31x9.5 / LT245/75R16 size; Sport standard tire is P225/75R15. 3,832 lb curb weight Retailer claim Quadratec 2005-2006 TJ specs, 2026-10-30
  • A club ramp event page (last updated March 1999) lists one heavily built TJ at RTI 599, second of nine, and stock-ish YJs lowest (273). The ramp is 30 degrees, so these are not comparable to 20-degree scores. 599 (built TJ) Owner report Flatlanders Jeep Club, 1999-03-16

Fitment shows only what a maker or seller names, so a kit can fit a trim its listing leaves out. Check the source before you buy. A range can include several shock or arm options. Prices are what the listing showed on the date checked. Many TJ kits showed as temporarily out of stock when we checked, so confirm a kit still ships before you plan around it.

Common issues after lifting a TJ

Each item below is one source's report, with its label. Owner reports are single experiences, not failure rates.

CV joints and front geometry

An independent front end changes CV and control arm angles as it lifts, so these findings matter more than the lift height.

  • Fabtech says Rubicons need a shortened front driveshaft with its 4in kit because of added travel; MetalCloak says a Rubicon with the fixed yoke takes a CV driveline directly, while non-Rubicons also need an SYE. Shorter front shaft (Fabtech); CV driveline (MetalCloak) Manufacturer claim ExtremeTerrain (Fabtech K4001), 2026-10-30
  • ExtremeTerrain's general Wrangler article ranks causes: front track bar, ball joints, drag link and tie rod ends, control arms, bushings, steering stabilizer; a stabilizer can mask the real fault; lifting reduces caster. Track bar first Reviewer opinion ExtremeTerrain death wobble article, 2019-06-12

Track bar and axle offset

A taller spring moves the axle sideways on its track bar. Some kits include an adjustable bar to re-center it.

  • TeraFlex says its 3in and 4in base TJ kits require adjustable control arms and/or an adjustable front track bar, and ships an HD adjustable front track bar in its 8-arm, Enduro and Pro LCG systems. Adjustable front track bar needed at 3in+ Manufacturer claim TeraFlex blog (Jason Udy), 2017-07-31
  • A TJ owner reports death wobble on a 2004 TJ was traced to a worn track bar bushing, fixed with a two-piece bushing set, then returned about six months later and the owner suspected the bar itself. Per search excerpt only; the post was not opened. Track bar bushing; recurred in 6 months Owner report WranglerTJForum post, undated

Death wobble, caster and steering

Lift changes the angle of the control arms. These findings cover what that does to steering feel.

  • Rough Country's TJ install guide says to have a shop realign the front end and to set caster to the factory minimum with toe-in at the maximum. Alignment required Manufacturer claim ExtremeTerrain (Rough Country install guide), 2026-10-30
  • Rough Country says its 4in TJ kit needs no longer brake lines unless the sway bars are disconnected or the lift is combined with another; it advises checking front hose slack with a steering sweep and rear hoses at full extension. Extended lines if sway bars disconnected Manufacturer claim ExtremeTerrain (Rough Country install guide), 2026-10-30

Driveshafts, vibration and the transfer case

Lift steepens the driveshaft angle. On short wheelbases that is often the first problem to show up.

  • Superlift's K843 page says a small percentage of 4in-lifted Jeeps (excluding the Rubicon) may need a slip yoke eliminator and a replacement driveshaft to cure vibration, which can only be confirmed after installation and a road test. SYE and driveshaft possible at 4in (non-Rubicon) Manufacturer claim Superlift K843 via LiftKits4Less, 2026-10-30
  • A Quadratec 3.5in kit owner (2023) reports no highway driveline vibration after fitting the transfer case drop and plans an SYE later, on a TJ with about 4.5in of lift including a 3/4in front spacer. No vibration with TCD at about 4.5 in Owner report Quadratec (Maximum Duty 3.5in TJ review), 2023-06-17
