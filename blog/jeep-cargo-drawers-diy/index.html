DIY Jeep Cargo Drawers: Plans for the TJ, XJ, JK and JL

A plywood drawer system is the most useful interior project on a trail rig, and on some Jeeps it is the only real option. The hard part is not the woodworking. It is that a 2-door JL and a JL Unlimited need different boxes, a factory subwoofer can take up the space you planned on, and the floor under an XJ hides a plastic fuel tank. Here is how the kits rate, then four builds with cut lists.

October 30, 2026 · 20 min read · Written and maintained by an AZ wheeler and driveway wrencher · How we source guides
The direct answer

Buy if you have a JL Unlimited, want it installed this weekend, and have no reason to change the layout. Build if you have an XJ, a 2-door, a factory subwoofer, or a specific fridge. Most owners should start with 6-inch side-by-side drawers: they stay about 9 inches tall, hold most recovery gear and tools, and leave room above. Choose 9-inch drawers if you rarely stack on top. Choose the half-depth version with a hinged rear bay if you carry long gear you rarely need. Whatever you build, bolt it down with real hardware, because this box becomes a projectile in a crash.

Measure your Jeep first

Jeep's published figures are measured at the trim and they do not agree with owners' tape measures, and owners do not agree with each other. Use the table to choose a starting box, then build a cardboard mockup in your own Jeep before cutting anything. Width has to clear two things: the wheel wells and the opening you carry it through, unless you plan to assemble it inside.

Traps by platform

  • TJ and LJ. A TJ has to lose the rear seat for most drawers. The LJ is the exception: the Tuffy 131-01 goes behind the LJ seat on factory holes, but the TJ needs the seat out. Some door frames have a 1 in lip that calls for a riser. A box near 36 in wide is tight: owners report 34.25 to 34.5 in with brackets.
  • XJ. Do not drill blind. One owner's floor mount ran into frame rails, the plastic fuel tank, filler hoses and shackle mounts. The spare is also inside: a full-width box means moving it, and owners report needing about 1.5 in of floor clearance for the latch.
  • JK. On a 2-door, plan on the seat out. On an Unlimited, an owner measured 43 in between fenders but 36.5 in at the factory sub. One owner found the floor anchors are M6x1.0 on a 2014 JKU, which is small for this job.
  • JL 2-door. We found no reliable seat-up depth, and owners describe it as very short. Build for seat-out, or look at the Goose Gear 2-door plate as a reference.
  • JL 4xe. The battery sits under the rear seat, and cargo volume drops to 67.4 cu ft folded. Owners describe the folded seat as a ledge about 7 in above the floor. Kit makers disagree on fit: Tuffy excludes it, while Rugged Ridge lists it.
  • JL 392. Goose Gear sells a separate 392 plate, with sub only, so do not assume a standard JLU plan transfers. Mock it up.

Look under the cargo floor panel before you buy a single sheet of plywood. Whether you have a sub depends on the audio package, not only the trim.

Three ways to deal with a JL or JK sub

  • Build around it. Narrow the drawer on the sub side, or raise the box on battens until it clears, and cut the base panel. The half-depth build below handles this best, because the hidden bay has no fixed bottom.
  • Relocate it. On an Unlimited JK, under-seat subs such as the Alpine PWE-S8-WRA replace it, though owners report seat vibration.
  • Remove it. JK owners report about 20 minutes with star bolts, and on a JL the connector is behind the passenger-side plastics. Removing only the sub is reported fine by one owner. Do not bypass the factory amp to do it, because it holds the tuning and volume control. Keep the parts: it is reversible.

XJ

We found no current commercial drawer kit. The JCR XJ Cubby Mate is no longer available, and Tuffy's 130-01 underseat drawer is discontinued. If you own an XJ, the rest of this article is your plan. One owner who adapted a Tuffy drawer had two slide screws pull through and replaced them all with M6 grade 10.9 flange bolts. Build that lesson in from the start.

Between the kit and a full build, a forum seller offers CNC-cut kits for $270 to $390, by the seller's own description. One owner estimated a DIY build at about two-thirds of the cost of a Goose Gear setup, tools included.

"Double" here means side by side. You can stack two 6-inch drawers instead, at about 16 in overall, but that takes more height than most Jeeps can spare with the seat up, and it puts weight higher. Side by side is the better default.

Materials and hardware

Slides: where to spend the money

Slides decide whether this project works after a year of washboard. Get a pair that locks closed, so the drawer stays in on a climb, and that locks open, so it does not roll back into you on a slope. ARB advertises an anti roll-back lock for the same reason. Check the slide's rating against what you plan to load, and treat unbranded ratings with caution.

Skip the Fulterer FR5000 for this: it is rated at 100 lb and sold as medium duty. The cut lists below assume slides 3/4 in thick. Check yours, and if it differs, change the drawer width by twice the difference.

Plywood

  • Use 1/2 in Baltic birch for boxes and 3/4 in for the deck and divider. Goose Gear uses 11-ply Baltic birch dovetail boxes. True Baltic birch has been reported as hard to find, so a void-free cabinet-grade sheet is a fair substitute.
  • Do not trust the glue to keep it dry. Sources conflict on whether Baltic birch is water-resistant, so seal it. One builder used spar varnish on 1/2 in birch.
  • Skip solid boards. An XJ owner reports solid 1x12 cracks and splits.

Fasteners and latches

  • Bolt the slides. Use machine screws through to flange nuts or T-nuts, not wood screws. That is the failure the XJ owner above had.
  • Latches. Southco's 64-series paddle latches come non-locking (64-21-10) and key-locking (64-22-10) in stainless. Recess them in the drawer face so the face stays flat. We found no published vibration rating, so test yours on a rough road with the drawer loaded.
  • Quiet it. Owners of steel drawers fix rattle with foam weatherstrip and nylon spacers. Add both from day one.

Power and tie-down

  • Use the fuse printed on your fridge's plug label. Dometic's support page says the value varies.
  • One retailer recommends 12 AWG for a DIY circuit to a CFX3 45. Size your run with the wire gauge calculator, and fuse it to protect the wire.
  • Strap the fridge to the slide. Dometic's slide includes four straps. A loose fridge is one of the heavier objects in the Jeep.

Build D: half-depth drawers and a hinged rear bay

The drawers take the half nearest the tailgate, and a hinged lid covers the half nearest the seat. You get quick access at the back and a protected bay for gear you rarely touch, such as traction boards, a jack, straps and a compressor. It also suits a rig with a floor sub, since the bay has no fixed bottom and can sit over it. Measure the sub's height first: the bay has 8 1/4 in under the lid.

  • Latch the lid. Fit a latch at the front edge, so it stays shut in a rollover or a hard stop. An unlatched lid lets everything in the bay out.
  • Check the arc. A 14 in lid needs about 14 in of clear height above the deck, and a hardtop or cargo cover can get in the way. Mock up the swing with cardboard.
  • Check the reach. You reach over the drawer section into the bay. Put the heavy items at the drawer end of the bay, and pull a drawer out to stand closer if your bumper allows. On a rig with a folding seat, you can also reach it from the back seat.
  • Hold gear down inside. Add tie-down points in the bay, since it has no drawers to contain things.

Anchoring: the part you cannot skip

A loaded drawer box sits directly behind your passengers, so this section matters more than any joint. Crash forces are far higher than the load's weight. The seat standard uses 20 times the seat's mass as a forward load, and a European luggage test runs two 18 kg blocks at 20 g. Researchers found that test unrealistically light, and crash tests still wrecked seat backs.

By our arithmetic, a 200 lb loaded box at 20 g is about 4,000 lbf, treated as a static load. That is not a design standard. It is a sense of scale. We found no drawer-specific US standard, and we cannot tell you a home-built system is crash-safe.

  • Never use wood screws or sheet metal screws into the floor. They have little thread engagement in thin sheet.
  • Through-bolt with backing plates wherever you can see both sides, as Tuffy's TJ drawer does with four 7/16 x 1-1/4 in bolts through the floor. Look underneath first, and on an XJ especially.
  • Reuse factory anchor holes as locating points, and verify the thread yourself. Owner reports vary: M6 on a JK, and M8-1.25 button heads on one JL rail install, which may not be the factory thread. A M6 tie-down was designed to hold a tie-down loop, not your whole system. Use several, and add a through-bolt if you can.
  • Rivnuts are not magic. One reference gives about 510 lbf for a 1/4-20 in 0.030 in steel, and about 2,090 lbf in 0.125 in. Thin floor metal changes the answer.
  • Do not bolt to seat belt anchors unless you know what that does to the belt. We found no source saying it is safe, though one owner has done it.
  • Keep the load low and behind the seat back, and add a cargo barrier if you carry anything above it.
  • Add up the weight. Read the door sticker for your payload, because published figures conflict: Quadratec lists 1,000 lb for 2007-11 JKs, and another of its pages cites 700 lb for a 2012 Rubicon. The box, the fridge, your gear and your passengers all count against it.

Build order

  1. Check the floor. Pull the carpet or panel, find any sub, the anchors and whatever sits below the floor.
  2. Mock it up in cardboard. Include the drawer in its extended position, seat recline, gate swing, and latch clearance. Check it fits through the opening if you plan to build it outside the Jeep.
  3. Buy the slides before cutting. Their real thickness and length set the drawer width and depth.
  4. Build the carcass. Glue and screw the base, sides, divider and back, then check it is square before the glue sets. A racked box binds the slides.
  5. Mount the slides. Bolt them level and parallel, using spacers wherever the slide does not sit on a flat panel.
  6. Build and fit the drawers. Cycle each one fully, empty and loaded, before adding the faces. Attach faces last, with a 1/8 in gap all around.
  7. Seal everything, including the cut edges, and let it cure before it goes in the Jeep.
  8. Fit the deck so you can reach the slide bolts later. Add carpet or a rubber mat, plus tie-down points.
  9. Anchor it, and test on a rough road with a full load. Then re-check every bolt after the first trail run.

Common questions

Is it cheaper to build Jeep cargo drawers than to buy them?

Usually, going by owner estimates. JL and JK kits run from about $450 to about $2,500. On the XJ, building is the realistic option, because we found no current commercial kit.

Will a drawer fit over the factory JL subwoofer?

Only if it is built for it. The Alpine sub sits in the cargo floor. Some products have a sub version and some exclude it, so check the listing or build around it.

What drawer height should I build?

6-inch drawers keep the deck low at about 9 inches overall and leave room for a fridge on top. 9-inch drawers bring the deck to about 12 inches and hold tall items, but make a top-mounted fridge impractical on an XJ.

How solid is this information?

Dimensions, fit and owner reviews come mainly from listings and owner forums, and several forum sources could only be read as search excerpts. Measurements conflict, so we show them as starting points. We did not build each of these on each Jeep. We did not find reliable numbers for the JL's tie-down thread, its exact sub size, its seat fold angle, or a 2-door's seat-up depth, so measure those yourself. Cut-list dimensions are computed for the reference boxes shown.
