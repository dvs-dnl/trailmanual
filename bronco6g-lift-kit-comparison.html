91 kits from 28 brands, with price, the fitment each seller names, what owners report after the install and what went wrong. Listed alphabetically, not ranked. Most of this log is what sellers claim, and we found no independent before-and-after test of a lifted Bronco, so check each label before you trust a number.

Stock baselines

Start from your own rig. These are the factory numbers and the stock findings in the log.

  • SAE's engineering write-up describes independent front suspension and a solid Dana 44 AdvanTek rear axle on a 5-link setup with outboard coil springs and a Panhard rod. IFS front, 5-link coil rear Reviewer opinion Mobility Engineering Technology (SAE), 2021-07-01
  • Ford's Eric Loeffler claims 17 percent more front articulation and 10 percent more rear than Wrangler. The article notes live axles usually score higher on RTI, and no Wrangler trim or test method is given. 17 percent front, 10 percent rear (Ford's figure) Manufacturer claim Mobility Engineering Technology (SAE), 2021-07-01
  • Autoblog gives 8.3 to 13.1 in depending on trim. It does not break the range out by door count. 8.3 to 13.1 in Reviewer opinion Autoblog
  • New Atlas reports 11.5 in with the Sasquatch package, and a Bronco6G poster quotes Ford at 11.5 in with 35s. Published elsewhere as 11.6 in, so treat either as a claim. 11.5 in Reviewer opinion New Atlas
  • HOSS 1.0 is the black-shock setup on Base, Big Bend, Outer Banks and Black Diamond without Sasquatch. HOSS 2.0 has yellow Bilstein shocks and comes with Sasquatch on those trims and is standard on Badlands, Wildtrak, First Edition and Everglades. Check shock color before buying any kit. Black shocks HOSS 1.0, yellow HOSS 2.0 Retailer claim Shock Surplus ICON listings, 2026-10-30
  • Zone says the Sasquatch package adds 1.1 in of ride height and that the Bronco clears up to 35 in from the factory. 1.1 in taller with Sasquatch Manufacturer claim Zone Offroad, 2026-10-30
  • Forum chart attributed to AccuTune Offroad: factory Sasquatch Bilstein is 17.0625 in compressed and 23.3125 in extended at the front, 18.625 and 26.125 at the rear. Shop figures relayed on a forum, not an independent test. 6.25 in front, 7.5 in rear shock travel Retailer claim Bronco6G (AccuTune chart), 2023-05-23

Common issues after lifting a Bronco

Each item below is one source's report, with its label. Owner reports are single experiences, not failure rates.

CV joints and front geometry

An independent front end changes CV and control arm angles as it lifts, so these findings matter more than the lift height.

  • AccuTune Offroad posted that front CV joints bound at 24.6 in of shock extension, did not at 24.3 in, with stock Sasquatch shocks at 23.3 in, and that King front lengths ran a bit too long. A shop's figures on a forum, no method stated. CV bind at 24.6 in, not at 24.3 in Retailer claim Bronco6G (AccuTune post), 2023-05-23
  • Shock Surplus compared Fox and King coilovers: King cycles more travel, with what it calls the slightest CV bind at full extension, and King tires can hit at full bump on 37s. The retailer sells both. King: slight CV bind at full extension Retailer claim Shock Surplus blog
  • JKS says the factory steering tie rod ends have proven a weak point with larger tires, failing at the threaded rod, and sells a sleeve kit (JKS9700) for them. A maker selling the fix, with no failure count given. Tie rod weak with larger tires (seller claim) Manufacturer claim JKS Manufacturing, 2026-10-30
  • Owner's front passenger axle broke at the CV joint on a gravel pad. The first dealer denied warranty citing the lift and non-OE tires; the owner estimated $1,100 for the axle and says Ford later helped get a new one. Replies argue over whether the spacer or driver input caused it. Dealer denied CV axle claim Owner report Bronco6G, 2022-01-18

Caster and steering

Lift changes the angle of the control arms. These findings cover what that does to steering feel.

  • Owner reports the truck felt sketchy in curves after a dealer-installed Rough Country 2 in lift. Replies blame caster, a poor alignment, a loose sway bar or higher center of gravity; the first page of the thread reports no fix. Sketchy in curves, cause unresolved Owner report Bronco6G, 2022-04-08

Spacer sag and ride

  • Forum estimates put the front gain at about 1.5 times top spacer thickness and the rear at about 1:1. AccuTune's own rule is 1/2 in of top hat spacer for about 1 in at the wheel, with a don't-hold-me-to-it caveat. None of this is measured. About 1.5 to 2 times at front (estimate) Owner report Bronco6G, 2023-05-29

Tires, fenders and gearing

  • Owner put only the lower half of a Zone 1 in kit up front and says it sits level; he says the full 1 in would leave the front higher than the rear. Ran 35s on +18 wheels for a week of trails with no rubbing noticed. Half kit levels a non-SAS Badlands Owner report Bronco6G, 2022-11-06
  • Owner with a 2 in level and 35x12.50R17 tires reports no rub, but says the crash bars had to come off. Another owner runs 35x11.5 on a stock height Base 2-door with no street issues. No lift measurement given. Crash bars removed for 35s Owner report Bronco6G, 2022-01-15

Other reports

  • Owner had a garage install the kit so the warranty applied, at about $500 labor plus an alignment still to come. He calls the on-road ride very good but not softer than stock. The tech stated 2 in; the owner took no before or after measurement, and Ford publishes no travel figures that he could find. About $500 labor, ride good, lift unmeasured Owner report Bronco6G, 2022-12-13
  • Owner praises the install and ride and says he took out the stock UCAs for Elevate arms alongside this kit. Owner added aftermarket UCAs Owner report ExtremeTerrain reviews

Findings for any Bronco lift

  • Shock Surplus says the factory HOSS 2.0 shocks are firm over small bumps and that the internal jounce stops can feel bouncy, especially in the rear of a short-wheelbase two-door. A seller's view, from its own Johnson Valley drive. Two-door rear feels busiest Retailer claim Shock Surplus blog
  • ExtremeTerrain lists 47-325593 as a 2-door front kit, while a ThreePiece buyer's guide lists the same number as a rear kit. Confirm with Bilstein before ordering. Front or rear disagreement Retailer claim ThreePiece, 2026-09-03
