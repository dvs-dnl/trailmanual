Ford F-150 Raptor · Buyer's Guide

Buying a Ford F-150 Raptor

Four generations, three powertrains, one clear purpose. The used market has all of them at very different prices. Here's what each one actually is — and what to look for on the lot.

Trail Manual·F-150 Raptor · 2010–Present

The Honest Summary

Bottom line

Gen 2 (2017–2020) is the used market sweet spot. It offers the biggest performance jump from Gen 1, the EcoBoost is proven at this mileage, and the Live Valve shocks are well understood. Inspect cold-start cam phaser behavior carefully. The Raptor R is the best truck on this list, full stop — if you can afford $90,000+. Gen 1 SVT is a valid buy for anyone who wants a naturally-aspirated engine and doesn't mind the fuel bill. Gen 3 base is hard to justify unless it has the 37 Package.

Generation Breakdown

2010–2014
Gen 1 SVT

6.2L Naturally-Aspirated V8

411 hp, 434 lb-ft. Ford SVT built this one. Simpler mechanically — no turbos, no intercooler, no cam phasers. Fox internal-bypass shocks without Live Valve. Expect 11–14 MPG. Used: $28,000–$45,000 for clean examples. The simplicity has value if you're willing to pay for fuel.

2017–2020
Gen 2 — Sweet Spot

3.5L Twin-Turbo EcoBoost

450 hp, 510 lb-ft. Fox Live Valve shocks arrive here. Baja mode. Meaningfully faster than Gen 1 in every performance metric. The cam phaser issue is real on 2017–2018 trucks — inspect cold. Used: $42,000–$62,000 for clean examples. This is the buy.

2021–Present
Gen 3 Base

Updated 3.5L EcoBoost

450 hp (recalibrated), improved Fox Live Valve, optional 37 Package (37" BFG, 4.10 gears, Brembo brakes). The 37 Package is worth the premium. Base Gen 3 without it is hard to justify over a well-inspected Gen 2 at the current price delta. Used: $58,000–$80,000.

2023–Present
Raptor R

5.2L Supercharged Predator V8

700 hp. Same engine family as the Mustang Shelby GT500, detuned for truck use. The most capable Raptor ever built. MSRP ~$109,000 new; used examples trade at $90,000–$120,000+. The R is extraordinary — it's simply in its own price category.

What to Inspect

  • Cold-start cam phaser behavior (Gen 2, 2017–2018) Start the engine cold and listen for 45–60 seconds. A grinding or clattering sound from the top of the engine that disappears as it warms is cam phaser rattle. Ford issued TSBs (including TSB 19-2345) covering revised phasers. Out-of-warranty repair: $2,500–$4,500. Walk away from a rattling truck unless you're pricing in the repair.
  • Fox shock condition — all generations Look for oil tracking on all four shock bodies. Gen 2/3 Live Valve shocks need rebuilds at 20,000–30,000 hard-use miles. A full rebuild at a Fox authorized shop runs $1,200–$2,000 for all four. A truck that has been hard-run with no shock service history needs a rebuild in your cost calculation.
  • Rear spring sag (Gen 2) Hard desert use causes the Gen 2 rear leaf springs to take a set. A truck sitting tail-low or bottoming easily on moderate terrain may have sagged springs. Re-arch: $350–$550. Full spring pack replacement: $750–$1,400. Either way, it's in your cost calculation.
  • Terrain Management System and Baja mode Cycle through all modes during the test drive. Baja mode in particular — verify the transmission and throttle respond correctly. Any fault codes stored around terrain mode engagement warrant investigation before purchase.
  • Intercooler plumbing (Gen 2/3) Inspect the charge air cooler hose connections on the 3.5L EcoBoost. The lower intercooler hose can blow off under sustained boost. Look for oil residue near the hose connections — that's boost leak evidence.
  • Skid plate and underbody condition The Raptor sees hard use. Inspect the factory skid plates for significant bending or cracking. Light scraping is expected — bent or cracked skids on a truck represent actual hard impact. Look underneath at the frame rails and transmission crossmember.
  • 37 Package verification (Gen 3) If the seller claims the 37 Package: verify via window sticker or build sheet. Confirm the Brembo calipers are present (red, Brembo casting visible) and verify the axle gear ratio via the differential fill tag or vehicle build data. Don't pay 37 Package premium for a truck without it.
  • Suspension fastener torque On a hard-used truck, control arm bolts, ball joint hardware, and shock mount fasteners can loosen. A shop inspection that includes a torque check on suspension fasteners is worth the cost on any Raptor with documented hard use.

Watch List

  • 2017–2018 cam phaser rattle on Gen 2 The highest-risk window for the cam phaser issue. Never buy a 2017 or early 2018 truck without verifying cold-start behavior in person. A cleared code in the OBD history that the seller didn't disclose is also possible — worth having a scanner present.
  • Shock service history missing on hard-use trucks Any Raptor with documented desert or competition use that can't show shock service records needs a Fox-authorized inspection before purchase. Worn Live Valve shocks on a truck that's supposed to be stable at speed are a safety issue, not a comfort complaint.
  • Lifted trucks with unknown suspension work Aftermarket lifts on a Raptor can ruin the Fox shock tune if the geometry changes weren't accounted for. Inspect lifted trucks carefully — verify what was installed and whether the Fox recalibration was done. A Raptor with a botched lift can be genuinely unsafe at speed.
  • Gen 3 priced as 37 Package without the hardware Some sellers market any Gen 3 as "37 Package spec" because it looks similar. The 4.10 gears and Brembo brakes are what make it the 37 Package. Visual inspection of the caliper and gear ratio verification are the checks.
