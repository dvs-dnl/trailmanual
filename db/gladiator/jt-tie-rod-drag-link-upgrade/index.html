Jeep Gladiator JT / Steering

Tie Rod and Drag Link Upgrade for the Gladiator JT: Parts, Axle Width, and Torque

Difficulty 3/51–3 hrs$468–11332020-2026Jump to steps
Short answer

Replace the factory tie rod and drag link when one is bent or worn, or when 37s and hard trail use will hit them. On stock parts with 35s and careful driving, Quadratec's take is that factory steering holds up for a reasonable time. Before you buy anything, confirm whether your JT has the standard or wide M210 front axle, since factory parts split on that, and keep the tie rod's stabilizer mount in the plan. This is safety-critical work, so follow the torque-plus-angle numbers and do not reuse fasteners marked one-time.

Safety-critical system. Steering and front-end work decides whether the vehicle tracks straight. Torque every joint to the factory spec and check toe before highway speed. Torque values and sequences come from the factory service manual for your year.

The two parts do different jobs. The tie rod links the two front knuckles and sets toe. The drag link connects the steering box to the knuckle and sets where the steering wheel sits. Quadratec's catalog sells them separately, and either can be upgraded alone if the other is tight.

This is the Gladiator-specific trap. Stellantis describes third-generation Dana 44 axles across the launch range, but parts catalogs treat the front axle as two widths. Quadratec's JT linkage page splits the inner drag link socket, the right outer tie rod socket, and the stabilizer bracket by standard or wide (M210) axle, each with its own part number.

Aftermarket parts handle this differently, so check each one:

  • Synergy describes its drag link as fully adjustable for a variety of axle widths.
  • Steer Smarts sells its YETI XD JL tie rod with separate Rubicon (78088001) and non-Rubicon (78076001) part numbers, and that page lists JL fitment only, not JT. It sells a drag link for both, so ask them which tie rod fits your JT before you order.
  • TeraFlex lists one tie rod kit for JL and JT at 0-6 inches of lift, with no width split on the page. Confirm with them.

Track width is the best public clue. Jeep's 2024 spec sheet lists a 62.9-inch front track for the Sport and Willys, 64.4 inches for the Rubicon and the Sport with Max Tow, and 65 inches for the Mojave. It doesn't name the axle part, and earlier trims such as the Overland aren't on it, so read your build sheet or ask a dealer with your VIN.

If you are on 35s and only one part is suspect, buy that part. If both are worn or you are moving to 37s, the 37-inch guide is where a full linkage upgrade earns its cost. Aluminum tie rods exist, but no source opened here supports recommending them, so they are left out.

Relocation is the follow-on cost people miss. Synergy says its tie rod needs a relocation kit to retain the stabilizer. The kit runs about $108, comes in sizes for a 1-1/2-inch Synergy tie rod or a 1-5/8-inch factory tie rod, and requires drilling the stabilizer bushing sleeve to a 1/2-inch inside diameter. Quadratec rates that kit Level 3. The stabilizer guide covers what a damper can and cannot fix.

High steer is a separate project. Synergy's drag link has an optional flip adapter that moves it to the top of the knuckle. That requires drilling, 3-inch bump stops, the right wheel backspacing, and a track bar relocation bracket. The stock location has no extra requirements. The WAYALIFE JL write-up says a flip and relocation bracket are needed from about 4 inches of lift, so a truck at 2.5 inches can leave this alone. See the track bar guide.

The chart says these apply to factory parts. MetalCloak's JL chart, which cites the factory service manual, disagrees: it lists tie rod and outer drag link nuts at 47 ft-lb, the inner drag link nut at 77 ft-lb, and the damper bolt and nut at 59 ft-lb. Neither chart is the JT service manual, so check that before you trust any of these. Aftermarket instructions override them when they list a value. Sources differ on the tie rod clamp: the WAYALIFE write-up gives a 15mm clamp nut at 45 ft-lb, against Northridge's 13mm at 34 ft-lb. Follow your parts' sheet, and verify factory values against a service manual if you are reinstalling factory clamps.

  • A factory service manual page for any value above. Northridge4x4's chart is a parts vendor's reference.
  • A pitman arm torque for the JT. The chart has none, so none is given here.
  • Rough Country's and Apex Chassis's full JT kits. Their pages could not be opened for this guide, so they are not listed. Both appear in search results with lift and axle limits worth reading directly.

Steps

  1. Park level, chock the rear wheels, loosen the front lugs, and support the frame on stands with the axle secure. Pull the front wheels.
  2. Measure and write down the center-to-center length of the part you are replacing. Inspect the pitman arm and ball joints while you are there.
  3. Remove the stabilizer from the tie rod if it is in the way, noting the orientation.
  4. Remove the nuts and separate the ends with a proper tie rod separator.
  5. Set the new part to your recorded length, threading both ends evenly.
  6. Install it and tighten to the numbers above or the maker's sheet. New fasteners where marked. Grease any fittings.
  7. Refit or relocate the stabilizer and tighten it.
  8. Refit the wheels, tighten the lugs, and lower the truck.
  9. Set toe from the tie rod. The JL write-up targets the front of the tires about 1/16 inch closer than the rear, and not more than 1/8.
  10. Center the wheel from the drag link. Per the JL write-up, loosen the clamp and rotate the adjuster: up turns the wheel left, down turns it right. Tighten and road test.
  11. Check lock to lock for any contact, get a shop alignment, and recheck everything after your first trail run.

Why it works

  • Makers sell these as heavier-duty than factory, with greasable ends and replaceable boots on the Synergy parts. No independent strength test turned up.
  • Direct bolt-on at the factory location.
  • Can be done as one part at a time.

Trade-offs

  • You probably do not need it on 35s with light use and tight factory parts.
  • Costs run from about $468 for one part to about $1,133 for a full Synergy kit with relocation.
  • Axle width and stabilizer fit both need checking first.
  • Alignment is required afterward, and three of the listed parts were out of stock when checked.

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • 21mm socket
  • 13mm and 15mm sockets
  • torque wrench with angle gauge
  • tie rod separator
  • tape measure
  • wheel chocks
