Oil Change — Jeep Gladiator JT 3.6L Pentastar V6

Difficulty 1/50.5–1 hrs$35–752020-2024

The Gladiator's 3.6L Pentastar V6 requires full synthetic oil — 0W-20 is the factory spec. Using the correct viscosity matters more than the brand; using conventional oil in this engine is a mistake.

The 3.6L Pentastar powers every JT Gladiator. It's a robust, well-proven engine with one known sensitivity: it requires full synthetic oil at all times. The tight tolerances in the Pentastar's variable valve timing (VVT) system are designed around synthetic oil's viscosity characteristics — conventional oil doesn't flow fast enough at cold start to properly lubricate the VVT actuators, and degraded synthetic causes sludging in the VVT passages more readily than the spec'd fluid.

Jeep's oil life monitor (OLM) uses engine parameters (temperature cycles, idling time, load) to estimate when the oil should be changed. For most drivers, this works out to roughly 7,000–10,000 miles between changes. For a Gladiator doing heavy trail work with sustained low-speed high-load operation, pull the interval in to 5,000–7,500 miles — the OLM doesn't fully account for trail-use abuse.

Oil capacity: 5.9 quarts with filter change. Have 6 quarts on hand — drain capacity varies slightly by how long the oil drains.

Oil filter: The Pentastar uses a cartridge-style oil filter (not a spin-on canister). The housing is on the front of the engine, passenger side, accessible from above with a 13/16" socket or the Mopar filter cap tool (PN: 4884899AC). Mopar's OEM filter fits correctly; WIX 57356, Fram Ultra XG10575, and Purolator Boss PBL14621 are compatible aftermarket alternatives.

  • Overtightened drain plug: Torque to 20 ft-lbs — that's hand-tight plus about 1/4 turn. Overtightening strips the aluminum pan threads. If the threads feel suspect, use a thread repair kit (Timesert, not Helicoil) before the next change.
  • Filter housing O-ring: If you forget to replace the O-ring and the old one is compromised, the housing will weep oil. This is the most common cause of post-oil-change leaks on the Pentastar.
  • Underfill vs. overfill: Overfilling by more than 1 quart can cause the crankshaft to whip oil into foam, reducing lubrication. The JT's 5.9-qt capacity is precise — add, check the stick, add again in small amounts.

The factory spec for the 3.6L Pentastar in the JT is 0W-20 full synthetic, and that is the viscosity to run in almost every case. It's chosen for cold-start flow into the VVT phasers and for the fuel-economy targets the engine was certified against. The brand matters far less than the specification — Mobil 1, Pennzoil Platinum, and Castrol Edge in 0W-20 full synthetic are all correct.

Where owners ask about deviating:

  • Hot climates and heavy towing. Some Gladiator owners in desert heat or those towing at capacity in summer step up to a 5W-30 full synthetic for a slightly thicker film at operating temperature. This is a defensible choice for a truck that lives under sustained high load, and the Pentastar tolerates it, but understand you're trading a little cold-start flow and a fraction of fuel economy for it. If the truck is under warranty, staying on 0W-20 keeps you aligned with the spec.
  • Cold climates. Do not go thicker than 0W-20 in genuine cold. The 0W winter rating is what gets oil to the top end on a sub-freezing start; a 5W or 10W cold rating flows slower when you need it most.

One rule that isn't negotiable: this engine runs full synthetic only. Conventional or synthetic-blend oil in a Pentastar sludges the VVT passages and contributes to the oil-system problems the platform is known for — including the housing failure covered in /db/gladiator/pentastar-3-6-oil-cooler-leak/.

Every drain is a free diagnostic if you pay attention. Before the pan drains, look and smell:

  • Milky, coffee-with-cream color means coolant is entering the oil — on the Pentastar, suspect the oil filter housing before anything else.
  • A gritty or metallic sheen in the stream or on the drain plug magnet points to internal wear worth investigating.
  • A sharp, gasoline smell suggests fuel dilution, common on short-trip trucks that never fully warm the oil.

For a trail truck that works hard, a used-oil analysis (Blackstone or similar, roughly $30–35) once or twice is worth it. Pull a sample mid-stream into the lab's bottle, and the report tells you wear-metal levels and whether your interval is right for how you actually drive. It's the honest way to decide whether to hold the factory oil-life-monitor interval or pull it in — pair the result with the interval guidance in /db/gladiator/jt-service-intervals/ rather than guessing.

Mobil 1 0W-20 (5 qt jug): ~$30–35. Industry-standard quality at a reasonable price.

Mopar OEM filter: ~$12–16 at the dealer or through online Mopar parts suppliers. The correct specification, not worth compromising on.

Full DIY kit (6 qt oil + OEM filter + new drain plug washer): ~$50–55. A dealer oil change on a JT typically runs $75–100 depending on location. DIY saves $25–50 per interval.

Steps

  1. Warm the engine for 2–3 minutes. Warm oil drains faster and carries more suspended particles with it. Don't change oil on a cold engine if you can avoid it.
  2. Position your drain pan under the oil drain plug, located on the passenger-side rear of the oil pan. The drain plug uses a 13mm hex head.
  3. Remove the drain plug and let the oil drain for 5–10 minutes. Inspect the old oil — metallic particles, a milky color (water intrusion), or a burnt smell are worth noting.
  4. Replace the drain plug washer if your replacement plug kit includes one (or on every other change). Reinstall the plug and torque to 20 ft-lbs. Don't overtighten — the oil pan threads are aluminum.
  5. Move to the oil filter housing on the top-passenger side of the engine. Remove the housing cap with the filter cap tool or 13/16" socket (counter-clockwise). Lift out the old filter element.
  6. Inspect the O-ring on the housing cap — replace it if it's cracked or flattened. Most OEM and quality aftermarket filters include a replacement O-ring.
  7. Install the new filter element into the housing cap. Lightly lubricate the O-ring with fresh oil before reinstalling the cap. Torque the cap to 18 ft-lbs.
  8. Remove the oil filler cap on top of the engine (yellow cap on the valve cover). Add 5.5 quarts of 0W-20 full synthetic. Wait 30 seconds for the oil to settle in the pan, then check the dipstick. Add in small amounts to reach the full mark — don't overfill.
  9. Replace the filler cap. Start the engine and let it run for 30 seconds. Check under the vehicle for leaks at the drain plug. Check around the filter housing for weeping.
  10. Turn off the engine, wait 2 minutes, and verify the oil level on the dipstick. Add if needed to reach the full mark.
  11. Reset the oil life monitor: turn the ignition to Run (don't start the engine), press the gas pedal to the floor three times within 10 seconds. The OLM resets and the maintenance reminder clears.

Tools required

  • oil filter wrench
  • drain pan
  • funnel
  • rags
  • 13/16" socket or oil filter cap (PN: 4884899AC)
