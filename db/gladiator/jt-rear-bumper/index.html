Aftermarket Rear Bumper for the Jeep Gladiator JT

Difficulty 3/51.5–3 hrs$349–12992020-2024

The JT Gladiator's rear is the truck section of the build — and the factory rear bumper is plastic-covered sheet metal that won't withstand a hard backing incident or a recovery strap attachment under load. An aftermarket rear bumper adds rated recovery points and, on the JT's truck platform, often retains the factory towing hitch receiver.

The JT Gladiator's rear bumper sits lower than the JL Wrangler's because the truck has a tow hitch receiver and a longer tail section. This affects departure angle: the factory bumper and hitch receiver are the first contact points when descending a steep exit. A tubular or high-clearance aftermarket bumper raises the effective departure angle and moves contact points inward to the frame rails.

JT-specific rear bumper considerations

Hitch retention: Most JT aftermarket rear bumpers include or integrate with a 2" receiver hitch. The factory hitch is rated at 7,650 lbs tow capacity (Class III). Aftermarket bumpers with integrated hitches often carry a similar rating, but verify before towing — not all aftermarket hitches are rated to the same specification.

Spare tire carrier: The JT doesn't carry a spare tire on the rear bumper (unlike the JL Wrangler) — the JT's spare is typically stored under the bed. This means JT rear bumpers don't need a swing-out spare carrier, which streamlines the design and reduces cost compared to JL Wrangler equivalents.

Backup sensor provisions: If the JT has a factory backup camera (all trims), the camera mounts in the factory tailgate, not the bumper — so camera relocation isn't required for a rear bumper swap. Parking sensors on some Sport S and Rubicon models may be bumper-mounted. Confirm before ordering.

Bumper options

Rough Country ($349): The entry point. Basic steel construction, includes a 2" hitch receiver, and provides rear recovery point tabs. Fit and finish is adequate; build quality is lighter gauge than premium brands. Correct for budget builds on a mild-trail JT.

Poison Spyder Brawler Pro ($549): Frame-mounted with recovery point tabs rated at 4.75 tons. Tubular design improves departure angle versus full-width plate bumpers. Better construction than Rough Country — cleaner welds, heavier gauge, better powder coat.

Warn Ascent ($799): Solid mid-premium option with full-width recovery points and hitch integration. Warn's hardware quality and finish consistency are the reasons to choose it over Poison Spyder at the $800 tier.

Fab Fours Premium ($999): Full-width plate design with a tire step and recovery tabs. The full-width design provides more comprehensive protection against corner contact — relevant for a JT that backs into obstacles in technical terrain.

ARB Rear Bar ($1,199): The premium tier. Factory-quality fitment with provisions for ARB-system air connections, integrated recovery tabs, and a hitch receiver rated for the factory tow spec. The ARB is the correct choice for a JT that will see sustained hard use and where fitment quality matters.

  1. Support the factory rear bumper with a floor jack before removing mounting hardware — the bumper is heavier than it looks.
  2. Remove the mounting bolts from the factory rear bumper: typically two frame rail bolts per side plus bumper reinforcement bolts. On the JT, the reinforcement bar may be integrated — remove the complete assembly.
  3. Disconnect backup sensor harness if equipped. Do not force any connector — squeeze the release tab.
  4. Position the new bumper against the frame mounting points. Use a helper or a floor jack to support the bumper during positioning.
  5. Start all mounting bolts hand-tight before torquing — allows self-alignment.
  6. Torque to spec: typically 60–80 ft-lbs for frame rail hardware.
  7. Verify hitch receiver is square and level. Test the connection with your tow ball or receiver insert before first towing use.
  • Departure angle improvement: Measure the factory bumper height at its lowest point and compare to the new bumper's lowest point. A 2"–3" gain in departure angle clearance is typical with a tubular high-clearance bumper — meaningful on a steep trail exit.
  • Trailer wiring harness: The factory 4-pin or 7-pin trailer wiring harness connects to a bracket on the factory rear bumper. Relocate the bracket to the new bumper's hitch area during install.
  • Reverse light visibility: A full-width rear bumper may partially block the factory reverse light angles. Check that backup visibility is acceptable after install; add supplemental LED reverse lights if not.

A steel rear bumper is not a free upgrade on a truck you load. The factory rear bumper assembly weighs roughly 50 lbs; aftermarket steel replacements run 80-150 lbs depending on design (tubular designs like the Poison Spyder at the light end, full-width plate like the Fab Fours at the heavy end). That net 30-100 lbs comes directly out of payload — and on a Rubicon with ~1,200 lbs to start, armor at both ends plus a winch plus a bed full of gear eats it faster than most owners track. Weigh the truck loaded once at a CAT scale (~$14) and know your real number.

Tongue weight is the second rating to check. The factory setup is rated around 750 lbs tongue weight with the tow package. An aftermarket bumper with an integrated receiver carries its own tongue rating, and the cheaper units are sometimes rated lower than factory — a problem if you tow a trailer with real tongue load. The rating should be stamped on the hitch or stated in the documentation; if the manufacturer does not publish one, that silence is the answer.

Steel bumpers rust from the mounting hardware outward. During install: put anti-seize on every mounting bolt (future-you removing them after five winters will be grateful), touch up any powder coat scratched during installation immediately with cold galvanizing spray before topcoat, and if you live where roads are salted, spray the bumper's internal cavities with a cavity wax (Fluid Film, Krown) once a year. The difference between a bumper that looks right at year eight and one that streaks rust down the paint at year three is thirty minutes of prevention. Recovery points deserve a periodic look too — check shackle tab welds for cracking after any hard recovery, and pair the bumper with proper gear from the JT recovery essentials guide rather than trusting a strap over a bare hitch ball.

Rough Country: ~$349. Entry-level adequate protection.

Poison Spyder Brawler Pro: ~$549. Better construction, same departure angle improvement. The honest value in this category.

Warn Ascent: ~$799. Premium hardware and finish, correct for a daily-driven trail JT.

Fab Fours Premium: ~$999. Full-width protection, looks substantial.

ARB Rear Bar: ~$1,199. The benchmark. Long-term value for a JT that will be driven hard consistently.
