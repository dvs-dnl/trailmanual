Jeep Gladiator JT / Engine

Pentastar 3.6L oil cooler / filter housing leak (TSB fix)

Difficulty 3/52.0–4.0 hrs$80–1802020-present
Short answer

The Pentastar 3.6L's plastic oil filter housing develops cracks around 60,000–100,000 miles. When it fails, you get oil in the coolant, coolant in the oil, or both. The fix is replacing the factory plastic housing with a metal aftermarket unit. This isn't a difficult job — it's a medium-complexity engine bay task.

What's Happening

The Pentastar 3.6L — shared across the JK, JL, and JT — uses a plastic oil filter housing that also integrates the oil cooler. The housing is a single assembly that sits on the front of the engine block. Coolant runs through it to cool the oil; oil runs through it to the filter.

The plastic material degrades over time from heat cycling. Cracks typically develop around the gasket surfaces, at the coolant ports, and at the threads for the oil filter cap. When a crack appears, you can get oil mixing into the coolant (look for a brownish, milky sheen on the coolant reservoir cap), coolant mixing into the oil (look for a coffee-with-milk color on the dipstick), or external leaks pooling under the truck.

Catch this early. Oil-coolant mixing is a fast path to bearing damage and a ruined engine if left unaddressed. If your coolant smells like oil or your dipstick shows discoloration, stop driving and diagnose.

Symptoms

  • External oil leak near the front of the engine, below the intake
  • Coolant reservoir showing a brownish tint or emulsified appearance
  • Dipstick oil with a milky or frothy appearance (coolant contamination)
  • Coolant loss without visible external leaks
  • White smoke from exhaust (coolant burning in cylinders — late-stage)

Diagnosis Before Replacing

Before pulling the housing, confirm the housing is the source. A flashlight and a rag: wipe the area around the housing clean, run the engine to temp, then let it cool slightly and look for fresh seepage. The housing-to-block gasket and the coolant hose connections are the most common leak points.

If you have oil-coolant mixing confirmed, the housing replacement is mandatory. You'll also need to flush the cooling system and change the oil after the repair — the contaminated fluids need to come out completely.

The Replacement: Plastic vs. Metal

The factory plastic housing can be replaced with an identical plastic unit (the leak may return) or with a metal aftermarket housing. The metal housing is the better repair. Dorman makes a direct-fit metal replacement that covers JK, JL, and JT applications; verify your year and engine application before ordering.

The Dorman unit (product number in the 926-series for this application) retains the factory oil filter thread spec and coolant port locations. It bolts in where the factory unit came out.

Cleaning up after oil-coolant contamination

If the housing failed internally and mixed oil with coolant (not merely an external weep), replacing the housing is only half the job. Contaminated coolant leaves an oily film through the entire cooling system, and oil that emulsified with coolant has to come out of the crankcase completely.

  1. Flush the cooling system twice. After the new housing is on, fill with distilled water and a cooling-system flush chemical, run to temp, drain, and repeat until the drain runs clear with no oil sheen. Only then fill with the correct Mopar OAT coolant. Full procedure and coolant spec: /db/gladiator/jt-coolant-flush/.
  2. Change the oil, then change it again short-interval. Do a full oil-and-filter change with the housing repair, drive 500–1,000 miles, then change it a second time. Coolant contamination degrades oil additives and a single change rarely clears residual moisture from the crankcase. Correct capacity, filter part number, and torque specs are in /db/gladiator/jt-oil-change-pentastar/.
  3. Inspect the oil after the second change. Milky residue on the dipstick or filler cap after a clean flush and two changes means coolant is still entering the oil — recheck the housing seal and, if it persists, have the block and head gaskets evaluated. Emulsified oil that reached the bearings is the failure mode that turns an $150 repair into an engine.

Preventing the next failure

The plastic-to-metal swap is the prevention — a metal Dorman housing removes the material failure mode entirely, so once it's on, this specific leak does not come back. Beyond that:

  • Watch the coolant reservoir at every oil change. A faint brown tint or an oily ring at the cap fill line is the earliest warning, usually months before external dripping. Catching it here means a planned repair instead of a roadside one.
  • Don't over-torque the housing bolts. The most common way people re-introduce a leak on the metal unit is crushing the gasket by exceeding the ~18 ft-lb spec. Use a torque wrench, not feel.
  • Keep coolant fresh. Degraded, acidic coolant accelerates gasket and seal wear at every joint in the system, including a freshly installed housing. Stay on the coolant interval rather than running the factory fill indefinitely.

Steps

  1. Allow the engine to cool completely — at least 2 hours after last operation. Do not work on a hot cooling system.
  2. Drain the coolant from the lower radiator drain petcock into a catch pan. Mark the container — used coolant is toxic and needs proper disposal.
  3. Drain the engine oil — place a drain pan under the oil drain plug, remove the plug, remove the oil filter cap from the housing to help the oil drain faster.
  4. Disconnect the coolant hoses from the oil cooler housing (two hoses, clamps). Have rags ready — residual coolant will drain.
  5. Remove the oil filter housing mounting bolts (typically 10mm). Note the number and location.
  6. Lift the housing assembly free of the block. Inspect the block sealing surface — clean it with a rag if there's residue.
  7. Install the new metal housing with a fresh gasket/O-ring kit. Apply no sealant unless the kit instructions specify it. Torque mounting bolts to spec (check the Dorman instructions — typically 18 ft-lb for the housing bolts).
  8. Reconnect coolant hoses. Reinstall drain plug and new oil filter.
  9. Fill engine oil: 0W-20 full synthetic, the JT spec in this site's oil change guide. Jeep's 2024 spec sheet lists 5.7 quarts with filter, so have 6 on hand.
  10. Refill cooling system with fresh coolant. Burp the system: leave the reservoir cap loose, run the engine to temp, watch for air bubbles, then seal.
  11. Check for leaks after first full heat cycle. Recheck coolant level when cold.

Why it works

  • Metal housing eliminates the material failure mode that caused the original crack
  • Direct-fit replacement — no modifications required
  • Resolves oil-coolant mixing before it causes downstream engine damage

Trade-offs

  • Must flush and refill both oil and coolant — adds to total cost
  • If coolant contamination reached the oil passages significantly, inspect further for bearing damage
  • Aftermarket housing quality varies — buy from a reputable supplier and verify fitment before ordering

Tools required

  • 3/8" drive ratchet
  • 10mm socket
  • 13mm socket
  • torque wrench
  • drain pan
  • funnel
  • coolant catch container
