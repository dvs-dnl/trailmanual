Jeep Gladiator JT / Steering

Front Track Bar Upgrade for the Gladiator JT: When You Need One and How to Center the Axle

Difficulty 2/51–2 hrs$205–3402020-2026Jump to steps
Short answer

A stock Gladiator or one lifted up to about 2.5 inches can usually keep the factory front track bar, because the axle shift from lift stays small until around 3 inches. From 3 inches up, an adjustable bar is how you put the axle back under the truck. Buy one sooner only if your bar is worn or you need to correct a measured offset. This is a steering-critical part: torque it to spec and recheck it.

Safety-critical system. Steering and front-end work decides whether the vehicle tracks straight. Torque every joint to the factory spec and check toe before highway speed. Torque values and sequences come from the factory service manual for your year.

Stellantis describes the Gladiator as a five-link coil truck at both ends, with full-width forged steel track bars controlling the axle side to side. The front bar runs from the passenger side of the axle to the driver side of the frame on the JL, and the aftermarket sells front bars as one JL and JT part. That is the basis for using JL setup information here. Check the listing for your exact truck before you order.

Because the bar is angled across the truck, lift pulls the axle to one side. The WAYALIFE JL write-up says any lift shifts the axle toward the driver side. Quadratec describes the same behavior from the other direction: a bar that is too short pulls the front axle to the driver side, and one that is too long pushes it to the passenger side.

Thresholds from the two sources line up:

LiftWhat to do
Up to about 2.5 inchesFactory bar is generally fine. Measure, and re-center the steering wheel if needed.
About 3 inches and upAdjustable bar worthwhile. Quadratec prefers an adjustable, longer, heavy-duty bar, with a frame brace.
About 4 inches and upThe write-up calls for a drag link flip and track bar relocation bracket, not a longer bar alone.

A worn or misadjusted bar also causes other problems. Quadratec ties it to extra wear on other suspension parts, bump steer, and aggravating steering wobble. A new bar does not cure a worn front end. Work through the checks in the stabilizer guide and check the steering recall guide for open campaigns and the shaft check if you are chasing a shimmy.

Both are forged or heat-treated alloy steel, and both list JL and JT. Rubicon Express markets its bar against deflection from oversized tires, and TeraFlex's bolts into the factory brackets. The lower-priced bushing bar does the centering job for about $130 less. No independent durability comparison turned up, so decide on budget and which joint style you are willing to inspect.

Skyjacker also sells an adjustable bar (JLFTBA18) with a 3/4-inch rod end at the axle, listed for the 2018 JL at 0 to 6 inches. Its install sheet is the source for the steps below, but it names only the JL, so confirm JT fitment before buying that one.

Northridge4x4's factory chart lists the JL/JT track bar bolts at 52 ft-lb plus 115 degrees at both the frame and axle ends, on 21mm heads. That chart states it covers factory parts, so if your bar's instructions list a number, use theirs. Skyjacker's sheet tells you to reuse the factory hardware and tighten to the factory spec, and warns that its generic bolt-grade chart is not for bolts run through a bushing.

Do not borrow numbers from a JK. The 125 ft-lb figure that shows up on forums, including in the WAYALIFE write-up, is labeled there as a JK spec. Northridge's JK section matches it, and that is not the JL/JT number.

  • A factory service manual page for this torque value. Northridge4x4's chart is a parts vendor's reference, so verify it against your manual or dealer.
  • A torque spec for each bar's jam nut. Use the instruction sheet that ships with the bar.
  • Whether any front bar differs between standard and wide-axle JTs. None of the bar listings opened here split by axle.
  • Aftermarket frame-bracket reinforcement. Braces exist for the JL and JT, but the product pages could not be opened for this guide, so check them yourself if your bracket shows wear.

Steps

  1. Park on flat ground with the wheels on the floor and the weight on the suspension. Set the parking brake and block the front and rear wheels.
  2. Measure your current offset. Hold a level against each front tire sidewall and measure to a fixed point, such as the fender flare or spring perch, on both sides. Write the numbers down.
  3. Remove the axle-end bolt with a 21mm socket. If the bolt is loaded, a ratchet strap between the frame and differential takes up the tension.
  4. Remove the frame-end bolt and take the factory bar out.
  5. Install the fixed end at the frame with the factory hardware. Leave it loose.
  6. Check that the body is centered over the front tires, then adjust the bar's length to line up with the axle mount.
  7. Install the adjustable end at the axle with the factory hardware. Leave it loose until the length is right.
  8. Tighten both bolts to spec with the truck at ride height, then tighten the jam nut or clamp per the bar's instructions.
  9. Drive it, then re-measure from step 2 and adjust if you need to.
  10. If the steering wheel sits off-center, re-center it with the drag link adjuster. See the tie rod and drag link guide. Get an alignment.
  11. Cycle the steering lock to lock on the ground and check for contact. Recheck all hardware after 100 miles, as Skyjacker directs, and periodically after that.

Why it works

  • Re-centers the axle at any lift in the listed range.
  • A bolt-in job with hand tools, no shop required.
  • Can be readjusted if you change lift later.

Trade-offs

  • You probably do not need it under about 3 inches of lift.
  • It does not fix caster, bump steer at taller lifts, or worn ball joints and tie rod ends.
  • Above about 4 inches it needs a relocation bracket and drag link flip too.
  • Adjustable joints and jam nuts need periodic checks.

Tools required

  • 21mm socket and wrench
  • torque wrench with angle gauge
  • tape measure
  • carpenter's level
  • ratchet strap
  • wrench for the bar's jam nut
  • wheel chocks
