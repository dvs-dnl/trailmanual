Jeep Gladiator JT / Armor & Skids

Rock Sliders for the Gladiator JT

Difficulty 3/53–5 hrs$449–7992020-2024Jump to steps
Short answer

Rock sliders protect the JT's rocker panels when the trail gets narrow and you're trading paint with the terrain — and the Gladiator's longer wheelbase means those rockers are exposed across more of the truck's length than on a Wrangler.

The JT Gladiator is 19" longer than the JL Unlimited, and that extra length shows up in the rocker panel exposure on the trail. A JL slider won't cover a JT's rockers — this is not a cross-compatible part. When you're sourcing sliders for the JT, verify the listing says JT explicitly and check the coverage dimensions. Quality JT sliders should run the full length of the cab door openings, with enough tube diameter to take side contact without deforming into the rocker.

The core decision is weld-on vs. bolt-on. Weld-on sliders (like the Poison Spyder JT at $699 and the LOD Destroyer at $699) mount directly to the frame with stronger, lower-profile attachment — they don't depend on bolt holes in factory pinch-weld flanges. They're the right choice for a dedicated trail truck. Bolt-on sliders (Rock Hard 4x4 at $449, Smittybilt XRC at $499) use existing frame holes or require drilling into the body/frame at specific points. The install is accessible without a welder, but bolt-on designs have more flex under hard lateral contact. That doesn't mean they fail — the Rock Hard 4x4 units are well-regarded bolt-on sliders that have taken real abuse — but weld-on is structurally superior when you're doing legitimate rock work.

Smittybilt's XRC JT sliders at $499 are the budget-to-quality inflection point: bolt-on install, step functionality, and enough steel tube to handle moderate trail abuse. If you're building an overland-focused JT that occasionally sees rocky trails rather than a dedicated rock crawler, the Smittybilt units are honest value. Poison Spyder and LOD are the right call for a wheeling-first build where the sliders will get used hard.

What to watch for

The most common JT slider install mistake is mounting too low in pursuit of a beefier step. A slider that hangs below the frame rail becomes the truck's lowest point and turns every driveway and ledge into a hang-up. Mount the slider so its bottom edge sits at or slightly above the frame rail, not below it.

On bolt-on kits, the factory pinch-weld flange is not a structural mounting surface — it's sheet metal. Kits that clamp only to the pinch weld will crush it under a hard side hit and transfer the load into the rocker you were trying to protect. Verify the kit mounts to the frame rail itself (through-bolted or with frame-clamp brackets), not only the body seam.

For weld-on installs, grind the frame to bare metal at every weld location, tack in three or four spots, then hang your full weight on the slider before laying the final beads — a slider that shifts after welding is scrap. Keep a fire extinguisher and a wet rag ready: the fuel and brake lines run near the frame on the JT, and a stray spark or heat soak on a line is a real hazard. If you're not confident MIG-welding structural steel, this is a job for a fab shop.

After the first hard trail day, re-check every fastener. Bolt-on sliders back their hardware out under repeated impact — a slider held by two of four bolts is a liability.

Related builds

Pair sliders with a full skid plate kit for continuous underbody-to-rocker protection, and a front bumper if you're staging a complete armor package. Owners running larger tires load the sliders hardest during flex — mount to the frame accordingly.

Steps

  1. Clean and degrease the frame rail area along the rocker panels where sliders will mount.
  2. For bolt-on kits: locate the factory mounting points per kit instructions; drill any additional holes required. Use a center punch to prevent drill drift.
  3. For weld-on kits: position sliders using clamps and verify fitment before any welding. Tack-weld first, check alignment, then complete the weld.
  4. Apply the supplied hardware (bolt-on kits): hand-thread all bolts before torquing any, then torque to spec in a cross pattern.
  5. Inspect the slider length against the door sill — sliders should extend from the front door hinge area to at least the rear door hinge on the driver and passenger sides.
  6. Coat any raw or bare metal areas with rattle-can flat black or bedliner to prevent rust.

Why it works

  • Protects rocker panels from the most common trail damage source: rock contact on side slopes
  • Doubles as a step for cab entry on lifted JTs
  • JT-specific fitment covers the full longer wheelbase cab
  • Weld-on options are among the strongest armor dollar-per-dollar available

Trade-offs

  • JL sliders will not fit — verify JT fitment before ordering
  • Weld-on install requires a welder or a fabrication shop; adds $150–$300 to install cost
  • Adds 30–60 lbs per side — minor but worth noting on a truck you're weight-managing
  • Powder coat chips on rocks; touch-up paint or bedliner recoat is ongoing maintenance

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • socket set
  • torque wrench
  • drill
  • wire brush
