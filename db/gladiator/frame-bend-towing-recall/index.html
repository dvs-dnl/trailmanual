Jeep Gladiator JT / Body & Rust

Gladiator frame bend reports and tongue weight

Difficulty 2/51.0–2.0 hrs$02020-2022Jump to steps
Short answer

There is no Gladiator frame recall. NHTSA's recall list for the 2020 Gladiator has none, and neither do the lists for 2021 to 2023. The bent-frame photos that circulated in 2020 showed a modified 2020 Gladiator towing a loaded camping trailer on the Mojave Trail, with no statement from Jeep and no recall or investigation. The lesson still holds: load the trailer correctly, stay inside your truck's numbers, and inspect the frame after any overload.

What Was Found

The widely shared case was one truck. The Drive's write-up says the Gladiator was no longer stock, was towing a rental camping trailer of unknown weight and loading over rough terrain, and that the causes raised (shocks acting as bump stops, a trailer loaded heavy on the tongue, driving style) were never confirmed. It also notes that the Mojave has a strengthened rear frame.

The Gladiator frame is a pickup-truck ladder frame. Tongue weight, bed cargo and impacts all load its rear section, and a frame overloaded far enough will yield.

Still run your VIN through NHTSA's lookup. It shows every open campaign on your truck, and the JT has real ones in other systems.

Tongue Weight — The Number That Matters

Total tow rating gets the headlines. Tongue weight is the number you control with how you load the trailer.

Stellantis doesn't print a Gladiator tongue-weight limit on its spec sheets, so check the owner's manual for your model year. For loading, the common target is 10 to 15 percent of trailer weight on the hitch; less than that and the trailer is more likely to sway. On a 5,000 lb trailer, 10 percent is 500 lb, and every pound of it counts against your payload.

The common mistake: People load trailers and then add gear at the rear of the trailer to "balance" it. This pushes weight toward the tongue and over the limit. The trailer should be loaded with 60% of its weight ahead of the axle — this is how tongue weight stays in the 10–12% range.

How to Check Tongue Weight

A tongue weight scale is a $30–$60 tool that attaches to the ball mount and lets you read tongue weight directly before you put the truck under load. If you tow regularly, this is worth having.

If you can't weigh it, use the 60/40 rule: place the heavier items in the front half of the trailer (ahead of the axle), lighter items in the rear. Run the numbers: trailer weight × 0.10 to 0.15 = the target tongue weight range in lb.

Frame Inspection — What to Look For

On any Gladiator that tows or carries heavy loads, inspect the rear frame rails behind the cab:

  • Look for cracking or deformation along the top and bottom flanges of the frame rail
  • Check the area around the receiver hitch mounting bolts — look for elongated bolt holes or cracking at the attachment points
  • Look at the crossmember that ties the rear frame rails together — deformation here is a flag
  • Any visible bend in the frame rail is serious and warrants a shop visit before towing again

A frame that has bent does not bend back. If you find deformation, stop towing until the truck is inspected by a qualified shop and the frame is evaluated.

Weight Distribution Hitch — When It Earns Its Keep

For trailers near the top of the JT's rating, a weight-distributing (WD) hitch changes how tongue load is carried. Instead of dumping the full tongue weight onto the rear hitch and the short rear overhang, a WD hitch uses spring bars to transfer part of that load forward to the front axle and back to the trailer axle. On the JT specifically — where the concern is stress concentrated at the rear frame section — a properly set WD hitch reduces rear-frame loading and restores front-axle steering and braking authority that a heavy tongue would otherwise lift away.

A WD hitch does not raise your tongue weight limit or your tow rating. It manages how the rated load is distributed. Match the spring bar rating to your actual measured tongue weight — bars rated far above your load over-correct and ride harshly; bars rated below won't distribute properly. If you're towing anything past ~3,500 lb regularly, price a WD hitch alongside the trailer. The payload and towing reality guide works through how quickly passengers, gear, and tongue weight eat into the JT's real-world payload before you ever hit the tow number.

After a Suspected Overload

If you towed over spec — even once, even briefly — inspect before you assume the frame is fine. Overload damage is cumulative and doesn't always show as a dramatic bend. Look for paint cracking or flaking along the frame rail flanges behind the cab, elongated or egg-shaped holes at the hitch mounting bolts, and any daylight or gap change at the rear crossmember-to-rail joints. A frame that has yielded will often show a subtle waviness in the rail that a straightedge laid along the flange will reveal. Any of these findings means a qualified frame shop, not a wait-and-see. A compromised frame also changes how the truck behaves under recovery loads — review recovery gear essentials before you rig to a truck whose structure you're unsure of.

Steps

  1. Go to nhtsa.gov/recalls, enter your VIN, and deal with any open campaigns
  2. Inspect the rear frame rails with a flashlight
  3. If you currently tow, weigh your tongue weight and compare it to 10 to 15 percent of the trailer's weight and to the limit in your owner's manual
  4. Adjust trailer loading if it falls outside that range, and check that tongue weight plus cargo stays under your payload

Why it works

  • No frame recall means no frame campaign to chase on a used JT
  • A frame inspection takes a flashlight and an hour
  • Correct trailer loading lowers sway risk and rear-frame load at the same time

Trade-offs

  • Overload damage is the owner's problem, not a warranty item
  • A bent frame is a serious structural issue — it's not a cosmetic repair
  • A used JT's towing history is unknown — inspect the frame before purchase

Tools required

  • flashlight
  • straightedge or level (for visual inspection)
