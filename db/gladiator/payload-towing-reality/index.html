Gladiator payload and towing: the honest numbers

Difficulty 1/50.5–1.0 hrs$02020-present
Short answer

The Gladiator's payload and tow ratings vary significantly by trim. A Rubicon's 1,200 lb payload disappears fast. The top tow rating, 7,700 lb on 2024–2026 trucks, needs the Max Tow package on a Sport, though an automatic Rubicon is still rated 7,000 lb.

TrimPayload (approx.)Max tow, 2024–2026 automatic
Sport with Max Tow (4.10 axles)1,710 lb7,700 lb
Sport / Sport S (3.73 axles)1,600 lb6,000 lb (4,500 lb on 2025 spec sheets)
Willys1,540 lb6,000 lb
Rubicon1,200 lb7,000 lb
Mojave1,350 lb6,000 lb

Tow figures are Stellantis's 2024–2026 spec-sheet maximums for automatic trucks; 2024 manual-transmission trucks were rated 4,000–4,500 lb. Payload is the number on your truck's door-jamb sticker, which accounts for your exact trim and options, so use that rather than the approximations above. Stellantis doesn't publish Gladiator tongue-weight limits on the spec sheets; check the owner's manual for your model year.

The Rubicon is heavier than the base trims. Lockers, skid plates, rock rails, beefier axle hardware, the NV241 Rock-Trac transfer case, the sway-bar disconnect actuator — all of it adds curb weight. The factory sets payload capacity as GVWR minus curb weight, so a heavier truck has less available payload even if the GVWR is the same. The Rubicon's additional equipment is what cuts its payload rating to 1,200 lb.

The 7,700 lb rating is available only on the Sport with the Max Tow package, which pairs the automatic with 4.10 axles (Stellantis lists 4.10 as standard on the Sport Max Tow, Mojave and Rubicon). The package adds the towing hardware; check the window sticker for the exact content on the truck you're looking at.

The Rubicon can't reach 7,700 lb, but with the automatic it's rated 7,000 lb, well above any Wrangler. Its heavier curb weight works against payload, which is usually the limit you hit first.

A Rubicon towing a loaded camp trailer with two adults in the cab:

  • Two adults: ~340 lb
  • Full 17.5-gallon fuel tank: ~115 lb
  • Loaded bed (recovery gear, camp gear): ~300 lb
  • That's 755 lb used before the trailer hitch is connected.

With 1,200 lb total capacity, that leaves 445 lb for tongue weight and anything else in the bed. A medium-sized camp trailer like a small teardrop or a pop-up can easily put 400–500 lb on the tongue. You're at the limit before the trailer's loaded.

Overland buyers in camp trailers face this calculation constantly. The Gladiator is a shorter-wheelbase platform than a full-size truck. It was designed for trail access, not towing heavy loads. The platform is honest about that.

Regardless of tow rating, the Gladiator has a specific tongue weight limit: 10% of trailer weight, up to a maximum. The frame bend issues documented in early production Gladiators were specifically linked to tongue weight exceeding limits — not overall tow weight. See the separate frame bend recall guide for details.

The takeaway: even if you're well within tow rating, managing tongue weight to stay at 10–12% of trailer weight is important on the JT. The platform's pickup-truck framing is shorter than a full-size and sees different stress concentrations when tongue weight is high.

The tow and payload headlines hide four limits, and you hit whichever comes first — not whichever is highest:

  • GVWR (Gross Vehicle Weight Rating): the most the loaded truck itself can weigh — everything in the cab and bed, plus trailer tongue weight, plus the truck. This is the one payload buyers blow through.
  • GCWR (Gross Combined Weight Rating): the most the truck and trailer can weigh together. Max-tow numbers assume a nearly empty truck; load the cab and bed and your usable trailer weight drops below the sticker.
  • GAWR front / rear (Gross Axle Weight Rating): the most each axle can carry. Heavy tongue weight loads the rear axle specifically — you can be under GVWR overall and still overload the rear GAWR.
  • Max tongue weight: capped as a hard number regardless of trailer size.

The honest way to check is a CAT scale: weigh the loaded truck and trailer, then the truck alone, and compare each axle and the combined figure against the door-jamb and manual numbers. Guessing from the spec sheet is how people end up over rear GAWR while feeling "under tow rating."

For any trailer putting meaningful tongue weight on a Gladiator, a weight-distribution (WD) hitch earns its cost. Spring bars transfer some of the tongue load off the JT's rear axle and back onto the front axle and the trailer's own axles, which restores steering feel, levels the truck, and reduces the rear-axle and frame stress that matters on this platform.

This is not optional caution on the JT specifically. The early-production frame-bend cases were tied to concentrated rear-frame loading, and a WD hitch is one of the direct mitigations — see Frame bend and the towing recall on the JT. Pair the WD setup with an anti-sway component for anything tall or long; the JT's short wheelbase makes it more sway-prone at highway speed than a full-size truck. If your load-out is pushing the rear axle down, also revisit rear suspension behavior under load in Factory leaf spring axle wrap on the JT.

Steps

  1. Find your truck's door jamb sticker — use the actual payload number, not the spec sheet
  2. Weigh your real-world cargo: passengers, fuel, gear, bed items
  3. Subtract from your payload rating — what's left is available for tongue weight
  4. If that number is under 400 lb, your current load-out isn't compatible with a trailer
  5. Use a tongue weight scale (or weigh at a truck stop) before committing to a trailer purchase

Why it works

  • The Sport with Max Tow is a genuinely capable tow vehicle for mid-size trailers
  • All trims can tow something useful, and an automatic Rubicon is rated 7,000 lb
  • The solid-axle rear means no IFS flex under tongue weight load

Trade-offs

  • The Rubicon's 1,200 lb payload is straightforward to exceed in real-world camp and trail use
  • The best tow rating requires the Sport with Max Tow — trail buyers and tow buyers are often looking at different trucks
  • The JT's shorter wheelbase compared to full-size trucks affects stability at highway tow speeds — drive accordingly

Tools required

  • door-jamb payload sticker
  • CAT scale or trailer tongue-weight scale
