Jeep Gladiator JT / Armor & Skids

Aftermarket Front Bumper for the Jeep Gladiator JT

Difficulty 3/52–4 hrs$499–18002020-2024
Short answer

The factory JT front bumper is plastic-capped sheet metal — it looks reasonably rugged but won't withstand a direct ledge strike, and it doesn't offer a winch mount. An aftermarket steel bumper adds protection, a winch platform, and proper recovery point mounting in one upgrade.

The JT Gladiator's factory bumper bolts to the frame rails at four points. All quality aftermarket bumpers use the same factory mounting holes — no drilling, no modification required. The bumper weight varies significantly by design: tube-style bumpers run 60–90 lbs; full-width steel plate bumpers run 100–160 lbs. The extra weight sits at the extreme front of the vehicle and affects front suspension behavior. Account for the added weight when selecting shocks and, if significant, when choosing coil spring rates.

Factory parking sensor and camera compatibility

The JT Gladiator includes front parking sensors on some trim levels and a forward-facing camera on some packages. Before ordering a bumper, confirm whether your JT has these sensors and whether the replacement bumper provides sensor mounting provisions. Check each bumper's listing for sensor and camera provisions rather than assuming by price; budget bumpers often skip them.

Bumper categories

Tube-style (Poison Spyder, Rough Country): Tubular steel construction — lighter, allows better approach angle visibility, still provides frame-mounted recovery points. The open design does not protect the radiator from direct debris strikes the way a plate bumper does, but it maintains airflow better and reduces the visual bulk. Correct for trail-oriented JTs where approach angle and weight matter.

Stubby and full-width plate (Fab Fours, Rugged Ridge, Smittybilt): Steel plate bumpers in stubby or full-width form. Quadratec's JT listings include the Fab Fours Front Stubby from $557.99, the Rugged Ridge HD Full Width at $779.99 and the Smittybilt XRC Gen 3 at $809.99. Most include a winch mount, recovery tabs, and provisions for auxiliary lights; confirm each on the listing. The tradeoff: approach angle is slightly compromised versus a narrower tube bumper, and weight is higher.

Factory-look steel (Mopar 3-piece Rubicon bumper, from about $1,197 at Quadratec): the choice for a JT that divides time between daily driving and trail work and should keep the factory look. Check fog light, sensor and winch provisions on the listing for your trim before you order.

Winch compatibility

Most JT aftermarket bumpers that include a winch mount are compatible with winches in the 8,000–12,000 lb class. Confirm the bumper's winch plate dimensions before ordering a winch — a Warn M8000 (7.5" wide winch body) fits most mid-size bumper fairleads, but a Warn 9.5xp (wider body) requires a bumper with a wider opening. Some bumpers are drilled for one winch maker's pattern, so match the bumper's winch plate to the winch you plan to buy.

Installation

  1. Remove the factory front bumper: typically six bolts — two per side mounting to the bumper reinforcement bar, plus two frame rail bolts. The reinforcement bar (the steel bar behind the plastic fascia) also needs to come off — it's bolted to the frame rails separately.
  2. On JTs with parking sensors: unplug the sensor harness connectors behind the bumper before removing. Label each connector if multiple sensors are present.
  3. Set the factory bumper and reinforcement aside.
  4. Thread the new bumper onto the frame rails. Most designs require two people — one to hold the bumper in position, one to start the mounting bolts.
  5. Start all bolts hand-tight before torquing any of them — this allows the bumper to self-align.
  6. Torque the mounting bolts: typically 75–90 ft-lbs for frame-rail mounting hardware.
  7. Wire auxiliary lights per the light manufacturer's instructions and the bumper's integrated harness provisions (if any).
  8. Install recovery hardware (D-rings, shackles) in the recovery point tabs. Torque shackle pins to hand-tight plus 1/4 turn. Mousing wire if available.

What to watch for

  • Airbag sensor location: Some JT trims have a crash sensor on the factory bumper reinforcement bar. This sensor must either be relocated or confirmed to not be present before removing the reinforcement. Check the service manual for your specific trim.
  • Grille and hood clearance: Heavier plate bumpers can sit slightly higher than the factory bumper line and may require the hood stop rubber to be adjusted for correct hood-to-grille gap.
  • Weight and alignment: Adding 60–120 lbs to the front changes the front-to-rear weight balance. After bumper install, check that the caster and alignment are still within spec — and if adding a winch simultaneously, get an alignment check.

Adaptive Cruise Control radar

JTs equipped with Adaptive Cruise Control carry a radar module mounted low in the center of the front fascia, and this is the most-missed compatibility item on bumper orders. The module has to end up at the correct height and angle behind the new bumper or ACC and forward-collision warning will fault out — a dash full of warnings on the first drive is the usual symptom of a bumper installed without the radar bracket. Premium bumpers (ARB, Warn, Fab Fours) list an ACC-compatible variant or include a relocation bracket; many budget bumpers ignore the radar entirely. Confirm your truck's equipment before ordering, and buy the bumper variant that matches. If the system throws faults after a correct mechanical install, the radar may need re-aiming with a dealer scan tool — budget for that possibility.

Steel vs. aluminum

A handful of manufacturers offer JT front bumpers in aluminum at roughly half the weight of the equivalent steel design — a full-width aluminum bumper can come in near tube-steel weight while offering plate-style coverage. The tradeoffs are real: aluminum costs several hundred dollars more for the same design, and it responds to rock strikes differently — steel dents and bends where aluminum can gouge or crack, and a cracked aluminum weld is a harder field repair. For a JT that carries a winch and sees regular rock contact, plate steel remains the default answer. For an overland build fighting front-axle weight — where the winch, bumper, and larger tires stack onto the front springs — aluminum buys back meaningful payload and steering feel. Pair either choice with the front spring-rate conversation in the mid-tier lift guide, because a 140-lb bumper-and-winch package on stock coils drops the nose visibly.

Costs and sources

Rough Country Heavy-Duty: ~$499. Entry-level, basic winch mount, no sensor provisions. Acceptable for an off-road-only JT.

Poison Spyder Brawler Pro: ~$649. Better build quality, cleaner welds, sensor provisions available. The correct mid-tier choice.

Fab Fours Front Stubby: from about $558. Rugged Ridge HD Full Width: about $780. Smittybilt XRC Gen 3: about $810. Prices from Quadratec's JT front bumper listings, November 2026.

Mopar 3-piece Rubicon steel: from about $1,197. The factory-look option.

  • socket set (metric)
  • torque wrench
  • floor jack (for initial alignment)
  • wire stripper (if adding lights)
