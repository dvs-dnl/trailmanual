Bed Rack and Overland Platform Options — Jeep Gladiator JT

Difficulty 2/51–3 hrs$299–18002020-2024

The Gladiator JT's 5-foot bed is the feature that separates it from every Wrangler variant — it turns a capable trail rig into a capable trail rig that can actually carry gear. A bed rack expands that capacity vertically and gives you mounting points for overland accessories.

The JT's bed measures 60" long and 44.5" wide between the wheel wells — the standard 5-foot pickup measurement. This fits most bed rack systems designed for mid-size trucks, but the JT's specific stake pocket spacing (the tie-down slots along the top rail) is important to verify before ordering. Stake pocket depth and spacing vary by year, and not all universal racks fit the JT without adapter brackets.

Most JT bed rack systems clamp to the bed rail via stake pocket inserts — no drilling required. A small number of rack systems require drilling into the bed floor or using factory cargo track anchors. Know which type you're buying before you commit.

Universal crossbar racks (Yakima OverHaul HD, Thule TracRac): Two crossbars spanning the bed width, adjustable fore/aft position. Load rating typically 300–500 lbs. Mount roof boxes, kayaks, lumber, or a roof top tent (RTT) on a crossbar system. The JT's 5-foot bed needs careful RTT positioning — the tent must deploy rearward or to the side without conflicting with the cab.

Full bed platform systems (Leitner ACS, Sherpa Colorado): A full-coverage aluminum extrusion system that spans the entire bed floor and rails. Creates a platform above the bed floor for gear organization, drawer mounting, and a sleeping or tent surface. Higher cost ($1,200–1,600+) but more versatile — you can run an RTT on top, drawers underneath, and still have access to the bed below. The Leitner ACS is the benchmark for JT overland platform systems.

Stake pocket crossbars (Go Rhino XRS, Smittybilt): Ladder-style racks with two or three crossbars that drop into stake pockets. Lower profile, lower cost ($300–450). Load ratings run 400–600 lbs. Less useful for complex overland setups but adequate for equipment transport (bikes, roof boxes, auxiliary fuel cans).

The JT's cab height and bed rail position create a rear deck height of approximately 30–32" loaded (wheel arch height). An RTT mounted on a bed rack sits 6–12" above the bed rail when folded. This stacks to 38–44" — manageable for a garage with an 8' door, tight for lower clearances.

For RTT mounting: confirm the rack's dynamic load rating (what it can carry while driving, not parked). Most RTTs weigh 100–180 lbs. Most bed racks rate dynamic loads at 150–300 lbs. Match accordingly, and check that the RTT width (most are 56"–65") fits within the JT bed width without overhanging the wheel wells excessively.

Stake pocket systems (no-drill):

  1. Identify the stake pocket positions along the JT bed rail (typically 6 pockets, 3 per side).
  2. Drop the stake pocket inserts from the rack kit into the pockets. Most kits use expanding inserts tightened from above.
  3. Set the crossbars or frame across the inserts and thread the mounting bolts.
  4. Level and square the rack before final torque.
  5. Torque to spec per the rack manufacturer — typically 15–25 ft-lbs on stake pocket inserts.

Platform systems (Leitner ACS):

  1. More involved — follows a kit-specific sequence. Typically: install cargo track base to the bed floor (anchors to factory cargo anchor points), then assemble the extrusion frame to the cargo track, then mount the top deck.
  2. Allow 2–3 hours for first install.

The JT's real limitation is not rack strength — it's payload. A Rubicon's payload runs roughly 1,100–1,200 lbs, a Sport around 1,600 lbs. Everything you add counts against that number: the rack, the RTT, gear, water, fuel, passengers, and the weight of a built truck's armor.

A worked example on a Rubicon: rack (80 lbs) + RTT (140 lbs) + two adults in the tent (360 lbs) + bedding and gear (100 lbs) = 680 lbs before you have loaded the cab, filled a fridge, or added recovery gear. On a truck already carrying bumpers and a winch, you can exceed rated payload before leaving the driveway. Weigh the built truck at a CAT scale and do the arithmetic before committing to a heavy platform. The payload and towing reality guide covers the axle and GVWR numbers in full.

A rack that carries fuel cans, firewood, and recovery boards will drop debris into the bed. Pair a platform with a spray-in or drop-in bed liner so the rack's cargo does not chew up the factory bed finish, and use a bed drawer or cargo management system underneath a full platform to keep weight low and organized.

  • Dynamic vs. static load rating: The number that matters while driving is the dynamic rating, always lower than the static (parked) rating. Size the rack to the RTT plus occupants against the dynamic figure.
  • Stake pocket corrosion: Inserts that live in the pockets trap water. Pull and inspect them annually; a seized insert can crack the pocket casting.
  • Cab clearance on RTT deploy: The 5-foot bed is short. Confirm the tent opens rearward or sideways without hitting the cab or a high-mount third brake light.
  • Wind noise and MPG: A loaded crossbar rack adds meaningful drag. Remove or lower it for long highway stretches when the gear is not needed.

Go Rhino or basic stake pocket rack: ~$299–399. Adequate for occasional gear transport.

Yakima OverHaul HD or Thule TracRac: ~$699–849. The right choice for frequent RTT or overhead gear use.

Sherpa Colorado or equivalent mid-tier: ~$649. Good balance of build quality and modularity for JT overland builds.

Leitner ACS or similar premium platform: ~$1,399–1,600. The correct investment if the JT is a dedicated overland build — replaces both a bed liner and a rack with a modular, organized system.

Installation is DIY-friendly for all stake pocket and crossbar systems. The Leitner ACS is more involved but well-documented — allow an afternoon and follow the instructions carefully.

Tools required

  • socket set (10mm, 12mm)
  • drill (if required for your rack)
  • torque wrench
  • level
