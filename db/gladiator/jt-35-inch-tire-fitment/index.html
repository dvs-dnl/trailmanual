Jeep Gladiator JT / Wheels & Tires

35" Tire Fitment Guide — Jeep Gladiator JT

Difficulty 2/51–3 hrs$800–22002020-2024
Short answer

35-inch tires are the inflection point for the JT Gladiator — where the truck stops looking like it rolled off a dealer lot and starts looking like it belongs on a trail. Getting there cleanly requires knowing the trim, the lift, and the wheel offset interaction before you order.

The Gladiator JT's factory wheel arch dimensions are wider than the JL Wrangler's — the JT is a truck with a longer wheelbase and a wider stance. This matters for tire fitment: you can fit a 35" tire on a JT in stock trim conditions that would require a lift on the JL, depending on the specific trim and offset combination.

What fits stock (no lift):

  • Sport and Willys 17" wheels (5×5-inch bolt pattern, or 5×127 mm): A 285/70R17 (roughly 32.7": 285 × 0.70 × 2 ÷ 25.4 + 17) fits without rubbing at full articulation on stock suspension. A 35x12.50R17 will rub the front inner fender liner and the lower control arm skid at full articulation. Mild trimming of the inner fender liner (5 minutes, utility knife) gets you there without a lift on many builds, but it's not clean.
  • Rubicon 17" wheels: Same result — factory offset on the Rubicon places the tire slightly better than the Sport, but 35s still contact at full lock and full droop.

With 2" of lift:

A 2" spacer lift (or coil lift) with a 285/70R17 or 35x12.50R17 on stock offset wheels (factory JT offset is around +44mm) fits on most Sport and Willys JTs without rubbing at normal articulation. Full-lock turns may still contact. Running a wider wheel with less offset (0 to -25mm, zero to negative) gives additional clearance from the inner fender.

With 3.5"–4" of lift:

35x12.50R17 or 35x12.50R18 (for Rubicon 18" options) fits cleanly on all JT trims with no rubbing at full articulation and no trimming. This is the sweet spot for 35s on the JT — enough lift to clear the tire under compression, with correct geometry correction components (a track bar drop bracket is recommended).

Tire selection for the JT

The JT's longer wheelbase creates a different on-road experience with aggressive mud terrain tires than the shorter JL. On-road noise is amplified by the longer time the tire spends in contact with road surface per mile. All-terrain tires (KO2, Wildpeak AT3W, Ridge Grappler, Baja Boss A/T) hit the best balance of trail capability and livability on the JT's street/trail split use case.

BFG KO2 35x12.50R17 (~$299 each): The benchmark all-terrain. Works on both daily driving and moderate trails. Treadwear rating is excellent — 500 UTQG, which translates to 60,000–80,000 miles of normal use. The width makes it a good visual match on the JT.

Falken Wildpeak AT3W 285/70R17 (~$229 each): The slightly narrower footprint improves on-road behavior and fuel economy versus a 12.50" wide tire. "285/70R17" measures out to approximately 32.7" — not technically a 35, but often the better real-world choice for owners prioritizing highway comfort.

Nitto Ridge Grappler 35x12.50R17 (~$289 each): Hybrid AT/MT design with aggressive sidewall lugs. Better technical trail grip than the KO2; noisier on the highway. Correct for owners who trail-drive more than they highway-drive.

Wheel offset considerations

Factory JT wheel offset is approximately +44mm (Sport/Willys and Rubicon alike). Running the same offset with a 35" tire works with a lift; a lower offset (e.g., +18mm down to -12mm) pushes the tire outboard and creates more clearance between the sidewall and the inner fender at full lock. Most aftermarket 17" wheels for the JT run between +18mm and -12mm. Stay in that range — going past about -25mm adds real leverage on the unit bearings and steering, widens the scrub radius enough to feel in every rut, and on a 12.50-wide tire will poke past the flares (a legality problem in several states).

Re-gearing with 35s

The JT's factory gear ratio is 3.73:1 (Sport, Willys, Overland — 4.10 with the Max Tow package) or 4.10:1 (Rubicon and Mojave). Running 35" tires on 3.73s creates a drive feel issue — the taller tire drops engine rpm at freeway speed, so the truck lugs on grades and power delivery feels sluggish off-road in 4H. Re-gearing to 4.56 corrects this on Sport/Willys trims (4.10 works but leaves nothing in reserve); the full ratio decision is walked through in the JT re-gear guide. Rubicon owners are already at 4.10 and can run 35s without re-gearing. If you're pushing to 37" tires, re-gearing to 4.88 or 5.13 applies across all trims.

The spare tire question

The JT stores its spare under the bed, not on a tailgate carrier like the JL — and Jeep designed the under-bed well to accept up to a 35-inch spare. A 35x12.50R17 fits, but drop the carrier and test-fit yours before a trip: with the factory winch-style carrier a wide 35 is a tight, deliberate fit, and some 35s on aftermarket wheels (different backspacing) refuse to seat flat. If yours will not fit, the options are a bed-mounted tire carrier (~$250-400), accepting a 33-inch spare as a get-home tire (fine for short distances with an open diff, risky with a locker or limited slip — mismatched diameters load the clutch packs), or leaving the spare home and betting on plugs. Carry a plug kit either way; see the recovery gear guide for what belongs in the bed.

Speedometer recalibration

35s throw the speedometer off by roughly 6% — enough to matter for the odometer, shift points, and your next speeding ticket. A Tazer JT Mini (~$220-250) writes the correct tire size (and axle ratio, if you re-gear) into the ECU in five minutes through the OBD2 port. On a 35-inch build this is the difference between a truck that drives like the factory intended and one that always feels slightly off.

Costs and sources

Tire cost (per tire): $229–299 for quality AT options on 35s. A set of four runs $916–$1,196 in tires alone.

Add mount and balance: ~$15–25 per tire at most shops.

Add lift (if needed): See the JT lift kit guide — budget lift starts at $199, quality mid-lift at $800–$1,200.

Add re-gear (if needed on Sport/Willys): $1,200–$2,000 at a drivetrain shop for front and rear.

Total realistic cost for 35s done right on a stock Sport JT: $1,400–$3,400 depending on whether you need a lift, re-gear, and new wheels.
