Spark Plug Replacement — Jeep Gladiator JT 3.6L Pentastar

Difficulty 3/51.5–3 hrs$35–902020-2024

The Gladiator's 3.6L Pentastar has six cylinders arranged in a V configuration — three on each bank. Stellantis recommends spark plug replacement at 60,000 miles. In practice, iridium plugs often go longer; copper plugs need replacement closer to 30,000 miles if the JT does significant idling or towing.

The 3.6L Pentastar is a well-proven engine but the V6 layout means the rear bank (cylinders 4, 5, 6) is significantly harder to access than the front bank. Budget extra time for the rear plugs — they require working over the top of the engine near the firewall, and a ratchet with a universal joint or swivel extension makes the job manageable without skinning knuckles. A spark plug socket with a rubber insert (to grip the plug during extraction) is mandatory — dropping a plug down into the cylinder head on the rear bank is a very bad day.

**Factory plug specification:** The JT 3.6L uses a 14mm thread, 5/8" socket plug gapped at 0.042". Confirm with your NGK or Denso application guide before purchasing — plug length matters and an incorrect plug depth can cause pre-ignition damage.

  • **Stripped head threads**: If a plug feels loose when torquing (no resistance increasing), stop immediately. The threads are stripped or the plug is cross-threaded. Do not force it. Thread repair inserts (Heli-Coil) can fix this but require careful work.
  • **Broken plug removal**: On high-mileage JTs with original plugs, the ceramic can separate from the steel body during removal. Soak the plugs with penetrating oil two hours before the job if the vehicle has significant mileage or has been sitting.
  • **Rear bank coil accessibility**: Coils 4–6 are partially blocked by the firewall and intake runners. A 1/4" drive ratchet with a short extension sometimes works better than a 3/8" on the tightest rear coils.

Champion Platinum set of 6: ~$35. Adequate for normal use, shorter service life than iridium.

NGK Iridium IX set of 6: ~$48. The right choice for most JT owners — iridium lasts longer, fires more consistently, and the price premium over copper pays back in extended interval.

Denso Iridium TT set of 6: ~$58. Slightly better electrode design than standard iridium — minimal real-world difference from NGK Iridium on a naturally aspirated street/trail JT.

Mopar OEM set of 6: ~$65. Factory-spec iridium, carries a Mopar warranty. No performance benefit over NGK or Denso iridium — the premium is the brand.

Shop labor for spark plug replacement on a V6 (accessible bank + rear bank): ~$200–300. The rear bank access is what shops charge for — it's legitimate labor time. If you're comfortable with the task, the DIY save is real.

Tools required

  • 5/8" spark plug socket
  • 3/8" or 1/2" ratchet with 6" extension
  • torque wrench
  • spark plug gap tool
  • compressed air (for blowing out wells)
  • dielectric grease
  • anti-seize compound
