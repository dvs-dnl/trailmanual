Skid Plate Kit for the Gladiator JT

Difficulty 2/52–4 hrs$399–7992020-2024

The Gladiator JT's longer 137" wheelbase means more underbody exposure per trail obstacle — skid plates that fit the JL won't cover the full length of the JT, and buying a JL set is money wasted.

Stock JT underbody protection is minimal outside of a factory transfer case skid on Rubicon trims. The oil pan, transmission, fuel tank, and the stretch of unprotected frame between those components are all exposed to rock strikes on any trail with ledges or embedded rock. For a truck with the JT's capability envelope — and the market it attracts, which skews toward owners who actually use the truck — running without skids is a one-trail mistake.

The coverage priority on the JT is: (1) engine/oil pan, (2) transmission, (3) transfer case, (4) fuel tank. A kit that addresses all four gives you full protection from bumper to bumper on the underside. The JT's longer wheelbase means the fuel tank sits further back than on the JL, so JT-specific skids extend coverage rearward in a way JL skids can't. Teraflex's JT kit ($499) covers the first three in one purchase; the Rock Hard 4x4 system ($699) extends full coverage to the fuel tank and adds heavier plate gauge (3/16" vs. 1/8") for a rock-crawling-focused build. Rubicon Express's $449 kit is the value option and covers the essential surfaces without the upgrade-gauge material.

Plate gauge matters when you're shopping. 1/8" (11 gauge) steel is adequate for trail skimming and ledge glances; 3/16" is the right call for sustained rock contact in a crawling context. Aluminum skid plates exist in this price category too — they're significantly lighter but dent rather than deflect on hard hits, which can transfer impact energy to the component they're protecting. Steel is the correct choice for JTs going on rock.

Full-length skid coverage changes your maintenance routine, and it's better to plan for it than discover it at the next oil change. Most JT skid systems put a removable panel over the oil pan and drain plug — confirm yours does before you buy, and note which bolts come out for an oil change (usually four to six). Bag and label the hardware the first time so re-install goes quickly.

The transmission and transfer case fill/drain plugs sit behind a fixed panel on some kits; if you service your own drivetrain fluids, choose a system with access ports or removable sections at those points. Rock Hard's system and Teraflex's kit both leave the common service points reachable — verify against your specific fluid-change routine.

Bury the mounting hardware flush or recessed. Bolt heads that protrude below the plate catch on rock and shear off — a skid held by half its bolts flexes into the component it protects. Use the countersunk hardware the kit supplies.

Steel plates and an aluminum crossmember create a galvanic-corrosion couple in wet or salted conditions. In a rust-belt climate, use anti-seize on the fasteners and check the mounting points annually. Desert JTs don't face this, but the plates still trap trail mud against the frame — rinse the underbody after muddy runs so packed clay doesn't hold moisture against bare steel.

Skids are the underbody half of a protection package; rock sliders cover the rockers and a front bumper protects the nose. If you run larger tires and wheel aggressively, prioritize the heavier 3/16" plate gauge discussed above.

Steps

  1. Raise the vehicle and support on jack stands under the frame at all four corners — you need clearance to work under the full length of the truck.
  2. Locate the factory mounting points on the frame per the kit instructions. Most JT skid systems use existing threaded frame holes; some require supplied nut plates slid into the frame channel.
  3. Start with the forward engine/transmission skid: position the plate, insert all bolts hand-tight, then torque in a cross pattern.
  4. Install the transfer case skid next if supplied separately — overlap the rearmost edge of the transmission skid for continuous coverage.
  5. Install the fuel tank skid last — verify it doesn't contact the fuel tank directly at any point before tightening.
  6. Inspect all mounting bolts after first trail use and re-torque as needed.

Why it works

  • Protects the most vulnerable underbody components from rock strikes, ledges, and road debris
  • JT-specific fitment covers the full wheelbase length — no coverage gaps
  • Steel construction deflects impacts rather than transmitting them to the component
  • Bolt-on install accessible to any home mechanic with basic tools

Trade-offs

  • JL skid plates will not cover the JT's full length — verify JT fitment before ordering
  • Adds 30–70 lbs of underbody weight depending on gauge and coverage
  • Access to oil drain plug and transmission service points may require removing a skid panel — note this before your first oil change
  • Quality steel plates at this price tier have basic powder coat that chips; touch up annually

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • socket set
  • torque wrench
