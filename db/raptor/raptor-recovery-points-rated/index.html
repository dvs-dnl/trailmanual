Rated Recovery Points and Soft Shackles

Difficulty 2/51–3 hrs$120–4502010-2014, 2017-2020, 2021-2024

Run a Raptor at desert pace and it will eventually bog in sand, silt, or a hidden hole — that is the nature of the terrain. The difference between a thirty-second recovery and a dangerous one is whether the truck has genuine rated recovery points. This is inexpensive, critical safety gear, and it belongs on the truck before the first hard run, not after.

Some Raptors ship with tow hooks meant for flat-tow tie-down, not the shock load of a kinetic yank. A kinetic recovery rope stores enormous energy, and a failed mount or a slip-hook becomes a projectile. Properly rated, bolt-through recovery points or a shackle mount that ties into the frame give you a known, safe attachment for the strap or rope. Confirm the rating and the mounting into structure, not sheet metal.

Modern recovery has moved to soft shackles and kinetic energy ropes: lighter, safer when something lets go, and kinder to the mount than a steel bow shackle. A kinetic rope stretches to smooth the pull, which recovers a sand-stuck truck without the violent jerk of a static strap. Carry a pair of soft shackles, a kinetic rope sized to the truck, and a dampener to throw over the line during a pull.

Fit rated points front and rear so you can be pulled either direction. Recover in a straight line where possible, keep bystanders well clear of the rope's arc, and never join two straps with a metal shackle. Pair the points with the traction boards and winch in the companion guides — boards first for self-recovery, a strap or winch when you need help.

Rated recovery points and soft shackles are the cheapest critical safety upgrade on a Raptor. Fit frame-mounted points front and rear, carry a kinetic rope and soft shackles with a line dampener, and recover in a straight line with bystanders clear. Do this before you run the truck hard, not after you are buried.
