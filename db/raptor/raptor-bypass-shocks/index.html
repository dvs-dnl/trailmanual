Bypass Shock Upgrade — King and Fox 3.0

Difficulty 4/54–10 hrs$4000–90002010-2014, 2017-2020, 2021-2024

The Raptor is one of the only trucks that arrives with genuine internal-bypass shocks, so a shock upgrade is not about fixing a weakness — it is about buying more capacity for owners who run fast and long enough to heat-fade the factory units. This card is the premium step beyond the companion shock-upgrade guide, and an honest look at who actually needs it.

Larger-body 3.0 internal-bypass coilovers and rear shocks from King, Fox, or Bilstein hold far more oil and offer position-sensitive damping — plush in the early travel for washboard, then ramping firm deep in the stroke to resist bottoming on big hits and jumps. The extra fluid volume resists the heat fade that flattens any shock on a long, hard desert run, so the truck stays composed lap after lap where the stock shocks would soften.

Be honest about your use. If you run high-speed whoops, race-pace desert, or repeated big landings, the 3.0 bypass package is a real, felt upgrade and the long-travel builds in the sources are built around it. If you mostly overland and trail at moderate speed, the factory Fox shocks — kept fresh per the rebuild schedule — already do the job, and the money is better spent elsewhere. This is a top-tier expense.

Bigger shocks pair with a width decision: a bolt-in 3.0 in the stock geometry, or a mid- or long-travel arm kit that widens the track for more wheel travel and a smoother ride at speed. Going wider means new arms, possibly new CVs, and fender work. Whichever path, bypass shocks must be tuned — valving and bypass-tube settings dialed to your weight and terrain — and the truck aligned afterward.

Step to 3.0 King or Fox bypass shocks only if you run hard enough to fade the factory Fox units; otherwise maintain the excellent stock shocks instead. Decide on travel width up front, budget for arms and tuning, and align afterward. For a true desert runner it is the suspension that unlocks the chassis.
