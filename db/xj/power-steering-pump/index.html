Power Steering Pump Replacement

Difficulty: intermediate

Replace a whining or leaking power steering pump on the 4.0L XJ — and know why the pulley swap, not the pump itself, is the part that trips people up.

A worn XJ power steering pump announces itself two ways: a whine or groan that rises with engine RPM and gets loudest at full steering lock, or a film of fluid creeping down the front of the block from the shaft seal. Low fluid and trapped air whine too, so top off and bleed before you condemn the pump — but a pump that whines with a full, foam-free reservoir, or one that leaks from the shaft, is done. The job itself is straightforward wrenching. The catch is that almost every replacement pump ships without a pulley, and that pulley is pressed on. You move your old pulley to the new pump with a dedicated puller-and-installer tool, and that step is where a careless swap turns into a bent pulley or a ruined pump.

Steps

  1. Park on level ground, engine cold, and disconnect the negative battery cable. Empty the reservoir with a turkey baster or fluid pump to cut down the mess.
  2. Relieve the serpentine belt with the tensioner tool or a breaker bar on the tensioner pulley, and slip the belt off the power steering pulley.
  3. Place a drain pan under the pump. Disconnect the high-pressure line at the pump with a flare-nut wrench — a regular open-end will round the fitting. Then loosen the return-hose clamp and pull the return line. Let both drain and cap or bag the line ends.
  4. Remove the three pump-to-bracket mounting bolts. On most XJs you reach two from the front and one from behind through an access hole in the pulley — rotate the pulley to line the hole up with each bolt. Lift the pump out.
  5. Mount the old pump in a vise by its body, not the reservoir. Set up the pulley puller per its instructions and draw the pulley straight off. Pull it square — cocking it bends the pulley and it'll wobble the belt forever after.
  6. Before pressing the pulley onto the new pump, note the original's depth: the pulley face should sit flush with the end of the pump shaft, not proud and not buried. Use the installer tool to press the pulley on by threading against the shaft — never hammer it on, which drives debris through the front bearing.
  7. Transfer the reservoir to the new pump if your replacement came bare, using a fresh O-ring at the reservoir-to-pump seam.
  8. Set the pump back on the bracket and start all three mounting bolts by hand before torquing any. Torque the pump mounting bolts to 20 ft-lb and the bracket-to-engine bolts, if you removed them, to 35 ft-lb.
  9. Connect the return hose with a fresh clamp. Connect the high-pressure line with a new O-ring or sealing washer and torque the fitting to 21 ft-lb — snug but not gorilla-tight, since it seals on the O-ring, not by crushing metal.
  10. Reinstall the serpentine belt, confirming it's seated in every pulley groove before releasing the tensioner.
  11. Fill the reservoir with the correct fluid for your year (early XJs use power steering fluid; later years call for ATF+4 — check your cap or manual). Bleed the system: with the front wheels off the ground and engine off, turn the wheel slowly lock to lock six to ten times, topping off as the level drops. This pulls air out without churning it into foam.
  12. Start the engine and turn lock to lock a few more times at idle. Recheck the level, inspect every fitting for leaks, and confirm the whine is gone. A little noise on the first start that fades over a minute is normal as the last air clears; a whine that stays means air is still trapped — repeat the cold, engine-off bleed.

Why it works

  • Kills the whine and the slow shaft-seal leak that otherwise drips onto the belt and front crossmember
  • A new or quality reman pump restores full assist at low-speed lock, where a tired pump fades worst
  • Pump, reservoir, and lines are all accessible from the top — no axle or suspension teardown

Trade-offs

  • The pressed-on pulley swap requires the dedicated puller-and-installer tool; a standard jaw puller will bend the pulley, so rent the right one
  • Most parts-store reman units are the same Cardone core regardless of the "premium" label on the box — a new pump is often worth the extra money for a part you don't want to do twice
  • Air is stubborn in this system; skipping the slow engine-off bleed leaves a whine that mimics a bad pump and sends people chasing a part they already replaced
  • Pressing the pulley to the wrong depth misaligns the belt and wears it fast — match the original depth exactly

Tools required

  • Floor jack and jack stands
  • Power steering pump pulley puller and installer (rentable at most parts stores)
  • Flare-nut wrench for the high-pressure fitting
  • Metric sockets and wrenches (10mm, 13mm, 15mm, 18mm)
  • Torque wrench
  • Serpentine belt tensioner tool or breaker bar
  • Drain pan and fluid funnel
