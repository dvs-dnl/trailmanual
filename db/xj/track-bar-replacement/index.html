Track Bar Replacement and Upgrade

Difficulty 2/51–3 hrs$80–2501984-1990, 1991-1995, 1996, 1997-2001

The front track bar locates the axle side to side under the Jeep — a worn one lets the axle wander, which shows up as loose steering and is a leading cause of death wobble.

The front track bar is the diagonal link that ties the axle to the unibody and keeps the axle centered as the suspension cycles. The coil springs hold the XJ up, but they do nothing to stop the axle from sliding left or right — that job belongs entirely to the track bar. When its bushings crack or the frame-end mount wears, the axle shifts under cornering and braking, your steering feels vague, and on a lifted XJ the slop becomes a death-wobble trigger. Replacing the bar restores a centered axle and tight steering. If your Jeep is lifted, this is also the moment to fix the geometry the lift threw off by switching to an adjustable bar that re-centers the axle.

Steps

  1. Park on level ground and chock the rear wheels. You want the suspension loaded at ride height for the final torque, so plan to do the final tightening with the Jeep's weight on the wheels — not hanging on jack stands.
  2. Soak both track bar fasteners with penetrating oil before you touch them. The frame-end bolt and the axle-end stud have sat in road grime for decades — give the oil time to work, even overnight on a winter Jeep.
  3. Loosen and remove the frame-end fastener first. On most XJs this is a bolt through a bushed eye on the driver-side frame bracket. If the bolt is seized in the sleeve, soak it again and work it back and forth rather than forcing it.
  4. Free the axle end. On the axle side the bar usually attaches with a tapered stud into the axle bracket. Pull the cotter pin if equipped, back off the nut, and separate the taper with a ball joint / tie rod separator. Strike the bracket ear — never the stud threads — if it's stubborn.
  5. Remove the old bar. Compare it side by side against the new bar before you install anything: confirm the eye and stud orientation, length, and bend match your application.
  6. If you're reusing the original bar with fresh bushings, press the old bushings out and the new ones in now, and grease them lightly on install.
  7. Hang the new bar at the frame end first, hand-tight. Leave the fastener loose enough that the bar can pivot.
  8. Connect the axle end. The axle-side hole rarely lines up on the first try — have a helper turn the steering wheel (or pry the axle over with a bar) until the stud or bolt drops through. On an adjustable bar, set the length so the axle sits centered under the Jeep: measure from the frame rail to the wheel on both sides and adjust until they match.
  9. Seat the axle-end stud and snug the nut. Install a new cotter pin if your bar uses a castle nut; advance the nut to the next slot to line up the hole rather than backing it off.
  10. Apply blue thread locker to the frame-end bolt threads, then bring both ends to hand-snug.
  11. Lower the Jeep so the suspension carries its full weight at ride height. Now torque the fasteners: frame-end ball-stud nut to roughly 74 ft-lb, axle-end bolt to roughly 63 ft-lb. These figures vary slightly by year and by aftermarket bar — confirm against your factory service manual or the bar's instructions, and follow the manufacturer's spec if it differs.
  12. Recheck axle centering and steering wheel position. If the wheel is off-center or the axle still sits to one side, adjust an adjustable bar; a fixed bar that leaves the axle off-center on a lifted Jeep is the sign you need the adjustable unit.
  13. Test drive at low speed first, then recheck both fasteners after the first 50 miles. Suspension fasteners settle, and the track bar is not one to let loosen.

Why it works

  • Restores a centered axle and tight, predictable steering
  • Removes one of the top death-wobble triggers on lifted XJs
  • An adjustable bar corrects the axle shift a lift introduces, fixing alignment and reducing driveline angle issues
  • Doable in an afternoon with hand tools and a separator

Trade-offs

  • Seized frame-end bolts are common on older or rust-belt XJs and can turn a quick job into a fight
  • A fixed replacement bar won't re-center the axle on a lifted Jeep — if you're lifted, you probably want the adjustable bar instead
  • You'll likely want to verify alignment and steering wheel centering afterward
  • The axle-end taper can be stubborn to separate without the right tool

Tools required

  • floor jack and jack stands
  • metric and SAE sockets
  • torque wrench
  • breaker bar
  • tie rod / ball joint separator
  • penetrating oil
  • pry bar
  • thread locker (blue)
