Closed Cooling Overheating Fix Sequence

Difficulty 2/51–6 hrs$15–6001984-1990, 1991-1995, 1996, 1997-2001

Methodical sequence to diagnose XJ 4.0L overheating: fan clutch, thermostat, radiator, water pump, head gasket. Skip steps and you'll spend money on the wrong thing.

XJs run hot in summer when neglected. The standard diagnostic order from cheapest/most-likely to most-expensive:

1. Verify temp gauge: clip-on IR thermometer at the thermostat housing — gauge can be off by 20F either way.

2. Coolant fill and air bleed: trapped air in the heater core or block reads as overheating. Park nose-up, fill slowly with the heat on max.

3. Fan clutch test: cold engine, spin fan by hand — should freewheel 1-3 revs. Hot engine, hood off, fan should be hard to spin. If it freewheels hot, fan clutch is bad. ($60 part, 30 min job.)

4. Thermostat: a stuck-open thermostat means the engine never reaches operating temp; a stuck-closed means instant overheat. Test in boiling water. ($15 part.)

5. Radiator condition: 25-year-old plastic-tank radiators clog with scale internally. Front fins clog with bug schmoo externally. Power-wash from the engine side. If the truck overheats highway-only (low airflow at idle is fine), the rad is suspect.

6. Water pump: a tired pump cavitates at high RPM. Pull the fan and feel the shaft — any play means replace.

7. Coolant temp sensor (the gauge sender, not the PCM sensor): can lie. Cheap to replace.

8. Head gasket: leaking combustion gas into coolant pressurizes the system, pushes coolant into the reservoir, and overheats with bubbles. Block tester from any auto parts store ($30 rental).

The 'XJ overheating bingo' on Cherokee Forum has a chart for this. Most overheats are #3 or #5.
