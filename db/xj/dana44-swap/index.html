Dana 44 Rear Axle Swap Overview

Difficulty 4/516–40 hrs$800–50001984-1990, 1991-1995, 1996, 1997-2001
Short answer

Whether you actually need a Dana 44 rear under your XJ — and which donor to chase — instead of the cheaper, more common Ford 8.8. Most builds don't need it. The ones that do should know what they're buying.

The stock Dana 35 rear is the weakest link on a built XJ. Its center casting and axle tubes are smaller than a Dana 44's, so the whole assembly flexes under load — that flex is what snaps shafts, gears, and carriers, not only the thin 27-spline shafts. Once you're on 33s with a locker, or 35s at all, the D35 is living on borrowed time. The question is what replaces it. For most people the answer is a Ford 8.8 (see that guide). The Dana 44 is the next step up, and it earns its place on a narrower set of builds than the forums imply.

A complete Dana 44 weighs roughly 230 lbs versus the Dana 35's 175 — about 30% more iron in the housing and tubes, which is where the real strength gain lives. Reach for it when you're running 37s, you've done a torquey engine swap (4.6 stroker, 5.3/5.7 LS or Hemi), or you compete on rock. Below that, the 8.8 matches it for less money and easier sourcing.

TJ Rubicon Dana 44 (2003-2006) is the popular choice: factory air or e-locker depending on year, disc brakes, 30-spline shafts (upgradeable to 33 or 35), 4.10 or 4.56 gears. Salvage price has climbed to $1,200-1,800 because everyone wants them. Width is the catch — it's roughly 3 inches wider than a stock XJ rear, so you'll run wheels with less backspacing or wider fenders to keep tires off the flares.

ZJ Grand Cherokee Dana 44 (1998 5.9 Limited, some V8 HD) sits closer to XJ width and often carries a factory limited-slip and discs, around $900 salvage. The trap: many ZJ and all WJ rears are the Dana 44a aluminum-center axle. It's fine on the street, but the aluminum casting is a dead end for serious gearing and locker upgrades. Verify cast iron before you buy.

Factory XJ Dana 44 — some 1987-1989 Cherokees with the tow package left the factory with a real Dana 44 rear. They're nearly impossible to find now because they've all been pulled for swaps. If you stumble on one, it bolts in with only a driveshaft tweak, since the D44 pinion sits about an inch longer than the D35's.

Aftermarket crate (Currie, Dynatrac, Ten Factory) is built to your spec — 35-spline shafts, 1-ton bearings, your gear ratio, your locker, your width. $2,500-5,000. The right call if you want it done once and done right and you're past worrying about budget.

Steps

  1. Decide honestly whether you're past the 8.8's limits — 37s, big-torque swap, or competition. If not, build the 8.8 instead and keep the cash.
  2. Pick a donor and verify the center is cast iron, not the Dana 44a aluminum housing. Confirm the gear ratio stamped on the tag matches your front Dana 30 exactly.
  3. Measure the donor's wheel-mounting-surface width and compare to your XJ. Plan backspacing or fender work before the axle is under the truck, not after.
  4. Cut the donor's factory spring perches and shock mounts off the tubes with a cutoff wheel.
  5. Set perch-to-perch width to the XJ spec of 43.5625 inches using a swap kit's jig, then MIG weld the new perches and lower shock mounts. Full-penetration welds — this is a load-bearing structure.
  6. Sort the brakes: adapt the donor's hard line to the XJ master cylinder flare, and adapt or relocate the e-brake cable ends.
  7. Shorten or replace the rear driveshaft for the longer pinion and any pinion-angle change — a CV shaft paired with an SYE is the durable answer.
  8. Set pinion angle, torque the U-bolts, bleed the brakes, and check ring-and-pinion backlash if you re-geared.

Why it works

  • Strongest realistic rear for 37s and high-torque engine swaps
  • Factory locker and disc brakes on Rubicon and some ZJ donors
  • Deep aftermarket support for 33/35-spline shaft and gear upgrades
  • Heavier casting and tubes resist the flex that kills a Dana 35

Trade-offs

  • Costs more than a Ford 8.8 for the same strength up to 35s — overkill on most builds
  • TJ Rubicon donors are wide and getting expensive; ZJ/WJ aluminum 44a is an upgrade dead end
  • Welding, brake-line adapting, and driveshaft work all required
  • You probably don't need it if you're on 33s or 35s with a stock-ish engine — the 8.8 is the smarter money

Tools required

  • MIG welder
  • cutoff wheel
  • angle grinder
  • metric sockets
  • torque wrench
  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • brake bleeding tools
  • tape measure
