Jeep Cherokee XJ / Armor & Skids

XJ Gas Tank Skid Plate — Options, Nut Strips, and Install

Difficulty 3/52–6 hrs$260–5601984-1990, 1991-1995, 1996, 1997-2001
Short answer

The XJ fuel tank hangs behind the rear axle with a stamped shield under it, and a 3/16 in plate over that costs $260 to $500 — but the thing most write-ups get wrong is the install: bolt-on gas tank skids hang from the rear frame-rail nut strips, so the tank stays where it is.

The tank on an XJ is a plastic shell carried in two straps aft of the rear axle, with a thin stamped shield under it from the factory. The shield turns branches and parking curbs. It creases on a ledge, and a creased shield presses into the tank. The failure that ends a trip is not a neat puncture — it is a crushed shield wearing a hole through plastic over the next twenty miles, with fuel on hot exhaust.

Symptoms that say you need this: a dented or folded factory shield, scrape marks on the tank itself, a shield rusted thin enough to flex under a thumb, or a fuel smell after a rocky run. If you have added a lift and 31s or larger you are driving over bigger obstacles at the same tank height, not a higher one. A rig that sees fire roads can run the factory shield for years. A rig that sees ledges cannot.

How these actually mount, and why that matters. The bolt-on skids from Iron Rock, JCR, Rock Hard, RuffStuff, Rusty's, Skid Row, and Barnes all hang from the same place: the pre-drilled factory hitch mounting points in the rear unibody rails, where two nut strips slide in. The plate goes up over the existing plastic shield, not in place of it, and the tank and straps stay untouched. That means no fuel work, no tank drop, and no fuel-pressure relief for a straight skid install — which is a different and much shorter job than the forum consensus suggests. You drop the tank only to replace a rotted factory steel shield on an 84-96, or to service the tank, pump, or straps; that procedure lives in Fuel Tank Skid and Tank Drop Procedure.

What stops this job cold: the nut strips. If the rig has a factory or aftermarket receiver hitch, the strips are already in the rails and you share four bolt holes per side with the hitch, using longer bolts. If it never had a hitch, or the strips have rusted away in a salt-belt truck, you are pulling the rear bumper to slide new strips in. Budget for them: a full set with all 10 holes and 1/2 in-13 Grade 8 hardware runs about $58. Thread pitch is worth checking before you order, because the factory strips are not consistent — owners report metric M12x1.75 on the passenger side of some trucks while the aftermarket replacements are 1/2 in-13. Thread a bolt in by hand before you buy a box of the wrong ones. Soak the bumper bolts with penetrating oil the day before; they are the fasteners most likely to shear.

Year split is real. The 84-96 and 97-01 plates are not interchangeable. Owners have forced an early skid onto a late truck by trimming the rear edge and folding out the lip, and a 93-98 ZJ skid onto a 97-01 XJ by opening two holes about 1/4 in — both work, neither is a bolt-on, and Iron Rock charges restocking plus two-way freight on a wrong-year return. Order to your year.

What you give up. These plates are 60 to 68 lb shipped, which is 45 to 60 lb added behind the rear axle, on the end of the lever arm that already sags on a loaded XJ. Several designs delete the factory exhaust heat shield and leave roughly 4 in between pipe and tank at the corner, and most are cut for exhaust up to 3 in — larger than that and you are massaging the pipe. The passenger-side tailpipe hanger usually needs a pry bar to clear. And a plate under a tank is a mud shelf: pack it with clay and leave it, and you have built a wet poultice against the shield. Hose it out when you wash the rig.

On HDPE. UHMW and HDPE belly panels are popular because they slide and they do not rust, but they are abrasion protection, not impact protection. Over a fuel tank, use steel.

Steps

  1. Park on level ground, chock the front wheels, and lift the rear. Support the unibody on four stands rated for the vehicle — never work under a jack alone. Fuel level does not matter for a bolt-on skid, since the tank is not coming down.
  2. Confirm which plate you have and that it matches your year range, 84-96 or 97-01. Test fit it on the ground against the tank area first; a plate that needs trimming is easier to find now than overhead.
  3. Check for nut strips. Reach into the rear frame rails at the hitch mounting holes and thread one of the supplied bolts in by hand. If it threads, you have strips and you can skip to step 6. If it spins free, you need strips.
  4. To install strips: remove the rear bumper end caps and the bumper, 15 mm hardware, after soaking the bolts. Slide a nut strip into each rail with the bends pointing up against the outer wall of the rail, and line the holes up with the rail holes.
  5. Wire-brush the rail flanges, treat any surface rust, and let the chassis paint flash off before anything gets bolted over it. Steel armor bolted to a soft sill tears the sill — if the rail is scaled or flexes, stop and read Rust Treatment — Surface vs. Structural first.
  6. Trim the plastic tank shield if the plate will not clear it. A strip about 1-1/2 in off each outer end, or a cut along the outside of each strap, is the common fix. Keep the shield under the plate rather than discarding it.
  7. Free the passenger-side tailpipe hanger with a pry bar and set the pipe aside from the mounting area. If the plate deletes the factory exhaust heat shield, confirm you have about 4 in between pipe and tank at the corner before committing.
  8. Raise the plate with a floor jack and a stack of 2x4s, or a second jack with a wide pad, and walk it up in stages. It is a tight fit and roughly 50 lb overhead — get a second set of hands rather than balancing it.
  9. Start every bolt by hand before tightening any of them. With a receiver hitch, run longer bolts through both the hitch bracket and the skid bracket so the skid can come off later without pulling the hitch.
  10. Snug the hardware in a cross pattern so the plate seats flat, then torque the 1/2 in-13 Grade 8 bolts to 75 ft-lb. That is below a dry Grade 8 spec on purpose: the strip is captive in sheet metal and will deform or pull before the bolt does. Stop and reassess if a fastener keeps turning without loading up — that is a strip tearing.
  11. Reinstall the exhaust hanger, then the bumper brackets, bumper, and end caps, 15 mm hardware.
  12. Lower the rig and check clearances cold: pipe-to-plate at idle and with the engine rocked in gear, filler neck and vent hose routing, and the fuel lines and pump access panel if the plate reaches that far forward.
  13. Retorque every skid bolt after the first trail run, and again after the first hard hit. Inspect the plate's forward lip for a folded edge — a lip bent down becomes a hook on the next ledge.

Why it works

  • 3/16 in steel over the one tank that holds 20 gallons of fuel behind the axle
  • Bolts to factory hitch-point nut strips, so no tank drop, no fuel work, no pressure relief
  • Spreads a hit across the rails instead of into the stamped shield
  • Comes off in four bolts per side for exhaust or pump access, if you run longer hitch bolts
  • At $260 to $500 it is the cheapest armor on the rig measured against what it prevents

Trade-offs

  • 45 to 60 lb added behind the rear axle, where an XJ is already soft
  • Nut strips add roughly $58 and a bumper removal if the rig never had a hitch
  • 84-96 and 97-01 plates do not interchange, and wrong-year returns carry restocking plus two-way freight
  • Several designs delete the factory exhaust heat shield and limit you to 3 in exhaust
  • Packs mud against the tank shield and traps moisture if it never gets washed out
  • Does nothing for the tank's leading edge at the filler neck, which is still exposed on a steep side hill

Tools required

  • Floor jack plus a stack of 2x4s or a second jack with a wide pad (to raise the plate)
  • Jack stands rated for the vehicle, four
  • Metric socket set, 3/8 in and 1/2 in drive (15 mm and 12 mm for bumper and rail hardware)
  • 3/4 in socket or wrench for 1/2 in-13 skid hardware
  • Torque wrench reading 20 to 100 ft-lb
  • Breaker bar and penetrating oil
  • Pry bar (exhaust hanger clearance)
  • Angle grinder or reciprocating saw for seized bumper bolts
  • Tin snips or a cutoff wheel to trim the plastic tank shield
  • Wire brush, rust converter, and chassis paint
