33s and 35s on the JK: Lift, Backspacing, and Gears for 2-Door and JKU

Difficulty 2/51–4 hrs$1100–22002007-2011, 2012-2018
Short answer

A JK fits 33s at stock height for street use, but expect rubbing once it flexes. For 35s, plan on at least 2 to 2.5 inches of lift and wheels with about 4.5 inches of backspacing, and budget for gears if the Jeep left the factory on 3.21s. Fender trimming is the common wild card.

ExtremeTerrain's door-placard list shows how the JK started: 225/75R16 to 255/75R17 on most Sports and Saharas, and 255/75R17 on the Rubicon. One JKU Rubicon owner measures that Rubicon tire at about 32 inches.

Sources disagree on the edges, so treat these as ranges rather than limits. Quadratec's wheel FAQ says up to 33s with no lift and up to 35s at 2 to 3 inches. ExtremeTerrain reports owners fitting 33s at stock height but warns rubbing likely follows and off-road use is another matter. One owner writes that a 33 on stock suspension rubs off-road and may touch in full on-road turns. 4 Wheel Parts is the most conservative, calling for 3.5 to 6 inches for off-road 35s.

Owner reports on WAYALIFE fill in the middle:

  • 33s, no lift: a 2-door ran 295/70R17 on stock fenders with no rub around town but had not flexed it. Another 2-door ran 285/75R16 with no lift. A JKU ran 33x12.50R15 on 15-inch wheels with no rub.
  • 35s at 2.5 inches: one owner ran 35x12.50s on factory wheels with 1.5-inch spacers (about 4.75 inches of effective backspacing) and no rubbing. A 2-door with trimmed fenders and 4.10s also reports no rubbing.
  • Lower lift, more cutting: an owner at stock height with cut fenders and 35s still rubbed the stock bumper and had to clearance the Rubicon rails. Another 2-door runs 35s on 1-inch coil spacers and 1-inch bump stops.
  • A failed test: JeepFan tried a 35 on stock JK rims with a TeraFlex 2.5-inch Budget Boost. It would not fit at the rear without wheel spacers and hit the driver-side rear upper control arm, so that build went to a 295/70R17 instead.

No source opened for this guide documents a different wheel-well limit between the two, and owners on both report the same lift and backspacing numbers. The difference is wheelbase (95.4 inches on the 2-door, 116 on the JKU) and how much weight the gears move, so the heavier JKU is the stronger case for regearing. If you want a single answer on clearance, use the same table for both and verify on your own Jeep.

Bolt pattern is 5x127mm (5x5). Factory JK backspacing is deep: one owner of a 2018 Rubicon gives 6.25 inches, and 4 Wheel Parts puts most JKs at 5 to 7 inches. That is why 35s rub the control arms on stock wheels.

  • 4.5 inches: the figure that owner calls standard for JK 35s. Hub bore on those wheels was 71.5mm.
  • 4.75 inches: two owners report no rubbing on 35s at 2.25 to 2.5 inches of lift, one of them with 2-inch bump stops.
  • 5.2 inches: one owner's AEV wheels on 35s rubbed the control arms slightly at full lock.
  • Wider tires: another owner warns that 13-inch and wider tires may rub control arms at full lock.

Spacers work, but the wheel guide and spacer guide cover why dedicated wheels are the better long-term answer.

The spare needs a plan too. Moving from 32s on 6.25-inch wheels to 35s on 4.5-inch wheels, the owner above had to raise the aftermarket carrier bracket and move it inward, and swapped its grade 5 bolts for grade 8. A heavier spare on the factory tailgate belongs in the rear bumper and carrier guide.

The Rubicon rock rails are their own rub point. That same owner trimmed 1.5 inches off the rear section after articulation pushed the tire into them.

Factory gearing runs from 3.21 to 4.10. Quadratec's 2007 spec lists 3.21 standard and 4.10 optional on the manual, standard on the Rubicon, and 4.10 on the 42RLE automatic. Check yours before you plan anything.

You probably do not need gears for 33s on 4.10s. One 2-door owner on 35s and 4.10s has no plans to regear. A 2012 2-door owner on 3.21s was unhappy even on 33s and expected 35s to be worse. Another owner would regear to 4.56 or 4.88 for 35s and estimated $1,600 to $2,000.

ExtremeTerrain's chart at 65 mph in a 1:1 gear puts a 35 at about 2,328 rpm on 3.73, 2,845 on 4.56, and 3,045 on 4.88. Its suggested highway target is around 2,600 rpm, and overdrive drops real numbers below the chart. One 2-door on 4.88s and 35x12.50s reports about 2,800 rpm at 65 in 6th gear and says 4.56s would be fine without high-altitude highway driving. The JK regear guide has the process.

  • Track bar: JeepFan's test showed the axle moving at droop on a kit with no track bar correction. The track bar guide covers re-centering.
  • Dana 30: owners commonly add C-gussets once 35s go on. See the Dana 30 upgrade guide.
  • A factory-issued JK clearance figure for any size. All numbers here come from vendors and owners.
  • A 2-door vs JKU clearance difference, as above.
  • Pinch seam trim requirements on stock flares. Reports conflict and depend on the flare and tire.
  • A 35-inch price below about $417 each. The cost range here covers four 33s at the low end and five 35s at the high end, at Quadratec prices when written, with no lift, wheels, or gears.

Steps

  1. Confirm trim, axle ratio from the build sheet, and current wheel backspacing with a straightedge and tape.
  2. Choose 33s or 35s, then choose the lift and wheels together.
  3. Set lift and re-center the axle before you test tires.
  4. Mount one tire and wheel per corner you can test, and cycle the steering lock to lock.
  5. Compress the suspension or use a ramp, and mark contact points with chalk.
  6. Trim flares, liners, and pinch seams only after you know where the contact is.
  7. Tighten the lugs to the wheel maker's number. Northridge4x4's factory chart lists 100 ft-lb for the 22mm factory lug on a JK. Recheck after the first drive.
  8. Sort the spare carrier, correct the speedometer, and schedule gears as needed.

Why it works

  • 33s fit with little money beyond the tires.
  • 35s at 2.5 inches and 4.5 to 4.75 inches of backspacing is a well-documented setup.
  • Trucks with factory 4.10s can often put off the regear.

Trade-offs

  • Stock flares usually need trimming or replacement for full flex at 2.5 inches.
  • Owners on 3.21s report a poor match even at 33s, and gears can cost about as much as a set of tires.
  • 35s cost about $417 to $432 each at the listings checked.
  • Heavier tires load the spare carrier and the Dana 30.

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • torque wrench
  • tape measure
  • chalk or paint marker
