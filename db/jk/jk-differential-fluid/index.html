Jeep Wrangler JK / Maintenance

Front and Rear Differential Fluid Change

Difficulty 2/51–2 hrs$40–902007-2018Jump to steps
Short answer

Both differentials on a JK want fresh gear oil every 30,000 street miles — and immediately after any water crossing deep enough to submerge the axle tubes, regardless of when the last change was. The whole job is under two hours for both axles and costs less than $90.

The JK's front axle is a Dana 30 on Sport and Sahara trims and a Dana 44 on Rubicons. The rear is a Dana 44 across the JK range. Fluid service is nearly identical front to rear and is the cheapest insurance available on a ring and pinion set that costs $600–$1,200 to replace with labor.

Fluid choice and capacities

AxleApplicationCapacityNotes
Dana 30 frontSport / Sahara~1.05 qtOpen or aftermarket locker
Dana 44 frontRubicon~1.35 qtFactory electric locker
Dana 44 rearAll JK~2.375 qtOpen, optional Trac-Lok, or Rubicon electric locker

75W-90 GL-5 synthetic covers most JKs. Step up to 75W-140 if you tow near capacity, run 35-inch or larger tires, have regeared to 4.88 or numerically higher, or live somewhere that sees sustained 110°F ambient like Phoenix — all four load the gear teeth harder and put more heat into the housing, and the heavier grade holds its film under that load. The tradeoff is a measurable drag penalty when cold; a JK on 75W-140 in a Minnesota February gives up a little fuel economy for the first ten minutes of every drive.

Limited-slip additive is required on non-Rubicon rear axles ordered with the optional Trac-Lok clutch-type limited slip (see the rear locker guide), and on aftermarket clutch-type units. The Rubicon's factory lockers don't take it. One 4-oz bottle of Mopar 04318060AC per fill. Skip it and the clutch packs chatter in tight turns — a shuddering, grinding feel at parking-lot speeds that owners routinely misdiagnose as a u-joint. Electronic selectable lockers (Rubicon front and rear e-lockers, ARB Air Lockers) need no additive; they are mechanical dog-clutch designs with no friction plates.

Reading what comes out

This is the part of the job that has diagnostic value beyond the fluid change itself.

Normal: dark amber to black oil with a strong sulfur smell, and a light gray paste on the magnet. GL-5 gear oil is supposed to smell terrible; that is the extreme-pressure additive package doing its job.

Water intrusion: milky tan or coffee-with-cream colored fluid. Water enters through the axle vent tube during a crossing, or past worn axle seals. Water in gear oil strips the EP additive package and pits bearing races within a few hundred miles. If you find this, change the fluid, then extend the vent hose — routing the axle vents up into the engine bay is one of the highest-value modifications on a JK that sees water, and costs about $15 in hose.

Glitter or chunks: stop. Fine glitter suspended in the oil means a bearing is shedding. Chunks or curls of metal mean tooth damage. Refilling and driving on either accelerates the failure. Pull the cover, inspect the ring and pinion contact pattern, and check backlash before you commit to more miles.

If you bought the JK used, pull the cover on the first service rather than draining through the plug. You get to clean the housing properly, see the gear pattern, and confirm nobody sent it a rock through the cover.

What to watch for

  • Overtorquing plugs. The plug threads into cast iron on the Dana housings but the plugs themselves are soft. 25 ft-lb is the number; a stripped plug means a helicoil or a $6 replacement and a re-tap.
  • Skipping the additive on a Trac-Lok. The chatter it causes is not harmful immediately but it does accelerate clutch wear, and the fix is another fluid change.
  • Mixing grades. Topping a 75W-140 fill with 75W-90 is not dangerous, but you no longer know what viscosity you have. Drain and refill with one grade.
  • The vent. Check that the axle vent hose is present, clear, and routed upward. A blocked vent pressurizes the housing as it heats and pushes oil past the pinion seal, which presents as a mystery leak at the front of the diff.
  • Interval after water. The 30,000-mile figure assumes pavement. One deep crossing resets it to zero. See the transfer case fluid guide — the t-case has the same water-intrusion exposure and is usually worth doing in the same session.

Costs and sources

Two quarts of Valvoline SynPower for the front and three for the rear runs about $65. Mopar 75W-140 pushes that to $90. Add $12 for Trac-Lok additive and $9 for RTV if you are pulling covers. A shop charges $120–$180 per axle for the same service, which makes the DIY version one of the better hourly rates available to a JK owner. The regear guide covers what happens when you are inside the housing for real work rather than a fluid swap.

Steps

  1. Drive the Jeep 10–15 minutes. Warm oil drains faster and carries far more suspended contamination out with it.
  2. Park level, set the parking brake, chock the tires that stay down.
  3. Open the fill plug first. 3/8 square drive on most JKs, at roughly the 9–10 o'clock position on the cover. If it will not break loose with the diff still full, you have lost nothing. Discover that after draining and the Jeep is immobile.
  4. Position the drain pan and open the drain plug at the bottom of the cover.
  5. Let it drain fully — 10 minutes minimum. Inspect the magnet and the used oil against the guidance above.
  6. Clean the drain plug magnet with brake cleaner and a rag. Reinstall and torque to 25 ft-lb.
  7. Rubicon rear only: add the 4-oz limited-slip additive first, before the gear oil, so it mixes as you pump.
  8. Pump fresh 75W-90 (or 75W-140) through the fill hole until oil dribbles back out. That dribble is the correct level — there is no dipstick and no "topping up" past it. Excess gets pushed out the vent and onto your driveway.
  9. Reinstall the fill plug at 25 ft-lb.
  10. Repeat on the second axle. Front and rear procedures are identical apart from capacity.
  11. Cover-off service only: scrape both mating surfaces clean and degrease them, lay a 3/16-inch bead of Ultra Black RTV inboard of the bolt holes, let it skin 10 minutes, bolt the cover on in a crisscross pattern to the service manual figure (Project-JK lists 30 ft-lb, Northridge4x4 25 ft-lb), and let the RTV cure a full 4 hours before adding oil.
  12. Take the used oil to any auto parts store for free recycling.

Why it works

  • Cheap insurance against a $600–$1,200 ring-and-pinion failure
  • Catches water intrusion and bearing wear while both are still fixable
  • Both axles done in 1–2 hours with no specialty tooling beyond a $15 fluid pump
  • Doubles as an inspection of axle seals, vent routing, and cover condition

Trade-offs

  • Trac-Lok additive is a separate purchase with no substitute
  • Cover-off service adds a 4-hour RTV cure before you can refill
  • Used gear oil stains concrete and the smell lingers in a garage for days
  • Fill plugs on salt-belt JKs seize and occasionally round off

Tools required

  • 3/8 ratchet
  • 3/8 square drive or 13mm socket
  • fluid transfer pump
  • drain pan
  • torque wrench
  • 13mm socket (cover bolts)
  • gasket scraper
  • brake cleaner
