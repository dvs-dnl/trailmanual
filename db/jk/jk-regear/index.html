Jeep Wrangler JK / Drivetrain

Re-Gearing the JK — 4.56, 4.88, and 5.13 Ratios

Difficulty 5/56–12 hrs$800–24002007-2011, 2012-2018Jump to steps
Short answer

Re-gearing restores the JK's effective drive ratio after a tire size increase — it's not optional once you go beyond 33s, and the correct target ratio is determined by tire size and how the rig will be driven.

Non-Rubicon JKs left the factory on 3.21 or 3.73 gears. Quadratec's 2007–2011 spec lists 3.21 standard with the manual and 4.10 optional (standard on the Rubicon and listed with the 42RLE automatic), so read your axle tag. Those ratios were matched to factory tires such as the Sahara's 255/75R17, which works out to about 32.1 inches (255 × 0.75 × 2 ÷ 25.4 + 17). When you increase tire diameter, the effective gear ratio drops because each tire rotation covers more ground. Running 35" tires on 3.73 gears, for example, is equivalent to dropping your final drive ratio by roughly 0.40 — the engine lugs on highway grades, the transmission runs hotter, throttle response is dull, and the crawl ratio in low range is compromised. The fix is a regear: new ring and pinion sets in both front and rear axles that restore the effective ratio to near-stock or improve on it. Both axles must match — mismatched ratios between front and rear axles will damage the transfer case.

The ratio targets are established by tire size: 4.56 gearing is correct for 35" tires (common on mild-trail and overland builds), 4.88 is the standard for 37s and represents the best balance of highway performance and trail crawl ratio, and 5.13 is for heavily built 37" rigs in serious rock terrain where maximum mechanical advantage is the priority. Crawl ratio is axle ratio times low range times first gear. At 4.88 on a JK with the factory 2.72:1 low range, that's about 59:1 with the 6-speed manual (4.46 first) and 48:1 with the 5-speed automatic (3.59 first). The Rubicon's NV241OR has a 4:1 low range, which pushes those to about 87:1 and 70:1. Non-Rubicon owners can get the Rubicon's crawl ratio benefit from an aftermarket 4:1 low range kit, but that's a separate project.

The parts cost for a regear is only part of the total bill. Yukon's ring and pinion sets for the Dana 30 and Dana 44 run $189–$290 each, but a complete master install kit adds another $150–$200 per axle (carrier bearings, pinion bearings, pinion seal, crush sleeve or solid collar, and shims). Labor at a shop adds $300–$600 per axle depending on market. Total cost for both axles including parts and professional installation typically runs $1,200–$2,400. If you're experienced with differential setups, the DIY savings are real — but ring and pinion pattern setup is the highest-skill task in most DIY builds. An improperly set gear pattern that sounds fine initially will fail within 10,000–15,000 miles.

One critical timing point: pair the regear with a rear locker installation if you haven't already done one. The differential is already apart, the carrier is already out, and you're paying for differential labor once. Installing the locker at the same time as the regear saves a full shop visit and $200–$400 in labor overlap.

Steps

  1. Drain both axles of gear oil before starting.
  2. Remove the driveshafts, brake rotors, and axle shafts to access the differential assembly.
  3. Mark and remove bearing caps; pull the carrier assembly from the housing.
  4. Use a dial indicator to record existing backlash and bearing pre-load before disassembly (reference for new setup).
  5. Remove the old ring gear from the carrier and the pinion from the housing.
  6. Press on the new carrier bearings; bolt the new ring gear to the carrier with new bolts (ring gear bolts are not reusable).
  7. Set pinion depth using the manufacturer's shim data — this is the most critical measurement in the entire process.
  8. Set bearing pre-load on the pinion using a new crush sleeve or a solid collar with shims.
  9. Install the carrier assembly and set backlash to the manufacturer's specification (typically 0.006"–0.010").
  10. Check the gear contact pattern with marking compound; adjust shims as needed until the pattern is centered.
  11. Torque all bearing caps and hardware to factory spec.
  12. Reinstall axle shafts, seals, rotors, and driveshaft.
  13. Fill with the correct gear oil — 80W-90 for most applications, 75W-140 synthetic if a limited slip additive is not required.
  14. Repeat for the second axle before returning to service.

Why it works

  • Restores highway performance and fuel economy lost when upgrading tire size
  • Correct regear dramatically improves low-range crawl ratio — often more impactful than a locker on sustained technical terrain
  • On a Rubicon, both axles geared at 4.88 with the factory NV241OR give about a 70:1 crawl ratio with the 5-speed automatic and 87:1 with the 6-speed manual
  • Can be combined with rear locker installation to save labor cost

Trade-offs

  • Highest-skill DIY task on the JK — incorrect gear pattern setup causes premature failure
  • Must do both axles simultaneously — mismatched ratios destroy the transfer case
  • Total installed cost ($1,200–$2,400) surprises buyers who only price the ring and pinion sets
  • 5.13 gearing is rough at highway speed for daily-driven rigs — only appropriate for dedicated trail builds

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • torque wrench
  • socket set
  • dial indicator
  • bearing puller
  • crush sleeve eliminator or new crush sleeve
  • inch-pound torque wrench
  • differential setup tools
  • RTV sealant
