Jeep Wrangler TJ / Cooling

TJ 4.0L Cooling System Flush and Coolant Replacement

Difficulty 1/51–2 hrs$25–601997-2002, 2003-2006
Short answer

Drain the lower radiator hose, flush with water, refill with fresh 50/50 coolant — the TJ 4.0L holds about 2 gallons. Do it every 2 years or 30,000 miles regardless of color.

The TJ's AMC 4.0L inline-six runs a conventional green-formula coolant system. Chrysler spec is green (silicate-based) coolant, which depletes inhibitor faster than OAT or HOAT formulas — 2 years or 30,000 miles is the real service interval even if the coolant still looks green. Old coolant turns acidic and starts attacking aluminum components: the water pump impeller, the thermostat housing, and the heater core are the first casualties.

The TJ doesn't have a petcock drain on the radiator — you drain by disconnecting the lower radiator hose at the radiator inlet. It's a 30-second job but messy. Have a wide drain pan and rags ready.

Capacity is approximately 7.5 quarts (1.875 gallons) for the 4.0L. The recovery bottle holds another 0.5–0.75 quarts. Two gallons of 50/50 premixed coolant covers it with a little left over.

Running a pre-flush product (BlueDevil, Prestone flush, or similar) is worth doing if the coolant hasn't been changed in more than 4 years or is visibly rusty. Add the flush product, run the engine for 10–15 minutes, drain, flush with clean water, and then refill.

  • Coolant smell at the heater after a flush usually means air is still in the system. Run the engine with the heater on MAX until the hot air holds steady.
  • Rusty or brown coolant is a sign the inhibitor is long gone. A flush product won't fully remove scale — a shop radiator flush machine does a better job on badly neglected systems.
  • If the engine is slow to warm up or runs cooler than normal after a flush, suspect the thermostat. A stuck-open thermostat often hides until a flush makes it obvious. Replace it while the system is open — a Stant 45352 (195°F) is about $12.
  • Coolant disposal: old coolant is toxic to animals. Don't pour it down a storm drain. Most auto parts stores accept used coolant.
  • 50/50 premixed coolant (2 gallons): $22–$28 at any auto parts store
  • Concentrate + distilled water (mix your own): roughly the same cost, more control over ratio
  • Radiator flush product: $7–$10, optional but worthwhile on older systems
  • New lower hose clamps if the old ones are rusty: $3–$5 each

Steps

  1. Let the engine cool completely. Don't open the cap on a hot system.
  2. Place drain pan under the passenger-side lower radiator hose (connects to the lower radiator inlet).
  3. Loosen the hose clamp with pliers or a clamp tool. Twist the hose to break the seal and pull it off the radiator neck. Let coolant drain.
  4. Reconnect the hose once draining slows. Tighten the clamp.
  5. (Optional flush): Add radiator flush product and top off with water. Start engine, let reach operating temperature, idle 10–15 minutes. Drain again.
  6. Flush with plain water: fill with water, idle 5 minutes, drain. Repeat until the drain runs clear.
  7. Reconnect the lower hose and tighten the clamp.
  8. Remove the radiator cap. Fill slowly with 50/50 premixed coolant (or a 50/50 mix of concentrate and distilled water). Fill to the base of the filler neck.
  9. Fill the overflow recovery bottle to the MIN mark.
  10. Start the engine and let it come to operating temperature with the heater set to MAX HEAT. This bleeds air from the system — the heater core is the highest point in the circuit.
  11. Watch the coolant level drop as air purges. Top off as needed.
  12. Install the radiator cap. Check for leaks at the lower hose.
  13. After the first drive, recheck the overflow bottle level and top to MIN/MAX as needed.

Tools required

  • drain pan (2+ gallon)
  • funnel
  • garden hose
  • pliers or hose clamp tool
  • rags
