Jeep Wrangler TJ / Axles

Chromoly Shafts and C-Clip Eliminators (TJ)

Difficulty 3/52–6 hrs$250–8001997-2002, 2003-2006
Short answer

Shafts are where a TJ axle actually breaks. The front Dana 30's stock u-joints and the rear Dana 35's c-clip shafts are the failure points once you add tire and traction. Chromoly shafts and a rear c-clip eliminator are the targeted, affordable fix that keeps a stock-housing axle alive on 33s — cheaper than an axle swap, and they address the dangerous failure mode.

The two weak shafts fail differently. The front Dana 30 snaps a u-joint under steering load with big tires; the rear Dana 35's c-clip shaft snaps and, because the c-clip is the only thing retaining it, the wheel can leave the vehicle. Both are upgradeable without replacing the housing, in a driveway, with the tools most owners already have plus a slide hammer.

What you are actually buying

Factory TJ shafts are induction-hardened 1040-series steel. Aftermarket "chromoly" shafts are 4340 alloy, through-hardened, and in most kits carry a larger-radius spline runout and a smoother transition at the c-clip groove — that geometry matters as much as the alloy, because the groove is where the rear shaft cracks. Expect a 25–35 percent strength gain over stock, which is the difference between a shaft that survives a locked 33-inch tire hooking a ledge and one that does not. The front Dana 30's inner and outer shafts are 27-spline; the outer joins the inner with a 1310-series u-joint, Spicer 5-760X, and the joint — not the shaft — is the piece that lets go first. The rear Dana 35 shafts are 27-spline and about 1.18 inches in diameter; nothing about a chromoly shaft changes the 7.56-inch ring gear or the thin tubes, so this is a 33-inch fix, not a 35-inch one. Read the Dana 35 guide before you buy if 35s are in your future.

Front: chromoly + better joints

Chromoly front shafts with quality Spicer u-joints (or RCV CV joints for the serious crawler) take far more abuse than the stock parts and resist the snap-under-steering failure. This is the cheapest meaningful front-axle reliability gain on a TJ. Yukon's 4340 Dana 30 TJ kit (inner and outer shafts both sides, with 5-760X joints installed) runs $500–650; Ten Factory's equivalent is $420–520; G2 and Alloy USA land in the same range. Use the non-greaseable Spicer 5-760X when you replace joints on their own ($15–20 each) — the solid cross is stronger than the greaseable version, whose grease passage is a stress riser. For a locked front on 35s, the RCV Ultimate CV set ($1,200–1,400 a pair) replaces the u-joint with a constant-velocity joint that does not bind at full lock and carries a lifetime warranty; it is overkill on 33s with an open front. The Dana 30 guide covers ball joints and the unit bearings you will inspect while the shafts are out.

Rear: c-clip eliminator is the safety part

A rear c-clip eliminator kit converts the Dana 35 to a bearing-and-retainer setup so a broken shaft stays in the housing instead of sending the wheel down the trail. The kit supplies shafts with a pressed-on bearing and a retainer plate that bolts to the brake backing-plate studs at the tube end; the factory tube bearing and seal come out, and the c-clips are gone for good. Yukon, Superior, and Ten Factory sell chromoly 27-spline rear shafts with the eliminator as one package, $400–550. Paired with chromoly rear shafts, it makes the Dana 35 trustworthy on 33s with a locker — though if you are headed to 35s, see the axle swap guide, because the money is better spent once. The Super 35 alternative (30-spline shafts, requires a 30-spline carrier and locker) is the same idea one size up at $900–1,300, and is what to buy if the locker is going in at the same time anyway.

The procedure, front

Chock the rear, lift the front, and pull the wheels. Remove the caliper (two 13mm bolts) and hang it from the coil, then the rotor. The unit bearing comes off with three 13mm 12-point bolts from behind the knuckle; they are tight and often seized, so a long ratchet and penetrating oil the night before help. Pull the hub and the outer shaft comes with it — inspect the hub bearing for play and the seal for weeping while it is in your hand. The inner shaft slides straight out of the tube; a light tug on the shaft or a slide hammer on the outer stub frees it. Grease the new inner splines, slide the new assembly in until the splines seat in the side gear, reinstall the unit bearing, and torque the three bolts to 75 ft-lbs. Check the axle tube seal on the way in — a torn seal lets diff oil wander down the tube. Top off the front with 1.2 quarts of 80W-90. Both sides take 2–3 hours the first time.

The procedure, rear

Lift the rear, pull the wheels and drums, and drain the diff by pulling the cover (ten bolts, catch pan ready). Remove the 8mm pinion-shaft lock bolt, slide the cross shaft out, push each axle shaft inward, and fish out the c-clips. Pull the shafts. For the eliminator, drive the old bearing and seal out of the tube end with a slide-hammer bearing puller, clean the bore, and install the new shafts with their pressed bearings, bolting the retainer plates to the backing-plate studs at 35 ft-lbs. Reinstall the cross shaft and torque the lock bolt to 8–10 ft-lbs with a drop of thread locker — that bolt backing out is a known Dana 35 failure on its own. Clean the cover and housing flange, run a bead of RTV, and refill with 1.6 quarts of 80W-90 (75W-140 plus friction modifier if there is a Trac-Lok in it). Reset the rear drum brakes while the drums are off. Plan 3–4 hours.

When to do it

If you run 33s and keep the factory axles, do the shafts before the first hard trip — they address the failure that strands (or endangers) you. If you are already planning a Ford 8.8 or Dana 44 swap, skip the rear shafts and put the money there instead. The rule of thumb: shafts and a c-clip eliminator are the right call when you intend to keep the factory housings; if a 35-inch build (and therefore a housing swap) is already on the horizon, spend the axle budget once on the swap rather than twice. Front chromoly shafts, on the other hand, transfer to any Dana 30 you keep, so they are never wasted money.

Costs and sources

Front: Ten Factory or Yukon chromoly Dana 30 kit with joints, $420–650; Spicer 5-760X joints alone, $15–20 each; RCV Ultimate CV set, $1,200–1,400. Rear: chromoly 27-spline shafts with c-clip eliminator, $400–550 (Yukon, Superior, Ten Factory); Super 35 kit, $900–1,300 with a 30-spline locker. Consumables: 80W-90 gear oil for both ends, RTV, a tube of moly grease, and a slide-hammer bearing puller ($40–60 or a loaner from the parts store). Every one of these is sold direct or through the usual Jeep parts houses; buy the joints from a driveline shop rather than the cheapest listing, because counterfeit Spicer joints are common enough to matter.

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • axle shaft puller
  • torque wrench
  • snap-ring pliers
