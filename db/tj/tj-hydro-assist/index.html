Hydraulic Assist Steering: Is It Worth It on a TJ?

Difficulty 4/56–16 hrs$1100–24001997-2002, 2003-2006

Hydraulic assist is a heavy-tire fix, not a general upgrade. On a TJ running 37s — or 35s with a stiff box and sticky tires — hydro-assist takes the load off the steering box and gives you authority in the rocks. On 33s and smaller it is unnecessary, and bolting it on to cure wandering only hides a worn front end.

Hydro-assist adds a hydraulic ram to the steering, fed by a ported box or an extra pump, so the system pushes the tires instead of asking the small TJ box to do all the work. The benefit shows up turning big tires against a rock at crawl speed, where the stock box overheats and the sector shaft flexes.

37-inch tires and up, hard rock use, or a box that already strains. The TJ box is on the small side, so heavy builds load it hard. Pair the ram with a steering box brace — the added hydraulic force will crack an unbraced sector shaft — and a cooler, because the extra work makes heat.

On 33s a healthy factory box with a brace and tight linkage steers fine. If your complaint is wander or wobble, that is a worn track bar, tie-rod, or ball joint — fix the front end first. Hydro-assist layered over worn parts only masks the problem until something lets go.

This system is your steering. Use a proper kit, brace the box, route and secure the lines away from heat and pinch points, and bleed it completely before trusting it. If you are not confident plumbing hydraulics, have a shop do it — a leak or a failed line here is a steering failure, full stop. Budget for the whole system, too — a ported box or second pump, the ram and mounts, a brace, a cooler, and quality hose all add up, and a half-finished install is more dangerous than the stock setup you started with.

  • flaring tools
  • torque wrench
