Jeep Wrangler TJ / Engine

TJ 4.0L Oil Change — Specs, Interval, and What to Use

Difficulty 1/50.5–1 hrs$25–551997-2002, 2003-2006Jump to steps
Short answer

The TJ 4.0L takes 6 quarts of 10W-30 (factory spec) or 5W-30 for colder climates. Change it every 3,000–5,000 miles with conventional oil or every 5,000–7,500 miles with full synthetic.

The AMC-derived 4.0L inline-six has been running since 1987 and it rewards consistent oil changes. The factory spec is 10W-30 conventional, but Jeep engineers used 10W-30 when most of their production went south of the Mason-Dixon line. For cold weather or extended intervals, 5W-30 full synthetic is a legitimate upgrade — it flows faster on cold starts, which is where most wear happens.

Capacity is 6 quarts with a filter change. The drain plug is on the passenger side of the oil pan, recessed toward the rear. The filter is on the driver's side of the block near the front — straightforward to reach. A filter wrench makes the initial break loose more manageable but hand-tight is the correct install torque on most spin-on filters.

High-mileage oil (100,000+ miles) makes sense if the engine is running dry between changes or if you're seeing minor seepage at the valve cover. The seal conditioners in high-mileage blends reduce seepage noticeably. Don't switch to high-mileage expecting it to fix a real leak — it won't.

What to watch for

  • 25 ft-lb on the drain plug is firm but not aggressive. The TJ oil pan threads strip if you gorilla it. If you feel it getting tight faster than expected, stop and check alignment.
  • A dry filter gasket can cause a slow oil leak that doesn't show up until the first heat cycle. Always oil the gasket.
  • Brown sludge on the dipstick or inside the fill cap means the oil is emulsified — usually a head gasket leak introducing coolant, or oil going too long between changes. Address the source before changing the oil.
  • New filter spin-on gaskets should seat against clean metal. Old gasket residue left on the block causes a double-gasket situation and guaranteed leak.

Costs and sources

  • Wix 51515 filter: $7–$9 at NAPA or Amazon
  • Conventional 10W-30 (6 quarts): $18–$24
  • Full synthetic 5W-30 (6 quarts): $30–$40
  • Drain plug (replace if threads look rough): $5–$8 at any parts store

Steps

  1. Warm the engine for 3–5 minutes, then shut off. Warm oil drains faster and carries more suspended contaminants.
  2. Set the Jeep on level ground. Raise and support on jack stands if needed to access the drain plug.
  3. Place drain pan under the passenger-side drain plug.
  4. Remove the drain plug (14mm). Let fully drain — 3–5 minutes.
  5. While draining: locate the oil filter on the driver's side of the block. Position drain pan under it.
  6. Remove the oil filter with a wrench or by hand. Some oil will spill — the pan should catch it.
  7. Wipe the drain plug threads and inspect. The magnetic drain plug (if equipped) will have gray metallic paste — that's normal wear debris. Wipe clean.
  8. Reinstall drain plug and torque to 25 ft-lb. Don't over-torque — that's a frequent cause of stripped oil pans on TJs.
  9. Lightly coat the new filter's gasket with fresh oil. Thread on hand-tight, then 3/4 turn by hand. No wrench at install.
  10. Add 6 quarts of oil through the valve cover fill cap. Check dipstick after adding 5.5 quarts — different filter brands hold slightly different amounts.
  11. Start engine, let run 30–60 seconds. Check for leaks at the drain plug and filter.
  12. Shut off, wait 2–3 minutes, check oil level on dipstick. Correct to full.
  13. Reset oil change interval if using an oil life monitor.

Tools required

  • 14mm box wrench or socket
  • oil filter wrench (optional)
  • drain pan
  • funnel
  • rags
  • torque wrench (optional but recommended)
