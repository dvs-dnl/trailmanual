TJ Steel Rear Bumper — Tire Carrier, Recovery Points, and Hitch

Difficulty 2/51–3 hrs$220–9501997-2006

The TJ's factory rear bumper is a plastic fascia over a light bar — no recovery rigging points, no usable tire-carrier capacity, and an overhang that costs you departure angle. A steel bumper with integrated D-ring mounts is the floor. A swing-out carrier is what makes a 33-inch or larger spare survivable, because the factory tailgate hinges will not.

Rear armor on a TJ buys three separate things, and it helps to decide which you actually need before shopping. Recovery rigging points, so you can be pulled out backward without hooking a strap to a bumper bracket. Spare-tire capacity, so a full-size 33 or 35 is not hanging on sheet metal. And geometry, because the plastic bumper's lower edge is the first thing to drag coming off an obstacle. Different bumpers deliver different combinations of those three, and the expensive ones are expensive because they deliver all of them.

The stock TJ rear is a plastic cover on a light steel beam. It has no shackle mounts, so there is nothing rated to pull on. Its hitch — when present — is a light-duty receiver sized for a small trailer, not for recovery, and pulling from a hitch receiver with a ball still installed is one of the recognized ways people get killed in a recovery.

The tailgate carrier is the other half of the problem. The TJ's tailgate hinges and the sheet metal around them were sized for the factory spare, roughly a 28- to 30-inch tire. Owners get away with a 31 or a light 32. Past that, the hinge load fatigues the tailgate skin and the hinge mounting area, and the failure shows up as a sagging gate that no longer latches square — a repair that costs more than the carrier would have.

Bumper-only with D-rings and a hitch. Smittybilt's SRC rear (76711) at around $300 and the Rugged Ridge Spartan (11542.10) at around $350 are the volume sellers. Both bring two integrated D-ring mounts and a 2-inch receiver, both bolt to the factory frame holes, and both install in about 90 minutes. Verify the D-ring mounts before buying — the cheapest units in this tier sometimes omit them, which removes the main reason to do the swap.

Bumper with swing-out tire carrier. Iron Rock Offroad's carrier bumper at roughly $620 and Poison Spyder's Brawler with swing-out at roughly $900 add a dedicated hinge rated for a full-size tire, with its own spindle bearing rather than a bolt through a plate. Install runs 2–3 hours. This is the tier to buy if you run 33s or larger, and the extra $300 over a bumper-only setup is the difference between a spare you can carry and a tailgate repair.

Corner-and-rear combinations. Several makers sell rear bumpers with matching rear corner guards. If your trail use includes tight tree-lined lines or rock gardens, the corners protect the most commonly crushed sheet metal on a TJ, and buying the set at once usually costs less than adding corners later.

  • Replacement bumper and hardware
  • 15mm socket for the factory bumper bolts
  • Torque wrench
  • Thread locker (blue) for carrier hinge bolts
  • Floor jack or a second set of hands — a steel carrier bumper is 70–120 lb
  • Wire brush and paint for the frame-rail contact surfaces
  1. Remove the factory bumper — typically six bolts through the frame ends. Support the bumper before the last two come out.
  2. Remove the stock tailgate carrier if you are relocating the spare. Note that the tailgate will need a delete plate or a factory-style cover over the hinge holes.
  3. Disconnect trailer wiring at the factory connector if present, and free any wiring routed along the crossmember.
  4. Clean and inspect the frame ends. This is the best look you will ever get at the rear frame rails; a TJ with salt history can have rot right where the bumper bolts, and finding it now is better than finding it under load.
  5. Position the steel bumper and hand-thread every bolt before torquing any of them. Steel bumpers have little slot tolerance and fighting one bolt at a time wastes an hour.
  6. Torque frame mounting bolts to kit spec, typically 50–65 ft-lb, working from the center outward.
  7. Reconnect trailer wiring to the factory or integrated connector, and relocate the license plate and its light to the new mount.
  8. If fitting a swing-out carrier: mount the tire before driving, then swing through the full arc and check clearance at the tailgate, the rear quarter panel, and the corner of the hardtop or soft-top bow. Set the latch so the arm loads against its stop, not against the latch pin.

Carrier weight rating against real tire weight. A 35-inch tire on a beadlock wheel approaches 80 lb, and a 37 on a heavy wheel goes past it. Confirm the carrier's rating covers your combination, not the marketing tire size.

Hinge play from day one. A swing-out that has any perceptible rock at the spindle when new will hammer itself loose within a season of trail miles. Torque the spindle per spec and re-check it after the first few outings — this is the single most common carrier complaint and it is nearly always a maintenance item rather than a defect.

Departure angle. Full-width plate bumpers that tuck up behind the frame ends improve departure angle over the factory overhang; bumpers with a deep lower skirt or a receiver hanging below the tube can make it worse. If clearance is the reason you are buying, check the profile from the side before you order.

Fuel tank skid interaction. The TJ's tank skid sits immediately ahead of the rear bumper mounts, and some aftermarket bumpers and some aftermarket skids compete for the same frame holes. If you already run an aftermarket skid plate package, confirm compatibility before ordering.

Rigging discipline. D-ring mounts on the new bumper are rated; the receiver is not a recovery point unless you use a rated receiver shackle mount, and never with a ball installed. Pair this with the recovery points guide and rate your whole kit as a system rather than one part at a time.

Bumper-only with D-rings and hitch: $220–360. Bumper with swing-out carrier rated for 33–35s: $600–950. Add $40–80 for a tailgate hinge delete plate or cover, and $20–40 for relocating the license plate light. Shop installation runs $150–350 if you would rather not wrestle 100 lb of steel alone.

For most TJ owners running 33s or larger, the swing-out tier is the correct purchase the first time. For a rig that stays on 31s and a stock spare, the bumper-only tier gets you rated recovery points and a real hitch for a third of the money. Either way, plan the rear as a pair with the front bumper so the truck ends up with rated rigging at both ends rather than one.
