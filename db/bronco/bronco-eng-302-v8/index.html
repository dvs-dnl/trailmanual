Factory 302 V8 — Specs, Carb, and Tune

Difficulty 2/50 hrs$01969-1977

The 302 is the sweet spot for a stock-appearing, reliable Bronco. Leave the points alone or swap to Pertronix — it's the single best $90 you'll spend on the ignition.

Ford's 302 Windsor (5.0L) became available in the Bronco in 1969 and was the dominant engine through 1977. Early versions used a 2-bbl Autolite 2100; later trucks got the 2150. Output was approximately 170 hp gross (pre-1971 SAE gross rating), which translates to roughly 130–140 hp net by modern measurement.

The 302 is well-suited to trail driving at stock gearing with 31–33" tires. It starts to feel strained above 35" tires, especially with a 4.11 or taller rear gear.

**Common issues to address:**

  • Points ignition: Pertronix Ignitor I ($90) eliminates maintenance and improves timing stability. Direct bolt-in.
  • Autolite 2100: If it leaks or won't tune, a Holley 2-bbl rebuild kit ($30) fixes most issues. The 2100 flows enough for a stock 302.
  • Timing: Set initial timing to 10–12° BTDC with vacuum advance disconnected. Advance springs wear out — a Summit Racing adjustable vacuum advance can ($12) gives you tuning range.
  • Heat soak: The 302 in an early Bronco runs hot in traffic. Ensure the 7-blade steel clutch fan is intact. Aluminum fans are not adequate for low-speed crawling.

**Oil leaks:** The 302 uses a rope rear main seal. Replace it with a rubber conversion kit (Fel-Pro BS 40596) if you're doing a major service. Front oil pan gasket leaks are common; use RTV sealant on the corners.

Why it works

  • strong parts availability
  • torquey and trail-capable
  • large aftermarket base
  • drops into later Broncos with no modification

Trade-offs

  • points ignition is maintenance-intensive (fix it)
  • gross-rated HP is optimistic
  • rope rear main seal leaks with age
