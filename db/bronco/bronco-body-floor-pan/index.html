Floor Pan Rust Repair

Difficulty 4/520–60 hrs$600–35001966-1977

Floor pan rust is the #1 early Bronco structural issue. If you can see daylight through the floor, the repair is mandatory — it's not cosmetic, it's the foundation for the seat mounts, seatbelt anchors, and body rigidity.

The early Bronco's body-on-frame construction means floor pans are structural but removable. AMD and Scott's Hot Rods make reproduction pan sections. A complete floor replacement on a heavily rusted truck is a two-weekend job for an experienced welder.

**Inspection approach:**

Remove carpet and sound deadener. Poke every seam with an awl — soft spots indicate rust-through. Check specifically:

  • Seat bolt areas (water pools here under carpet)
  • Forward floor behind the toe board
  • Rear floor toward the tailgate

**Repair steps:**

1. Cut out the rusted section with a body saw or angle grinder. Cut back to solid metal — don't weld to anything that's compromised.

2. Treat all exposed metal with rust converter (Eastwood Rust Encapsulator or similar). Let it cure fully.

3. Fit the new pan section. Use weld-through primer on mating surfaces before closing seams.

4. Stitch-weld the new pan in — 1" welds every 3" is the standard. Do not seam-weld continuously (warps the panel).

5. Apply seam sealer to all interior seams. Apply POR-15 or cavity wax to the underside.

**Structural note:** Floor pan bolts anchor the seat tracks and seatbelt mounts. These are safety-critical. If a seat track anchor is rusted, the floor repair must address the surrounding metal first — a seat track welded to a rusty floor is not a repair.

Why it works

  • reproduction pans are widely available
  • structural fix that adds longevity
  • cheaper than a body shop

Trade-offs

  • requires welding (not a bolt-on job)
  • rust always extends further than expected — budget contingency
  • full interior removal required

Tools required

  • MIG welder
  • angle grinder
  • body saw or plasma cutter
  • body hammer and dolly set
  • wire brush
  • rust converter
  • weld-through primer
