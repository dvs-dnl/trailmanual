Dana 44 Front Axle — Stock Specs and Weak Points

Difficulty 1/50 hrs$01971-1977

The early Bronco's front axle is a closed-knuckle (kingpin) Dana 44 from mid-1971 through 1977 — strong enough for 35" tires and moderate trail use. Trucks from 1966 to mid-1971 carry the smaller Dana 30 instead. The Dana 44's weak point is the inner axleshaft U-joint and the kingpin/knuckle seals, all of which live or die on regular service. Keep them greased and inspected and the axle outlasts almost everything bolted to it.

Ford ran two front axles under the first-generation Bronco. From 1966 to mid-1971 the truck used the Dana 30; in mid-1971 Ford switched to the larger Dana 44, which carried through to the end of the run in 1977. Both are closed-knuckle, kingpin designs — the steering knuckle is a sealed ball housing, not the open ball-joint knuckle that appeared on later Ford trucks. The 1976–1977 Dana 44 also gained factory front disc brakes; everything earlier used drums.

Identifying which you have matters before you buy parts: the Dana 44 has a larger center casting, larger U-joints, and (76–77) disc brakes, while the Dana 30 is visibly smaller. If you are unsure, measure the ring gear or count the cover bolts against a known reference before ordering gears or shafts.

Dana 44 key specs:

  • Ring gear diameter: 8.5 in
  • Knuckle type: closed-knuckle, kingpin (sealed ball housing)
  • U-joint: Spicer 5-760X on the front axleshafts — run greaseable joints
  • Hubs: manual locking hubs (Warn or Spicer); worn factory hubs should be replaced
  • Brakes: drum through 1975, disc on 1976–1977
  • Differential: open from the factory; limited-slip and locking options available
  1. Inner axleshaft U-joints. The inner joint on each front shaft takes the worst of the abuse — steering load plus drive torque at an angle. A dry joint fails without warning and can take the shaft ears with it. Run greaseable Spicer 5-760X joints and service them every season; carry a spare on a long trip.
  2. Kingpins and knuckle seals. The closed-knuckle ball housing relies on its outer felt/rubber seal to keep grease in and water out. A torn seal lets differential oil and trail water mix into the knuckle, which then chews the kingpin bearings. This is its own job — the closed-knuckle Dana 44 front service guide walks the kingpin and seal rebuild in detail.
  3. Locking hubs. Original mid-1970s hubs crack or seize. Warn Premium replacements are the standard upgrade and re-engage reliably in cold and mud.
  4. Wheel bearings. Tapered roller bearings, pre-1980 Ford pattern. Re-pack every 2 years or 20,000 miles with quality Timken bearings — cheap import sets run loose and wear fast.

The Dana 44 front is comfortable under 35" tires and moderate rock and trail work. Past 35", or with an aggressive locker and a heavy right foot, the limiting parts become the U-joints and the open differential rather than the housing itself. Stock gear ratios were 3.50, 3.73, or 4.09 depending on year and engine — and front and rear ratios must match exactly. Once you move past 33" tires, plan to regear; the tire-size-to-ratio logic is laid out in the gear ratio selection guide. For traction, the front axle is the higher-value end to lock on a closed-knuckle truck; the tradeoffs between selectable, automatic, and limited-slip units are covered in the locker options guide.

Inspect the knuckle ball seals at every oil change — a weeping seal is cheap to fix and expensive to ignore. Pull and grease the U-joints at the start of each wheeling season. Re-pack wheel bearings on the 2-year/20k schedule. Pop the locking hubs apart annually to clean and re-grease the internals; grit and old grease are what seize them. None of this is expensive, and it is the difference between a Dana 44 that runs for decades and one that strands you on the trail.

Why it works

  • closed-knuckle Dana 44 is strong enough for 35" tires
  • forged housing with a large aftermarket, including lockers and gears
  • greaseable U-joints available and inexpensive
  • 76–77 trucks came with factory front discs

Trade-offs

  • open differential from the factory
  • inner U-joints and kingpin seals demand regular service or they fail
  • 1966–mid-1971 trucks have the smaller, weaker Dana 30 — confirm before buying parts
  • practical tire ceiling around 35" without housing and shaft upgrades

Tools required

  • grease gun
  • torque wrench
  • dial indicator
  • tape measure
