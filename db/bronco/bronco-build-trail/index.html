Trail Build Recipe — 35s, Dana 44 Lockers, 4" Lift

Difficulty 4/580–180 hrs$15000–350001966-1977

This is the standard capable trail build for an early Bronco — not a show truck, not a dedicated rock buggy, but a rig that handles moderate-to-difficult trails reliably and still drives home on the street. Plan on $15K–$35K depending on starting condition and how much you do yourself. The single biggest variable is rust: a solid truck builds at the low end, a rusty one adds $5K–$15K before the fun parts start.

The recipe below assumes a 1969–1977 Bronco with solid floors and a solid frame as the starting point. If the body or frame is rust-compromised, fix that first — building a trail truck on rotten floor pans or rockers is wasted money.

A clean 5.0/302 truck with good sheet metal is the ideal core. Verify the floors, rockers, and frame rails with an awl before you commit a dollar to mods. Rust-compromised structure adds $5K–$15K to this budget before the build even begins, and it's the number one reason these builds blow past their estimate.

  • 5.0L HO engine swap if you're starting from an I6 or a tired 302: ~$2,200 with an Edelbrock intake and carb, covered in the 5.0 HO / EFI swap guide
  • Shorty headers and 2.5" dual exhaust: ~$650
  • Pertronix ignition conversion: ~$90 and the best small dollar on the engine
  • Skyjacker 4" complete kit: ~$1,100 with springs, extended shocks, and hardware — see the 4" lift baseline guide for fitment and geometry notes
  • Extended stainless brake lines: ~$80, safety-critical, never skip them at 4"
  • ARB Air Lockers front and rear: ~$1,160 for both, plus ~$340 for the twin compressor and ~$200 for air line and mounting
  • Yukon 4.56 gear sets: ~$680 for both axles, plus $700–$900 for professional setup; match the ratio to your tire per the gear ratio guide
  • 35x12.50R15 BFG KO2: ~$1,200 mounted and balanced for five
  • 15x8 steel wheels, 4.5" backspacing: ~$500 for five
  • Transfer-case and engine skids: ~$500 purchased or fabricated
  • Tube rock sliders: ~$400–600 fabricated

The parts list above targets the middle of the market. A budget version swaps the ARB Air Lockers for a Detroit Locker or Truetrac (saves roughly $700-900 combined but loses selectable open-diff mode), uses a mid-tier lift like a Rough Country in place of the Skyjacker (saves $200-300), and holds off on the 5.0 HO swap in favor of tuning the existing 302 (saves $1,800-2,000 but keeps the carbureted engine's cold-start and altitude quirks). That combination lands closer to $10,000-12,000 on a solid core truck. A premium version adds a coilover conversion, full tube doors, and a fully built (not merely regeared) axle on both ends -- $45,000-plus without much effort and closer to a dedicated rock buggy than a trail-and-street truck.

Engine first, so you're not building around a tired powerplant. Then suspension, brake lines, and an alignment. Then the axle regear, and lockers in the same teardown to avoid pulling the diffs twice. Tires last — confirm clearance at full stuff before you buy five of them.

The budget above gets the truck built — it doesn't cover what it costs to keep it running. 35s wear faster than stock-size tires under trail use; budget a replacement set every 25,000-35,000 miles at roughly the $1,200 figure above. The ARB lockers and twin compressor are low-maintenance but not zero-maintenance — inspect the air line fittings and the compressor's intake filter yearly. Fuel economy on a 5.0 HO with 35s and 4.56s typically lands in the 10-13 mpg range, worth weighing before you decide this is a daily driver rather than a trail-and-back rig. Insurance on a heavily modified classic usually runs through an agreed-value classic policy rather than standard coverage — get a quote before you spend the build budget so the number doesn't surprise you afterward.

Why it works

  • genuinely capable on moderate-to-difficult trails
  • streetable enough for the drive to and from the trailhead
  • ARB lockers stay transparent until you switch them on
  • builds resale as a clean, sorted driver — not a science project

Trade-offs

  • starting condition swings the budget by tens of thousands
  • ARB locker installation requires professional axle setup
  • 35s on 4.56 with the stock Dana 20 is marginal — 4.88 is preferred for 35s

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • socket set
  • torque wrench
  • welder (for skids/sliders)
