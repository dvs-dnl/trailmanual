Ford Bronco / Suspension

2" Mild Lift — Leveling and Small Tire Clearance

Difficulty 2/54–8 hrs$250–6001966-1977
Short answer

A 2" lift on an early Bronco is about tire clearance and recovering a sagged stance, not articulation. The early Bronco rides on coil springs and radius arms up front and leaf springs over the Ford 9-inch at the rear, and a lift kit replaces the front coils, the rear leaf springs and the shocks. Done right it gives a little more tire room and rides better than worn-out factory springs. It does not transform the truck off-road — for that, see the 4" lift.

A 2" lift nudges the departure and approach angles and levels a truck whose 50-year-old springs have settled. Because the front is coil-sprung and the rear is leaf-sprung, the cleanest way to gain height and restore ride quality is new springs at both ends — lift coils up front and a rear leaf pack or add-a-leaf — rather than a body lift.

What's in a typical 2" kit

  • Front lift coil springs (Wild Horses sells early Bronco kits in 1.5", 2.5" and 3.5" heights — see their suspension page)
  • Rear add-a-leaf or a replacement rear leaf pack
  • Four extended shocks — stock-length shocks top out and self-destruct with any lift
  • Fresh rear shackles and bushings, and radius arm bushings if yours are tired
  • A plan for caster: the front axle is located by radius arms, and caster is corrected with offset C-bushings

The installation

Work one end at a time so the axle never hangs unsupported. With the frame on jack stands and the axle supported on a floor jack:

  1. Front: remove the shocks and disconnect anything that limits droop (track bar end, brake hose brackets, sway bar links if fitted). Lower the axle slowly until the coils are unloaded and lift them out; set the new coils in the factory cups. Watch the brake hoses the whole time.
  2. Rear: remove the shocks, then the U-bolts holding the axle to the spring. Loosen the spring eye bolt and the shackle bolt. Fifty-year-old hardware is frequently seized — soak it in penetrant ahead of time.
  3. Install the new rear pack or add-a-leaf, starting the fixed eye bolt first, then the shackle. Fit fresh shackles and bushings while it is apart.
  4. Re-seat the axle on the spring center pin, torque new U-bolts in a cross pattern to the figure in your spring or kit instructions, and install the new shocks.
  5. Roll it off the stands, let the suspension settle, then measure ride height and check that the track bar still centers the axle.

Alignment, caster, and shocks

Any front lift on a radius-arm axle changes caster, which shows up as lazy steering return and wander. On an early Bronco caster is corrected at the radius-arm-to-axle joint with offset C-bushings (Wild Horses sells a 7-degree C-bushing kit for 1966–79 Broncos), and a shop then sets caster and toe on the rack. If the truck already wanders before the lift, address the steering box and linkage first — the steering stabilizer and box brace guide covers what a stabilizer can and cannot fix. For shocks, buy extended lengths that match the measured travel at your new ride height.

Tire fit and what to watch for

Tire clearance depends more on whether your fenders are cut than on a 2" lift: Wild Horses puts the practical limit for an uncut truck around a 30x9.50R15 on a 2.5" lift, with 33x12.50R15 fitting cut trucks with flares on a 2.5"–3.5" lift. Confirm clearance on your own truck at full articulation before committing to a tire, and cross-check the tire sizing and wheel backspacing guides. Bigger tires also push you toward regearing if you are running tall factory gears.

Costs and sources

Front coils, a rear leaf pack or add-a-leaf, shackles and bushings run a few hundred dollars in parts; add a set of four shocks and C-bushings if caster needs correcting. An alignment at a shop that understands solid-axle Fords is not optional. Wild Horses' complete early Bronco lift kits with shocks are listed around $1,000.

Why it works

  • Restores a sagged stance and refreshes worn springs at both ends
  • Bolt-in work with hand tools
  • Better ride than tired factory springs

Trade-offs

  • Little or no articulation gain
  • Tire clearance still limited on uncut fenders
  • Caster and alignment work are part of the job, not extras

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • spring compressor
  • 3/4" socket set
  • torque wrench
  • pry bar
