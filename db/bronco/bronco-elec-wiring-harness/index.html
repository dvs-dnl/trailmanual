Ford Bronco / Electrical

Wiring Harness Replacement and Charging Upgrade

Difficulty 4/58–25 hrs$250–9001966-1977
Short answer

Fifty-year-old wiring is the root cause of more early Bronco gremlins than any other system — brittle insulation, corroded grounds, and an undersized fuse box that was marginal when new. If you're chasing intermittent electrical faults, a full harness replacement plus a modern alternator and fuse panel ends the whack-a-mole for good.

The original early Bronco harness uses cloth-and-vinyl insulation that's now brittle, grounds that have spent decades corroding, and a fuse box with too few circuits. The classic symptoms are dim or flickering lights, gauges that read wrong, an ammeter pegging, and faults that come and go with bumps. You can chase these one at a time for years, or replace the harness and be done.

The honest assessment: a full harness swap is a real project — plan a weekend or two, not an afternoon — and it demands patience and a wiring diagram. But it's within reach for a methodical DIYer, and a labeled reproduction harness makes it far more approachable than splicing into the original. If your truck's electrical issues are isolated to one circuit, fix that circuit; if they're everywhere, replace the harness.

What you need

A reproduction harness comes two ways: a faithful year-correct OEM-style harness (Bronco Graveyard, Wild Horses) that plugs into original-style components, or a modern labeled chassis harness (Painless, Centech) with a blade-fuse panel and relay provisions. The modern route adds circuits for electric fans, lights, and accessories and uses standard ATO fuses. Pair either with a 1-wire alternator conversion — the original generator or low-output alternator can't keep up with electric fans, modern lighting, and a winch.

The procedure

Photograph and label everything before you touch it. Disconnect the battery. Work one circuit at a time, removing the old wire as you run the new one so you never lose track of where it goes. Mount the new fuse panel somewhere dry and accessible (under the dash on the driver's side is typical). Run grounds to clean, bare metal and add a dedicated engine-to-frame and frame-to-body ground strap — bad grounds cause more "electrical" faults than bad hot wires. For the alternator conversion, run the proper-gauge charge wire to the battery or starter relay and delete or bypass the external voltage regulator per the alternator's instructions.

Crimp with a ratcheting crimper and use heat-shrink connectors, then loom and secure the harness away from heat and sharp edges. Solder-and-shrink any high-vibration or critical joints.

What to watch for

The original ammeter routes full system current through the dash — a known fire risk on old Fords. When you rewire, convert to a voltmeter (which taps a low-current signal) and route charging current directly. This is the most important safety change in the whole job.

Grounds are half the battle. Add engine-to-frame and frame-to-body straps and clean every ground point to bare metal. A "dead" component is often a bad ground, not a bad wire.

Don't run a high-output alternator's charge wire on the original undersized gauge — it'll overheat. Match wire gauge to alternator output (typically 6 AWG for 100A over a short run).

Disconnect the battery for the entire job, and fuse every new circuit. An unfused hot wire that chafes through to the frame on a 50-year-old truck is how Broncos burn.

Costs and sources

A reproduction OEM-style harness runs $250–450; a modern Painless/Centech chassis harness with fuse panel is $400–700. Add a 1-wire 100A alternator ($110–160), a voltmeter to replace the ammeter ($30), and loom/connectors ($40). A full electrical refresh lands at $400–900. Centech and Painless harnesses are the common upgrade path for EBs because they're labeled and add modern circuits; a faithful OEM reproduction is the choice for a numbers-matching restoration where originality matters more than extra circuits.

  • wire strippers
  • multimeter
  • soldering iron
  • heat gun
  • test light
  • socket set
