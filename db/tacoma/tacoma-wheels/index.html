Wheel Selection for the Tacoma — 17" Steel, Alloy, and Beadlock

Difficulty 1/51–2 hrs$356–12802005-2015, 2016-2023Jump to steps
Short answer

The Tacoma's 6x139.7 bolt pattern is shared with 4Runner, Tundra, FJ Cruiser, and Land Cruiser — wheel selection is wide open, but backspacing is the critical dimension for lifted IFS Tacomas, and getting it wrong causes CV binding and premature bearing wear.

Backspacing and offset are the two dimensions that determine where the wheel sits relative to the hub. For a lifted Tacoma with UCAs, the target backspacing is 4.75"–5" (approximately 0mm to +5mm offset in the 17x9 format). Too much backspacing pushes the wheel inward, where it contacts the inner fender and upper control arm at full compression. Too little backspacing pushes the wheel outward, which widens the track but can create tire-to-fender contact and increases stress on the wheel bearing.

Pro Comp steel wheels are the budget choice — at $89 each they're the most affordable way to mount a set of 33s, they're nearly indestructible, and they're straightforward to true or replace in the field. The tradeoff is weight: a 17x9 steel wheel runs about 28–32 lbs vs 18–22 lbs for a comparable alloy. On a Tacoma running trail, the weight isn't a performance issue, but it does increase rotating unsprung mass which dulls suspension response slightly.

The Method MR701 is a popular alloy choice for lifted Tacomas: the 17x8.5 is a 0mm offset with 4.75" of backspacing on the 6x139.7 pattern, right in the range above. The 17x8.5 size pairs optimally with 285/75R16 (33") tires and the IFS geometry of the Tacoma. Fuel and Black Rhino offer comparable designs at lower prices, though with slightly more variable finish quality and a wider spoke pattern that can pack mud in deep clay.

Beadlock wheels mechanically clamp the tire bead to the wheel rim, preventing bead separation at very low tire pressures (under 10 PSI). They're the correct choice for rock crawling where you air down to 8–12 PSI. For trail and overland use at 18–25 PSI, standard wheels with a quality tire are sufficient. Most true beadlocks are sold for off-road use only — the KMC Machete Crawl, for example, is listed as off-road only and not DOT compliant — so read the listing for a road-use rating before you drive one on public roads, or choose a bead-grip wheel for a daily driver.

Steps

  1. Confirm current and planned lift height — this determines the correct backspacing range (4.75"–5" for 2.5"+ lift)
  2. Verify new wheel fits Tacoma hub bore — 106.1mm is the Tacoma hub bore; most aftermarket wheels use 108mm with hub-centric rings included
  3. Mount tires to new wheels at a tire shop; balance at this stage
  4. Transfer TPMS sensors from old wheels to new — requires TPMS sensor removal tool and tire machine; confirm sensor compatibility
  5. Install wheels on vehicle and torque the lug nuts in a star pattern. Toyota's owner's manual lists 83 ft-lb (113 N·m); with aftermarket wheels and lug nuts, use the wheel maker's figure
  6. Check clearance at full compression and full lock before trail use
  7. Recheck lug nut torque at 50 miles after install

Why it works

  • 6x139.7 bolt pattern gives access to the widest wheel selection in the off-road market
  • Method MR701 17x8.5 lands in the backspacing range lifted Tacomas want
  • Steel wheels are nearly indestructible and cheap enough to keep a spare in the bed
  • Beadlock wheels open up extremely low tire pressures for rock crawling
  • Aftermarket wheels typically reduce weight vs OEM steel factory wheels (on 3rd gen Tacoma)

Trade-offs

  • Incorrect backspacing on IFS Tacomas causes CV and bearing contact issues — verify before ordering
  • TPMS sensors may not transfer to all aftermarket wheels without an adapter kit
  • Beadlock ring bolts need regular checks at the torque the wheel maker prints, which varies by model (one Raceline beadlock calls for 17 ft-lb)
  • Low-offset wheels (-18mm and lower) push the tire outside the fender — requires flares on lifted builds
  • Pro Comp steel wheels are heavy — rotating mass affects fuel economy and very slightly dulls suspension feel

Tools required

  • torque wrench
  • breaker bar or impact
  • floor jack
  • jack stands
