35s on the Tacoma: Lift, Cab Mount, Cutting, and Gears for 2nd and 3rd Gen

Difficulty 4/58–24 hrs$3400–65002005-2015, 2016-2023
Short answer

A 35 fits a 2nd or 3rd gen Tacoma at about 3 inches of lift only if you are willing to chop the cab mount, cut fenders and hammer the firewall. More lift does not remove the cutting. If that sounds like too much, one owner's advice is to stop at a 285/75R17, about 34 inches, because the last inch is not worth the work. Either way, budget for gears on both axles.

Scope: 2005 to 2023 trucks. The 2024 and newer Tacoma is a different platform, and the sources here do not cover it.

Sources disagree, because lift height and cutting trade against each other.

ClaimSource

3 inches max, plus cutting3rd gen owner, TacomaWorld
2.5 inches plus heavy trimming3rd gen owner, TacomaWorld
3.5 inches or more3rd gen owner guessing from rubbing on 33s at 2 inches

6-inch drop bracket with little fender or cab mount work2nd gen owner, TacomaWorld

The 6-inch answer is not a clean escape. A 2nd gen owner on a 6-inch BDS lift with 35x12.50 Toyos still rubs. Others in that thread note that a drop bracket kit means cutting the front crossmember and is expensive. One 3rd gen owner makes the useful point: the bump stops decide how far the tire travels into the body, so ride height alone does not create clearance.

A 3rd gen owner with 2.5 inches shows what the low-lift route costs: about 2 inches trimmed from the flare and fender, a big cab mount chop, and wheel-well reshaping, and still scrubs at full bump. A 2017 owner adds that 35s are not going in on a 3-inch lift without serious work, and that even 33s can raise cab mount and flare problems.

2nd gen (2005 to 2015). Tacoma World owners list correct backspacing, a cab mount chop, firewall hammering, and fender cutting as the 35-inch requirement list. One says even 285s take a cab mount chop most of the time, and that wheel changes or spacers may be needed to clear upper control arms. Limiting up-travel is the alternative to some of the body work.

3rd gen (2016 to 2023). ThreePiece calls 3 inches the bare minimum and lists body mount chop plates, which require cutting and welding, plus about an inch off the inside of the fenders. Its example truck runs a 6-inch lift to reduce trimming. All Pro's weld-in kit moves the mount behind the firewall and says it clears the body mount for tires up to 35 inches, while warning that other trimming will still be needed.

The front cab mount sits right behind the tire, so it is where 35s stop. A chop means cutting and welding on the frame, per TacomaWorld owners. All Pro lists its $99 relocation for 2016 to 2020 trucks, 1/4-inch steel, and says welding should be done by an experienced welder. This holds the cab to the frame, so if you cannot weld structural work, hire it out.

Offroadium lists two 35-inch sizes, 35x12.50R17 and 315/75R16, both marked as needing modifications. By the arithmetic, a 315/70R17 is about 34.4 inches and a 285/75R17 about 33.8 inches.

Tacoma wheels are 6x139.7mm with a 106.1mm center bore. ThreePiece lists factory 3rd gen offsets of +23 on the SR5 16-inch, +13 on the Off-Road, +10 on the Pro, and +30 on 17 and 18-inch Sport and Limited wheels. For 35s, its other guide gives 17x8.5 or 17x9 at -12 to +25, with 17x9 -12 as its worked example, and its 3rd gen example is an 18x9 at 0. One owner on 285/75R16s and a 3-inch lift ran 4.5 inches of backspacing and pulled the front mud flaps.

No source opened for this guide gives a tested backspacing number for a 35 on a 2nd gen, beyond saying it has to be right. Read the wheel guide and 33-inch guide for how the rub points move as the wheel moves out. ThreePiece recommends upper control arms above 2.5 inches of lift. See the UCA guide.

ThreePiece calls regearing mandatory at 35s and names 4.88 or 5.29. Factory ratios are reported inconsistently, including 3.90 from a vendor and 4.30 on a 2019 V6 manual, so read your door or build information before ordering.

Tacoma3G vendors favor 5.29 for 35s. One vendor there runs 35s on 4.88 and says 5.29 would have been the pick had it been available. These are companies selling gears, so weigh that. A 2nd gen owner with a 6-inch lift lists 4.88s on 35s. Choose 5.29 for a loaded truck and 4.88 if it stays light and sees mostly highway. See the regear guide.

ExtremeTerrain's chart at 65 mph in a 1:1 gear puts a 35 at about 2,440 rpm on 3.91, 2,664 on 4.27, 3,045 on 4.88. 5.29 is not on the chart; the same formula gives about 3,300. Overdrive runs lower than that.

Four to five 35s at $417 to $432 each come to about $1,670 to $2,160 at Quadratec prices when written. Add about $100 to $300 in mount parts, from the kit above to pricier ones, plus welding labor you will need to quote locally. Add $1,600 to $4,000 for gears, the range on the regear guide. That is the $3,400 to $6,500 above, before the lift and wheels.

Steps

  1. Choose between a 285/75R17 and a true 35, and between trimming and a drop bracket kit.
  2. Install the lift, set bump stops, and get an alignment.
  3. Mount one wheel and tire. Compress each corner safely and turn lock to lock, marking contact with chalk.
  4. Remove mud flaps and trim liners and flare edges first.
  5. Chop or relocate the cab mount, or send the truck to a shop. Treat bare metal against rust.
  6. Trim fenders in small steps and hammer the firewall or pinch welds only where marked.
  7. Recheck at full lock and full compression with normal load in the bed.
  8. Tighten the lugs to your owner's manual or wheel maker's number. Recheck after the first drive and inspect the mount hardware afterward.
  9. Correct the speedometer, regear both axles, and sort out the spare.

Why it works

  • A 35 is documented on both generations.
  • A $99 mount relocation is available for 2016 to 2020 trucks, if you can weld or hire it.
  • A 285/75R17 gets you most of the way with less cutting.

Trade-offs

  • Fender and mount cuts cannot be undone.
  • Even at 6 inches, an owner still reports rubbing.
  • Gears are required, and they add thousands.
  • Most of this data is from owners, not testing, and it conflicts.

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • torque wrench
  • cut-off wheel
  • welder or a welding shop
  • body hammer
  • chalk or paint marker
