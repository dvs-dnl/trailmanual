Rear Locker for the Tacoma — ARB and Eaton Options

Difficulty 4/53–6 hrs$1250–15722005-2015, 2016-2023
Short answer

A rear locker transforms the Tacoma's on-trail capability on loose terrain, rocks, and mud. ARB and Eaton are the two selectable choices, and the right part depends on which rear axle your truck has. Check first that you need one: TRD Off-Road and TRD Pro trucks already have a factory rear locker.

Toyota lists an electronically controlled locking rear differential as standard on the 2023 TRD Off-Road, Trail Special Edition and TRD Pro. The Trail Edition is the one to read by year: the 2022 package includes the locker, and the 2021 package list doesn't. On 2nd gen trucks, the locker came in the TRD Off-Road package and was also a stand-alone option on 4x4 and PreRunner models, so look for the rear differential lock switch on the dash whatever the trim. An SR, SR5, TRD Sport or Limited without that switch is who this guide is for. Those trucks have Auto LSD, a brake-actuated system that is not a locker.

The rear axle changes by generation and transmission, so identify yours before you order. ARB's Tacoma fitment splits it this way: a 2005–2015 truck without a factory locker has the 8.4" rear (RD129). A 2016+ truck has the 8" rear if it's an automatic without a factory locker (RD132), and the 8.75" rear if it's a manual or has the factory locker (RD232). Eaton's 14211-1 covers the 2016–2023 automatic 8", and its 14235-1 covers the 8.75". Sources disagree on spline counts, so check the axle code on your axle tag and confirm against the locker maker's fitment chart before ordering.

The ARB Air Locker is the gold standard for air-actuated lockers. When the locker is disengaged, the rear diff behaves like a standard open differential, with no on-road handling compromise. When you flip the switch and air pressure engages the locker, both rear wheels are locked together regardless of traction. There's no ratcheting or clicking when disengaged. The tradeoff is the air system: you need a compressor, air lines routed to the diff, and a solenoid. Most ARB locker installs add a dedicated on-board air compressor (ARB twin or single compressor, $400–$700).

The Eaton ELocker is electrically actuated, so no air system is required. It behaves like an open diff when off and locks fully when activated, and for most trail use the behavior is close to the ARB in practice. Expect about $1,570 for the 8.75" unit.

Steps

  1. Verify rear axle ring gear size and transmission from the axle tag and the fitment chart
  2. Order the locker that matches your axle, year and transmission
  3. Drain rear differential gear oil
  4. Remove rear axle shafts (8 nuts each side on the backing plate flange)
  5. Remove differential cover; clean gasket surfaces
  6. Remove carrier bearing caps (mark left/right orientation before removal)
  7. Remove carrier assembly from housing — note shim positions
  8. Press out OEM side gears, spider gears, and cross-shaft; install locker internals per manufacturer instructions
  9. For ARB: install air line fitting in differential cover; route air line to bulkhead through axle housing
  10. Reinstall carrier into housing with original shims; set backlash per spec (0.005"–0.008" typical Toyota rear)
  11. Verify bearing preload with dial indicator
  12. Install new differential cover gasket or RTV; torque cover bolts in star pattern
  13. Reinstall axle shafts; torque backing plate nuts to spec
  14. Refill with GL-5 gear oil; add limited slip additive if manufacturer specifies
  15. For ARB: route air line, connect to compressor, test engagement before trail use
  16. For ELocker: route and connect wiring harness; test engagement from cab

Why it works

  • ARB Air Locker delivers full locker engagement on demand with zero on-road side effects when disengaged
  • ELocker eliminates the need for an air compressor while still providing on/off-road control
  • Dramatically improves traction on loose rock, mud, and off-camber terrain
  • Tacoma rear axles are robust and handle locker loads well
  • Adds real capability that no suspension upgrade can replicate in low-traction situations

Trade-offs

  • ARB Air Locker requires an air compressor, solenoid, and air line routing — adds $400–$700 to total cost
  • Differential disassembly requires proper tools and bearing press — not a beginner install
  • Setting backlash and bearing preload incorrectly causes premature ring-and-pinion wear
  • Three different rear axles across 2005–2023 — don't assume one part number fits

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • socket set
  • torque wrench
  • dial indicator
  • gear oil
  • RTV sealant
  • bearing press or access to press
