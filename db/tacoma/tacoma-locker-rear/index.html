Rear Locker for the Tacoma — ARB, Auburn, and Spartan Options

Difficulty 4/53–6 hrs$229–9992005-2015, 2016-2023

A rear locker transforms the Tacoma's on-trail capability on loose terrain, rocks, and mud — the ARB Air Locker is the highest-performing option, but the Auburn ECTED or Eaton ELocker deliver most of that capability at significantly lower cost.

The Toyota Tacoma uses an 8.4" ring gear rear differential (sometimes noted as the Toyota 8" or R180 in other Toyota documentation). The 2005–2013 2nd gen Tacoma and 2016–2023 3rd gen share the same basic rear axle housing, but the 3rd gen (specifically the 2014+ carrier — which actually carries through to the 3rd gen) uses a slightly revised carrier design. Confirm your model year and axle code (on the axle tag at the differential cover) against the locker manufacturer's fitment chart before ordering.

The ARB Air Locker is the gold standard for air-actuated lockers. When the locker is disengaged, the rear diff behaves like a standard open differential — no on-road handling compromise. When you flip the switch and air pressure engages the locker, both rear wheels are locked together regardless of traction differential. The engagement is reliable, immediate, and there's no ratcheting or clicking behavior on-road when disengaged. The tradeoff is the air system: you need a compressor, air lines routed to the diff, and a solenoid. Most ARB locker installs add a dedicated on-board air compressor (ARB twin or single compressor, $400–$700).

The Auburn ECTED and Eaton ELocker are electrically actuated — the engagement mechanism is electromagnetic rather than pneumatic, so no air system is required. The ECTED engages fully when activated and behaves identically to an open diff when off. For most trail use the behavior is indistinguishable from the ARB in practice. The Spartan Locker is a budget automatic locker (always locked) — it works but creates understeer on paved corners and clicking noise on tight turns on pavement. Reserve the Spartan for a dedicated trail rig or a vehicle rarely driven on pavement.

Why it works

  • ARB Air Locker delivers full locker engagement on demand with zero on-road side effects when disengaged
  • ECTED and ELocker eliminate the need for an air compressor while still providing on/off-road control
  • Dramatically improves traction on loose rock, mud, and off-camber terrain
  • Tacoma's 8.4" rear axle is robust and handles locker loads well
  • Adds real capability that no suspension upgrade can replicate in low-traction situations

Trade-offs

  • ARB Air Locker requires an air compressor, solenoid, and air line routing — adds $400–$700 to total cost
  • Spartan automatic locker clicks on tight pavement turns and compromises on-road cornering — not for daily drivers
  • Differential disassembly requires proper tools and bearing press — not a beginner install
  • Setting backlash and bearing preload incorrectly causes premature ring-and-pinion wear
  • 3rd gen carrier revision requires confirming fitment by year — don't assume 2005–2023 is one part number

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • socket set
  • torque wrench
  • dial indicator
  • gear oil
  • RTV sealant
  • bearing press or access to press
