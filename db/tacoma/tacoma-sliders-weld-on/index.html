Toyota Tacoma / Armor & Skids

Weld-On Rock Sliders — Heavy-Duty Rocker Armor

Difficulty 3/54–8 hrs$600–14002005-2015, 2016-2023Jump to steps
Short answer

The bolt-on sliders in the companion guide handle most Tacoma trail use. This card is for the owner who actually puts the rocker on rock and uses the slider as a high-lift and step point, where a weld-on or weld-reinforced hybrid carries the truck's weight without working the body loose. More capable, more committing — and on a Tacoma, the welding brings rust-proofing obligations that this guide treats as part of the job, not an afterthought.

When the welded joint matters

A bolt-on slider through-bolts to the frame and shrugs off rubs and light steps. A weld-on adds welded frame gussets so the bar takes the full weight of the truck pivoted on a rock — the load that, over time, can flex a bolt-only mount. For a Tacoma that crawls ledges and gets jacked from the rocker, the welded mount is what keeps the geometry true for years.

The spec sheet that matters

Insist on DOM tubing for the main bar rather than welded HREW — 1.75 in x 0.120 wall is the Tacoma standard, with 0.095-0.120 wall legs. HREW's seam is where cheap sliders crack under a hard drop onto rock. Count the frame attachments: a slider that will carry a jacked truck wants a minimum of three legs per side landing on plates or gussets, not two stubs. Angle is the other spec: 0 degrees (flat) is the best step and the best protection tucked tight; 10-15 degrees of upward kick sheds ledges when sliding sideways off an obstacle. A kick-out at the rear adds a wide step and pivots the tail off rocks, but snags in tight trees and rock gardens — choose it by terrain, not looks. Expect 60-90 lb per side; quality weld-on sets from RCI, CBI, or Southern Style run $600-900 bare steel, plus $200-400 if a shop does the welding.

Protecting a rust-prone frame

This matters more on a Tacoma than almost any other truck: 2005-2010 frames were part of a corrosion campaign that replaced frames outright, and every generation's boxed sections rust from the inside. Welding burns the internal coating off the far side of the frame wall, where you cannot see it. After paint cures, flood the frame cavity behind every weld zone with cavity wax through the factory access holes, and put the frame rust inspection on your annual calendar — a welded slider on a rotten frame is armor bolted to nothing, and the cab-mount rust repair guide shows how that story ends. No welder on hand means a shop session or a strong bolt-on instead; a bad structural weld is worse than a good bolt.

Bottom line

Go weld-on or hybrid only if you load the body on rock; the bolt-on is enough for most. Demand 1.75 x 0.120 DOM on three legs per side, disconnect the battery, shield the fuel and brake lines, set gaps off the body, and treat cavity wax and paint as part of the install. Done that way, it is the slider that outlasts the truck.

Steps

  1. Strip the rocker area: pinch-weld trim and any factory running boards off, then wire-wheel the frame landing zones to bright bare metal — paint, wax, and undercoating in the weld zone mean porosity.
  2. Disconnect both battery terminals before striking an arc. Welding current finding a return path through the ECU is a four-figure mistake on any modern Toyota.
  3. Locate and shield the lines: the fuel and brake hard lines run along the inside of the driver-side frame rail. Unclip and pull them back from any weld zone, and keep a shield or wet rag between them and the arc.
  4. Mock up on a floor jack and stands. Set the gap to the body, not the frame — the cab moves on its mounts, so hold roughly a finger-width minimum to the pinch weld and verify the doors swing free.
  5. Check tire clearance at full lock and full compression if the slider has a forward leg near the wheel well, then tack every leg.
  6. Re-verify gaps and door swing after tacking — tacks pull. Then weld out in staggered, skipped passes to control heat instead of running one long bead per joint.
  7. Fire watch: 30 minutes after the last bead, with the extinguisher standing by. Frame cavities hold wax and road grime that smolder long after the arc stops.
  8. Prime and paint every weld and every scorched patch the same day; bare welds flash-rust overnight.

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • welder
  • grinder
  • drill
  • torque wrench
