Budget Lift Kit — Coilovers and Add-a-Leaf (≤$600)

Difficulty 3/54–6 hrs$300–6002005-2015, 2016-2023

A Bilstein 6112 front coilover set paired with a quality rear add-a-leaf is the most capable budget lift option for the Tacoma — 1.5"–2" of lift, meaningfully better damping, and the foundation to go further if your build evolves.

The Tacoma's IFS front end responds well to a quality coilover even at the budget tier. The Bilstein 6112 is a height-adjustable strut that replaces the OEM spring-shock assembly in one unit. At full compression it delivers around 1.5"–2" of lift over stock without compromising ride quality the way a cheap spacer lift will. The rear add-a-leaf packs that pair well with this setup — OME Dakar or SCS — add progressive spring rate and lift the rear 1"–1.5" to keep the truck level.

What this tier does not give you is additional wheel travel or corrected CV joint angles. At 2" of lift the front CV shafts are operating near their design limits. If you're planning anything above 2" — or running aggressive terrain where the suspension cycles fully — upper control arms become necessary to restore caster geometry and reduce stress on the ball joints. Skipping UCAs and stacking more lift on top of the 6112s is a path to premature ball joint and CV wear. At the ≤$600 price point, the right call is to stay at 1.5"–2" and plan UCAs as the next upgrade.

The 6112 is height-adjustable via a threaded collar, which lets you dial in the front height precisely. Set the rear first by measuring loaded ride height, then match the front to achieve a slight nose-up rake (1/4"–1/2" front lower than rear when loaded) for a planted trail feel. Torque all suspension fasteners with the vehicle at ride height — not in the air — to avoid binding the rubber bushings at full droop.

Steps

  1. Chock rear wheels, break front lug nuts loose before lifting
  2. Lift front of truck, support on jack stands at the frame rails
  3. Remove front wheel and tire
  4. Disconnect sway bar end link from strut
  5. Remove brake line bracket from strut housing
  6. Mark OEM strut-to-knuckle pinch bolt orientation before removing
  7. Remove upper strut mount nuts from engine bay
  8. Remove strut-to-knuckle pinch bolts and slide strut out
  9. Set 6112 coilover collar to desired lift height per Bilstein's height chart
  10. Install 6112 into knuckle, torque pinch bolts to spec (148 ft-lb for most years)
  11. Reinstall upper mount nuts and torque to spec
  12. Reconnect brake line bracket and sway bar end link
  13. Repeat opposite side
  14. Move to rear: lift rear of truck, support at frame
  15. Remove rear wheels
  16. Support rear axle under differential housing with a jack
  17. Disconnect rear sway bar end links
  18. Lower axle slightly to create leaf spring access
  19. Remove U-bolts and lower spring plate
  20. Insert add-a-leaf into the pack per manufacturer's stacking order
  21. Reassemble spring pack, install new U-bolts if provided, torque to spec
  22. Reinstall wheels, lower truck, bounce suspension to settle
  23. Torque all suspension fasteners at ride height
  24. Get an alignment — caster and camber will be affected

Why it works

  • Best bang for $300–$600 in the Tacoma suspension budget
  • Bilstein 6112 is significantly better than OEM damping for trail and highway
  • Add-a-leaf rear increases load capacity slightly, useful for overlanders carrying gear
  • Height-adjustable front allows precise leveling
  • No alignment camber issues at 1.5"–2" lift
  • Maintains full IFS warranty-adjacent geometry at this lift height

Trade-offs

  • Does not address CV angle or caster — UCAs needed for anything above 2"
  • Add-a-leaf stiffens the rear spring rate; ride quality on pavement is firmer
  • No additional wheel travel compared to stock — bump and droop are unchanged
  • Budget rear add-a-leaf packs vary in quality; SCS is fine for mild use, OME Dakar is the better long-term choice
  • Does not fit 285/75R16 (33") tires without trimming at this lift height — 32" tires are the safe maximum

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • torque wrench
  • socket set
  • spring compressor
  • pickle fork or separator
  • breaker bar
