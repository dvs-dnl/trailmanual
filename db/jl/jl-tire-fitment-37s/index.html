Running 37" Tires on the JL — Rubicon vs Non-Rubicon Fitment

Difficulty 2/51–2 hrs$1200–20002018-2024
Short answer

No JL fits 37s from the factory. Jeep's own line on the Rubicon is 35s with no lift for the road and a 2-inch lift for off-road use, so 37s need more than that on every trim. Quadratec's lift guide puts 37s at 4 inches of lift and up, with some 3 to 3.5 inch setups making it with extra modifications.

The Rubicon has the most room of any JL. It comes on 33" tires from the factory, and Jeep says it takes 35s at stock height for road driving. Even then, Driving Line's stock-height Rubicon on 35s pressed hard into the inner fenders once it articulated. A 37 is another inch taller and usually wider, so treat a lift as part of the job on a Rubicon too. The rub points to check are the front upper control arm at the coil bucket and the rear fender liner.

Non-Rubicon JL trim levels (Sport, Sport S, Sahara) are a different situation. The factory fender openings are narrower, the suspension geometry is lower, and the tires are 31"–33" from the factory. Fitting 37s without a lift results in rubbing at every steering and suspension extreme. Quadratec's guide is the baseline here too: 4 inches of lift and up, or 3 to 3.5 inches with extra modifications. On the lower lift, expect to pull the rear fender liners and deal with front upper control arm contact at full steering lock.

The tire choices matter for fitment as much as the size. A 37x12.5R17 is the most common spec, and all three recommended options here are in that range. Note that actual measured width varies by brand — the BFG KM3 at 37x12.5 measures close to nominal, the Nitto Trail Grappler runs slightly narrower, and the Toyo Open Country MT runs slightly wider depending on the wheel backspacing. On a 17x8.5 or 17x9 wheel with 4.5"–5" backspace, all three mount cleanly.

Regear is not optional at 37s. Jeep's 2020 spec table lists 3.45 rear gears for the Sport and Sahara (3.73 for the diesels and 4.10 for the gas Rubicon), and those were engineered around 31"–33" tires. Adding 37s effectively drops your RPM at highway speed and loads the drivetrain heavily off-road. The Sahara with 3.45 gears and 37s is genuinely painful to drive — low-end torque evaporates, the transmission hunts gears on grades, and trail crawl ratios are inadequate. Plan for 4.56 gears with 35s, 4.88 gears with 37s on Sahara/Sport. Rubicon owners with the 4.10 stock ratio can run 37s tolerable, but 4.56 is the right answer for serious trail use.

TPMS: The JL uses a direct TPMS system — sensors in each wheel. When you move to aftermarket wheels, the sensors must either be transferred from factory wheels or new sensors purchased and programmed. TPMS sensors for the JL are vehicle-specific; verify compatibility before ordering.

Steps

  1. If installing new wheels alongside new tires, mount tires on new wheels at a tire shop — spec 4.5"–5" backspacing for lifted JL builds.
  2. Verify TPMS sensors are transferred or new compatible sensors are installed before mounting.
  3. Lift the vehicle on jack stands and remove factory wheels.
  4. Install new wheel/tire assemblies. Torque lug nuts to the wheel maker's number in a star pattern (factory lugs on factory wheels are 130 ft-lb).
  5. Lower the vehicle and re-torque after 50 miles.
  6. Turn steering wheel to full lock in both directions and articulate suspension through full travel (ramp or spotter needed for this) to check for rub points.
  7. If rubbing is present: identify the location and address — rear fender liner removal is a 20-minute task, front upper control arm rub may require trim cutting or a minor lift correction.
  8. If no regear is yet planned, reset tire size in the vehicle's computer (speedometer calibration). JL uses the Uconnect system; some builds use a programmer or dealer reflash for this.

Why it works

  • 37s are the meaningful capability threshold for serious trail work — more traction footprint, more ground clearance, better over-rock rollover
  • The Rubicon's factory 33s and Jeep's 35-inch guidance give it the shortest path to 37s of any JL
  • BFG KM3, Nitto Trail Grappler, and Toyo OPMT are all proven tires with extensive JL trail-use history
  • Proper 37s make the JL look proportional — the body was designed with large tires in mind

Trade-offs

  • Every trim needs a lift for 37s — that cost is additive to the tire cost
  • Regear is functionally required on Sport and Sahara (3.45 standard) — budget an additional $1,800–$2,400 for a proper gear change
  • 37s are heavy — rotating mass increase affects acceleration, braking, and fuel economy noticeably
  • TPMS sensor compatibility requires verification for aftermarket wheels; factory sensors may need reprogramming
  • A full set of 37s plus a quality wheel set plus regear is a $4,000–$5,000 package total — budget realistically

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • torque wrench
  • lug wrench
  • TPMS programming tool (optional)
