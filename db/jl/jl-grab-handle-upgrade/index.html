# JL Wrangler Grab Handle Upgrade and Installation

The factory JL grab handles do the job, but they're designed around liability, not grip. The plastic housing can flex under load, and the cord diameter is narrow enough that a tight trail section with a white-knuckle passenger isn't comfortable. Aftermarket handles — most commonly paracord or solid aluminum tube versions — are a low-cost fix that makes a real difference for anyone riding in the back on technical terrain.

  • Replacement grab handles (set of 4 or the positions you care about)
  • T40 Torx socket and ratchet
  • Trim panel tool (for removing the factory handles cleanly)
  • Thread-locker (optional but recommended for rollbar mounts)

Remove factory handles

  1. The factory grab handles bolt to the roll bar via T40 Torx fasteners — typically two bolts per handle.
  2. On the front passenger A-pillar handle, there may be a plastic shroud that pulls off first before accessing the bolts. Use a trim panel tool at the seam to avoid scratching the pillar.
  3. Unthread both T40 bolts. The factory handle and its rubber mounting pads will come off as a unit.
  4. Clean the roll bar contact area — the factory pads leave residue.

Install new handles

  1. Position the new handle over the factory mounting holes. Most aftermarket handles use the same hole pattern.
  2. Thread in the mounting hardware included with the kit. Hand-tighten first to confirm alignment.
  3. Apply a drop of medium-strength thread-locker to each fastener if the manufacturer doesn't specify a thread treatment. Roll bar vibration on rough trails backs out dry fasteners.
  4. Torque to manufacturer spec, or approximately 15–18 ft-lbs for most M8-threaded roll bar mounts.

Front vs. rear handles

The front A-pillar handles serve as door grab handles as well as trail handles — they see the most use. The rear roll bar handles are primarily for passengers bracing on trail. If budget is limited, prioritize the rear handles; front passengers have the door handle and dash.

  • Thread-locker: This step matters. A grab handle that spins freely on a rough trail section is useless. Apply and give it a cure time before wheeling.
  • Hardware compatibility: Most kits include hardware sized for the factory bolt pattern. If they don't fit, verify the bolt diameter (typically M8) and pick up the right fasteners — don't use shorter hardware than the factory length.
  • Paracord maintenance: Woven paracord handles absorb trail dust and mud. They're machine washable on a gentle cycle in a mesh bag. Replace if the cord shows significant fraying at the anchor points.
  • Weight limit: Most aftermarket handles are rated 300–500 lbs. Don't use them as climbing holds to haul into the Jeep — they're bracing handles, not structural mounting points.
  • Rugged Ridge grab handle set: $40–$60
  • Solid aluminum tube handles (Rugged Ridge, Poison Spyder): $80–$120

The paracord versions are the best value. They're lighter than aluminum, look better than stock, and the grip is noticeably more comfortable for passengers bracing on ledgy trails.

  • ratchet
  • trim panel tool
