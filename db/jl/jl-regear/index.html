Re-Gearing the JL — 4.56 and 4.88 Ratio Selection

Difficulty 5/56–12 hrs$900–24002018-2024

The stock gear ratios on most JL trims were sized for street-biased tires — if you've moved to 35s or 37s and haven't regeared, you're working the drivetrain harder than it was designed for and leaving significant trail capability on the table.

The math behind regearing is straightforward. Your engine makes peak torque in a specific RPM range. Larger tires rotate more slowly per mile, which effectively changes your final drive ratio and moves your RPM operating point down out of that optimal range. The result: sluggish acceleration, transmission hunting on grades, and a crawl ratio that feels inadequate on steep technical terrain. Regearing restores the RPM relationship to a range the drivetrain was designed to operate in.

JL gear ratio context by trim:

  • Sahara: Stock 3.45. With 35s, move to 4.56. With 37s, move to 4.88.
  • Sport/Sport S: Stock 3.73. With 35s, 4.10 is acceptable; 4.56 is better. With 37s, 4.88.
  • Rubicon: Stock 4.10. With 35s, 4.10 is workable; 4.56 improves trail performance meaningfully. With 37s, 4.56 at minimum; 4.88 for serious rock builds.

The Sahara with 3.45 gears and 37s is the worst-case scenario — the highway RPM drop is noticeable, low-speed trail control is compromised, and the transmission is working in a range it wasn't mapped for. This is not a "it runs, a bit slow" situation; it's a configuration that accelerates powertrain wear. Regear before running 37s on a Sahara.

Front and rear must be regeared together. Running mismatched ratios front to rear on a part-time 4WD system causes severe binding and drivetrain damage when 4WD is engaged. This is not optional.

Yukon Gear is the benchmark brand for JL ring and pinion sets. Their steel quality is consistent, the manufacturing tolerances are tight (which streamlines setup), and they support both Dana 30/35 (Sport/Sahara) and Dana 44 (Rubicon) fitments. The master overhaul kit ($249) includes all bearings, races, seals, shims, and hardware needed to complete the job — don't attempt to reuse factory bearings on a regear.

Labor consideration: Setting up ring and pinion gears correctly is one of the most skill-dependent jobs in the drivetrain world. Incorrect backlash or bearing preload will cause gear noise, premature wear, or catastrophic failure within 10,000 miles. If you haven't done it before, the first regear should be done at a reputable 4WD shop — watch, learn, then DIY the second axle.

Steps

  1. Drain differential fluid from both axles before disassembly.
  2. Front axle: support axle, remove wheels, disconnect front driveshaft, remove axle shafts.
  3. Remove front diff cover, mark bearing cap positions before removal.
  4. Extract carrier/ring gear assembly, remove pinion.
  5. Press old pinion and carrier bearings off using a bearing puller or press.
  6. Install new pinion bearings on new pinion. Set pinion depth using shim stack (this requires a dial indicator and pattern checking — follow Yukon's instructions precisely).
  7. Install pinion, set pinion bearing preload to manufacturer spec using an inch-pound torque wrench.
  8. Transfer ring gear to carrier (or install new carrier if upgrading). Install new ring gear bolts — do not reuse old bolts.
  9. Set carrier bearing preload and ring gear backlash to spec. Check gear mesh contact pattern with gear marking compound — the pattern must be centered before final assembly.
  10. Install diff cover with new gasket, torque to spec.
  11. Repeat procedure for rear axle (Dana 35 or Dana 44 depending on trim).
  12. Fill both differentials with correct gear oil per manufacturer spec (some ring and pinion installs require specific break-in fluid — follow Yukon's recommendation).
  13. Drive gently for the first 500 miles. Avoid sustained high-speed runs during break-in. Change break-in fluid at 500 miles.

Why it works

  • Restores factory RPM operating range after tire size increase — the single most important drivetrain step for builds running 35s or 37s
  • Dramatically improves trail crawl ratio, low-speed control, and pull-away performance
  • 4.88 gears on a Sahara with 37s transforms a sluggish street-and-parking-lot Jeep into a capable trail rig
  • Yukon Gear's quality and documentation are the best in the class for JL fitments
  • Regear done alongside a locker install saves a second teardown — plan both jobs together when possible

Trade-offs

  • Difficulty 5 — incorrect gear mesh setup causes noise and failure; this is the most technically demanding job on a Jeep
  • Both axles must be done simultaneously, doubling the parts cost
  • Parts alone run $650–$800 (front + rear gear sets plus master kit); shop labor adds $1,200–$1,600 for the pair, making total installed cost $2,000–$2,400
  • Break-in period requires restraint — spirited driving in the first 500 miles risks premature gear wear
  • Dana 35 (Sport/Sahara rear) has a smaller ring gear and lighter shafts — regearing it to 4.88 doesn't change the fact that it's a lighter-duty axle
  • The Rubicon 392 has a different rear axle (Dana 44 AdvanTEK) with different gear selection — verify exact axle spec before purchasing gears

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • socket set
  • torque wrench
  • bearing puller
  • bearing press
  • dial indicator
  • inch-pound torque wrench
  • ring gear setup tools
  • press or shop press
