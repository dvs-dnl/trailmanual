Wheel Spacers — 1.25" to 1.5" for the JL Wrangler

Difficulty 1/50.5–1.0 hrs$80–2202018-2024

A 1.25"–1.5" hub-centric spacer on the JL pushes the wheel out enough to clear inner fender interference on larger tires and fills the wheel wells visually without committing to an aftermarket wheel purchase.

The JL Wrangler runs a 5×5 (5×127mm) bolt pattern with a 71.5mm center bore. Most trims come with modest stock offsets — the Sport and Sahara sit around +44mm, the Rubicon at a slightly more aggressive +30mm. When you move to 35" or larger tires, particularly on the Sport and Sahara, the stock offset can leave tire contact with the lower inner fender on full articulation. A 1.25"–1.5" spacer resolves that without requiring a wheel swap.

Hub-centric fit is the non-negotiable specification here. A hub-centric spacer seats on the hub's center bore, keeping the wheel centered by the hub rather than relying on lug torque alone. Lug-centric spacers exist and are cheaper — they also allow microscopic hub runout to become vibration. Buy hub-centric.

The 1.25" spacer is the sweet spot for a JL running 35s on a mild-to-moderate lift. It resolves common inner fender contact and adds visual stance without pushing the wheel to the outer edge of the fender flare. The 1.5" spacer works for builds with wider aftermarket fenders or for owners running 37s who want maximum clearance on articulation.

Spidertrax is the long-standing premium choice in this category — their bolt-on spacers use extended studs, which are mechanically superior to a spacer with its own stud pattern that stacks studs. BORA makes a quality hub-centric unit at a lower price point. Rugged Ridge's OEM-style spacers are the budget option and perform the task without drama.

Do not run spacers over 1.5" on stock JL wheel studs without upgrading to extended studs — the engagement length on the lug nut drops to marginal beyond that thickness.

Why it works

  • Resolves inner fender contact on 35s and 37s without a wheel purchase
  • Hub-centric design eliminates vibration from runout — fits like a proper part
  • 1.25"–1.5" stays within the JL's fender flare footprint — no additional cutting needed
  • Reversible if you later switch to offset wheels that don't need the spacer

Trade-offs

  • Extended studs (or included stud hardware) must be torqued to spec — undertorquing causes loosening on trail
  • Spacers over 1.5" require upgraded studs for safe lug engagement — do not skip this step
  • Adds a maintenance item: re-torque after first 50 miles and check periodically on trail rigs
  • Budget slip-on (lug-centric) spacers are not acceptable for off-road use — pay for hub-centric

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • torque wrench
  • socket set
  • wire brush (lug seat cleanup)
