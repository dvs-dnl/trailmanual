Jeep Wrangler JL / Suspension

Budget Lift Kit — Spacers and Entry Springs (≤$600)

Difficulty 2/52–4 hrs$150–6002018-2024
Short answer

A budget lift on the JL clears 33s with room to spare and lifts the stance without touching the geometry — but you are making trade-offs, and you should know what they are before you buy.

Coil spacers and entry-level spring kits work on the JL because the factory suspension geometry is already reasonably dialed for street and mild trail use. Up to 1.5–2 inches, you can add height without significant caster correction or control arm adjustment — the JL's geometry tolerates that range better than the JK did. The Teraflex 1.5" spacer kit is the cleanest execution of this approach: it uses a proper spacer that mounts between the coil and the perch rather than a puck shoved in the spring, which limits bind. Daystar's 2" coil spacers are the same idea at a lower price point but ship as raw spacers with no additional hardware — fine for overland rigs that don't see aggressive flexing.

The Rough Country 2" kit steps up to replacement springs and includes N3 shocks, which is a meaningfully better outcome for the money than spacers alone. The shocks are not premium, but they are better valved for the lifted ride height than the factory shocks pushed beyond their intended range. If you're shopping this tier because budget is a hard constraint, the Rough Country kit is the most complete option under $250. know that the control arm angles are not corrected at 2" — you will notice mild vibration at highway speed in some rigs, and death wobble risk increases if your front-end components (tie rod, track bar, ball joints) are anything less than perfect.

What is not included at this price point: adjustable control arms, caster correction, geometry-matched sway bar end links, or track bar correction. Those items matter more as lift height increases and as trail use intensifies. A 1.5"–2" spacer lift on a mild-trail or overlanding JL is a defensible choice. A 2" spacer lift on a rig that sees rocky terrain regularly is asking for accelerated wear on your front-end components.

Steps

  1. Chock rear wheels, loosen front lug nuts, then lift the front of the vehicle and support on jack stands under the frame rails.
  2. Remove front wheels and set aside.
  3. Support the front axle with the floor jack — don't let it hang on the CV axles.
  4. Remove the front sway bar end links (or disconnect them if using detachable links).
  5. Slowly lower the axle to relieve spring tension. Pull the factory coil springs.
  6. Install spacers per manufacturer instructions. For Daystar puck-style spacers, seat them fully on the upper spring perch before seating the spring beneath.
  7. Reinstall springs, raise the axle to seat the spring, reconnect sway bar end links.
  8. Reinstall wheels, torque lug nuts to 130 ft-lb on factory lugs; use the wheel maker's number on aftermarket wheels.
  9. Repeat for rear axle: support frame, lower axle, swap rear coils or install rear spacers.
  10. Lower vehicle fully to ground, then torque all suspension fasteners to spec with the vehicle at ride height (critical — torquing at full droop will bind the bushings).
  11. Drive to an alignment shop within 50 miles. A 2" lift on the JL will require at minimum a front toe alignment check.

Why it works

  • Lowest cost entry into a lifted JL — Daystar spacers at $129 are hard to beat for a mild stance lift
  • Bolt-on installation with basic tools; no specialty equipment beyond a spring compressor
  • Retains factory ride quality at the 1.5" range
  • Rough Country kit includes shocks that are better matched to the new ride height than factory units
  • Reversible — you can return to stock by removing spacers

Trade-offs

  • No caster correction or geometry adjustment — factory control arm angles are not changed
  • Increased highway vibration risk, especially on JLs with any front-end wear
  • Spacer lifts stress the CV axles at full droop — more aggressive trail use will accelerate axle wear
  • Rough Country shocks are adequate but not trail-capable; they will not hold up to repeated hard hits on rocky trails
  • Does not clear 35" tires without fender trimming on non-Rubicon trims
  • Not suitable as a foundation for future lift height increases — spacers compound angle problems already present

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • socket set
  • torque wrench
  • spring compressor
