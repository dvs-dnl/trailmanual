Jeep Wrangler JL / Axles

Dana 44 AdvanTEK Front — Service and Upgrade

Difficulty 3/51–8 hrs$40–18002018-2025Jump to steps
Short answer

Service the JL front Dana 44 AdvanTEK every 30,000 miles with about 2.2 pints of GL-5 gear oil, and know that the FAD housing and stock axle shafts are the two weak points that snap first when you go bigger than 35s.

The JL Rubicon's front axle is the Dana 44 AdvanTEK (model M210, wide track). Sport and Sahara trims get a Dana 30 front unless the truck was ordered with the optional Dana 44s, so check the axle tag before you buy parts. Both front axles carry a Front Axle Disconnect (FAD). The Rubicon's electronic locker lives in this housing. Jeep's 2020 JL spec table lists 2.18 pints (1.03 L) of Mopar 75W-85 GL-5 for the Dana 44 front and 1.82 pints for the Dana 30, filled to the bottom of the fill hole. That is about one quart, not two. Change interval is every 30,000 miles under normal driving and every 15,000 miles for vehicles that see water crossings, sand, or regular trail use.

The FAD casting is the part to watch. The cast aluminum FAD housing is brittle and is the most common failure point on stock JL fronts, and the plastic FAD coupler bushing inside it wears out and dumps gear oil. If your build is heading past 35-inch tires, the honest move is a FAD-delete with Spicer chromoly shafts — this swaps the plastic coupler for a solid one-piece shaft and gets rid of the disconnect mechanism entirely. Rubicons have the disconnect too: Spicer's kit for the wide-track, electric-locker Dana 44 (PN 10044474) includes FAD removal, and its Dana 30 kit (PN 10044417) does the same for Sport and Sahara axles.

Stock M210 axle shafts are adequate up to 35-inch tires for trail use. Past 35s, especially with lockers and heavy throttle, expect to bend or twist stock shafts within a season or two of hard wheeling. Chromoly upgrades push the working limit to 37-inch tires with the rest of the drivetrain unchanged. An axle truss (Next Venture Motorsports or Artec) adds housing rigidity for builds running 37s+ with lockers — necessary on rigs that see rock crawling, optional for trail rigs.

Ball joints on M210s are a known wear item. Listen for clunks at full lock and inspect for grease weeping at every fluid change. Replace as a set with Synergy or Dynatrac ball joints; the stock joints are unsealed in the lower position and gravel finds its way in.

Aftermarket diff covers (Artec, ARB, Rusty's, AEV) add about 4 ounces of capacity and a magnetic drain plug. They protect the housing on rocks and make fluid changes cleaner because of the drain plug — the stock cover requires you to pull the whole cover to drain.

Steps

  1. Park on level ground, warm the axle by driving 5–10 miles so the old fluid drains cleanly.
  2. Position drain pan under the diff cover. Crack the fill plug (top of cover, 3/8" square drive) FIRST to confirm it backs out — if it's stuck and you've already drained, you can't refill.
  3. If you have an aftermarket cover with a drain plug, pull the plug and drain. Otherwise, loosen the cover bolts in a crisscross pattern, leave the top two snug, and pry the bottom of the cover loose to drain.
  4. Remove the cover, wipe the housing clean, inspect the ring gear teeth for pitting or chipping, and check the magnet for metal chunks (fine paste is normal; chunks are not).
  5. Scrape old RTV off both mating surfaces with a plastic scraper. Wipe with brake cleaner.
  6. Apply 1/4" bead of Mopar gray RTV or equivalent silicone around the cover, going inside the bolt holes. Install cover and torque bolts in a crisscross pattern to 15 ft-lb plus 20 degrees, the figure in both Northridge4x4's chart and Jeep's 2020 spec table.
  7. Wait 30 minutes for RTV to set, then fill through the fill hole until fluid is at the bottom of the threads (about 2.2 pints). Reinstall fill plug to 25 ft-lb.
  8. Drive 20–50 miles and recheck for leaks at the cover and fill plug.

Why it works

  • Fluid change is a one-hour job with a $40 parts bill
  • Service catches FAD wear, ball joint slop, and locker-fluid contamination
  • Chromoly shaft upgrade is a bolt-in once the axle is open

Trade-offs

  • FAD housing is a known weak point on every JL front axle, Rubicon included
  • Stock shafts bend on 37s+ — plan a chromoly budget if you're going big
  • Published fluid grades disagree between documents, so confirm the grade in your owner's manual before you buy

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • 13mm socket
  • fluid pump
  • torque wrench
  • RTV
  • drain pan
