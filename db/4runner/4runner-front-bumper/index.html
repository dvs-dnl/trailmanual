Steel Front Bumper for the 5th Gen 4Runner

Difficulty 3/52–4 hrs$849–17992010-2024

The 5th gen 4Runner's factory bumper sits low with a full-width plastic chin that reduces approach angle — a steel replacement improves the geometry, mounts a winch properly, and adds the recovery infrastructure the factory design lacks.

The ARB Summit Bar is the premium choice for the 4Runner and the most commonly specified bumper on serious build threads. It's full-width, designed to accept a Warn or Mile Marker winch in the integrated plate, and includes mounting provisions for OEM fog lights and parking sensors. The weld and finish quality is class-leading at this price point. It is not a light bumper — the Summit Bar adds approximately 80–100 lbs — but on a 4Runner that is carrying expedition gear, the additional mass is already in the budget.

Parking sensors are present on most 5th gen 4Runner trims from 2010 onward and the position varies by trim year. Most quality bumpers include sensor relocation brackets and drilling templates. Confirm your specific trim's sensor configuration (number of sensors and position) against the bumper manufacturer's compatibility chart before ordering. CBI, ARB, Expedition One, and Fab Four all offer sensor-compatible designs — some require minor drilling to accept the OEM sensor housing, which takes 20 minutes and is not an obstacle.

Full-width vs stubby is an approach-angle trade-off. Full-width bumpers protect the outer fender edges on brush and tight trail situations. Stubby bumpers (used by some Fab Four designs) improve the approach angle by removing the outer wing sections — the result is 3°–5° more approach clearance at the cost of the outer fender protection. For rock crawling, stubby is often preferred. For overland and mixed use, full-width is more practical.

The improvement in approach angle from any of these bumpers over the OEM unit is roughly 5°–7° on the 4Runner. The OEM bumper's low chin catches on exit lips and ledge faces that an aftermarket bumper clears. That's a tangible difference on moderate rocky terrain.

Why it works

  • ARB Summit Bar is the benchmark 4Runner bumper — excellent quality, proven fit, wide accessory ecosystem
  • Integrated winch plate provides the proper load path for 10,000+ lb winch pull
  • Approach angle improvement of 5°–7° is meaningful on technical rocky terrain
  • D-ring recovery tabs rated to 4.75 tons on ARB designs — correct for trail recovery use
  • Parking sensor compatibility built into most quality designs — no need for DIY bracket fabrication

Trade-offs

  • ARB Summit Bar is the most expensive option at $1,699 — verify the build justifies the premium vs CBI at $1,299
  • Full-width designs add 80–100 lbs to the front of the truck
  • Solo install is manageable but a second person makes alignment and bolt starting much more manageable
  • Parking sensor relocation may require software recalibration on some 4Runner trims — check with dealer
  • Stubby bumper designs sacrifice outer fender protection that matters on brushy trails

Tools required

  • socket set
  • torque wrench
  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • drill and bits
  • wire harness tools
