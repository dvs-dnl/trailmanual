Toyota 4Runner / Wheels & Tires

35s on the 5th Gen 4Runner: Lift, Body Mounts, Cutting, and Gears

Difficulty 4/58–24 hrs$3300–56002010-2024Jump to steps
Short answer

A 35 fits a 5th gen 4Runner, but it is a body-modification project more than a tire swap. Plan on about 3 inches of lift, cut fenders, and a chopped or relocated body mount, then gears on both axles. If you do not want to cut the truck, stop at a 285/75R17, about 34 inches, which still takes a body mount chop but far less work than 35s.

Scope: 2010 to 2024 trucks. The 2025 and newer 4Runner is a different platform, and its fitment guide could not be opened for this article.

Trail4Runner's chart lists these against the factory 265/70R17 (31.61 inches):

TireHeightWhat it takes
285/70R1732.71 inchesFine with a 3-inch lift, possibly touching liners. BMC may be needed on a 2-inch level.
285/75R1733.8 inchesRubs liners, body, body mounts, possibly firewall. BMC needed, plus numerous other mods.
35x12.50R1734.76 inchesListed only as needing adjustments and mods. No lift height stated.

C4 Fabrication gives the most specific answer for a 35: its kit is built for a truck with a 3-inch lift and promises minimal to no rubbing, and it requires cutting the factory fenders and welding in a relocated body mount. Trail4Runner's general advice is the same direction. An owner there with 285/75R17s on a 2-inch lift still rubbed after trimming, and the author told that owner to run at least 3 inches with serious trimming. ThreePiece's guide says 2 to 3 inches of lift clears 285/75R16 or 33x12.50 tires without trimming and does not address 35s.

Lift does not solve this by itself. The tire runs into the body behind the wheel, so body work follows whatever height you choose.

The body mount behind the front wheel is the biggest obstacle.

  • Chop (BMC): cuts the frame-side mount back and welds a plate over it. Trail4Runner says chops work well for 33s up to 34s and a deep one has been known to clear 35s. It puts parts and labor around $250, with a note that this is an estimate, and a reader lists one kit at $20 in parts.
  • Relocation: cuts the whole mount off and moves it behind the firewall with a welded backing plate, grade 8 hardware, and new bushings. Trail4Runner puts the kit at about $250 plus labor, and calls it far more involved, with carpet and sound deadener out and a bolt pulled through the floor.
  • Relocation is not a guarantee. The author's own 35-inch Mickey Thompson Baja Boss tires still touch the firewall after relocation, and the next step named there is tubbing the firewall.

Treat this as structural work on the mount that holds the body to the frame. If you are not a confident welder, hire it out, then check the nut and bolt after a week or two as the article advises.

Trail4Runner's 34-inch list is the floor for a 35:

  1. Trim the body around the wheel well for clearance at full lock.
  2. Push the front fender liner back and re-drill its mount further inboard. Their build cut back about six inches of liner before the rubbing stopped.
  3. Trim front and rear liners and the inner portion of the rocker or valance caps.
  4. Bend or hammer the pinch welds. Go gently, because too much force can throw the door jamb line off-center.
  5. Chop the body mount. Optionally bend the pinch seam and remove the splash guards.

The rear of the wheel well is not the only trouble spot; the article says the front of the well near the grille needs attention too. C4's kit replaces the trimmed liners with high-clearance liners, at $799 with the relocation parts included.

The 5th gen uses a 6x139.7mm (6x5.5-inch) pattern. Trail4Runner lists most factory wheels at 17x7 or 17x7.5, +15mm or +4mm depending on trim, all around 4.5 inches of backspacing, though its readers dispute some of those values.

For 34s, the author's replies suggest a wheel no wider than 9 inches, about -4 to -12mm offset, and 4 to 4.875 inches of backspacing. The author's 34-inch wheel was a 17x8.5 at -6mm with 4.5 inches of backspacing. No published offset for a 35 turned up. Trail4Runner warns that more negative offset makes rubbing more likely, and that a 2-inch spacer moves the tire out 2 inches. ThreePiece adds that aggressive offset may need a caster correction or diff drop to prevent CV binding. See the offset guide and upper control arm guide. Trail4Runner says UCAs add caster but are not always needed.

Trail4Runner calls a regear almost mandatory on 35s. Factory is 3.73. Most owners there choose 4.88, and every 35-inch owner in that article reports better performance on 4.88s. Its older sizing article suggests 4.88 or 5.29 and calls the choice subjective. The regear guide on this site leans to 5.29 for 35s, so treat 4.88 and 5.29 as the two real options and choose on weight and how much crawling you do.

ExtremeTerrain's chart at 65 mph in a 1:1 gear puts a 35 at about 2,328 rpm on 3.73, 3,045 on 4.88, and 3,201 on 5.13. 5.29 is not on the chart; the same formula gives about 3,300. Overdrive runs lower. One reader with 4.88s and a heavy truck likes the uphill crawl but finds it harder to slow in 4LO downhill.

  • KDSS interaction with a 35-inch build. C4's listing does not mention KDSS. A Trail4Runner author reports lifting a KDSS truck 4.5 inches without trouble, and that is the only data point we opened. Read the KDSS guide and ask the lift maker.
  • Whether a 35 stows in the factory underbody spare location. Trail4Runner's visible answer only confirms 285/70R17 spares. Plan a carrier until you have tested yours.
  • Long-term rear axle and CV durability on 35s. Owner forum threads on this could not be opened.

Four to five 35s at $417 to $432 each come to about $1,670 to $2,160 at Quadratec prices when written. Add $250 to $799 for body mount work and liners. Add $1,400 to $2,600 for gears, the range on the regear guide. That is the $3,300 to $5,600 above, before the lift and wheels. Five KO2 LT285/75R17s at about $352 each come to about $1,760, a few hundred less than a 35 set. The bigger savings from stopping at 34s are in the body work.

Steps

  1. Decide between a 34 and a 35. Mount one tire before buying the set if you can.
  2. Install the lift and get an alignment. Add UCAs if the shop cannot reach caster.
  3. Mount the wheel and tire and cycle it. Compress each corner safely and turn lock to lock, marking contact with chalk.
  4. Remove mud flaps and splash guards, push back and trim liners, and trim valance caps.
  5. Bend pinch welds in small steps.
  6. Chop or relocate the body mount, or send the truck to a shop for this step.
  7. Trim the body in the wheel well, and treat all bare metal against rust.
  8. Recheck at full lock and full compression. Add bump stops if contact remains at full bump.
  9. Tighten the lugs to your owner's manual or wheel maker's number. Recheck after the first drive and check the body mount hardware after a week or two.
  10. Correct the speedometer and regear both axles.

Why it works

  • 35s are documented on this platform by owners and by a purpose-built kit.
  • A 285/75R17 offers most of the height for less cutting.
  • Regear results are consistently reported as a real improvement.

Trade-offs

  • You will cut fenders and a body mount. Neither can be undone.
  • Even with a relocation, a 35 can still touch the firewall.
  • Gears are close to mandatory, and they add thousands to the tire bill.
  • Sources here are heavy on owner reports and a single kit maker.

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • torque wrench
  • reciprocating saw or cut-off wheel
  • welder or a welding shop
  • body hammer
  • drill
  • chalk or paint marker
