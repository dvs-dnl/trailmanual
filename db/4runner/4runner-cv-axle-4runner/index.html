Toyota 4Runner / Drivetrain

Front CV Axle Replacement

Difficulty 3/52–4 hrs$150–4502010-2024Jump to steps
Short answer

Replace a 5th-gen 4Runner front CV axle with a Toyota OEM unit (PN 43430-60083) in two to four hours, and use the updated nut PN 90177-A0023 with new OEM axles. Published axle nut torques conflict — Trail4Runner lists 174 ft-lb and toyota-specs.com lists 203 ft-lbf — so take the figure from the factory service manual.

Front CV axles on the 5th gen 4Runner (2010-onward) and the related GX460 typically fail from torn boots first. The boot rips on rocks or against a poorly clearanced UCA after a lift, grease flings out, the joint runs dry, and within a few thousand miles you have a clicking sound on tight turns under throttle. Once you hear the click, the joint is done — re-booting at that stage is a temporary fix. Replace the axle assembly.

Toyota OEM is the honest answer for stock or moderately lifted 4Runners that see daily driving. The OEM axle (43430-60083 fits both sides on 2011-2024 4Runners and FJ Cruisers) is rebuildable, the boots last twice as long as most aftermarket boots, and the joint stays quiet at full droop with lift. Expect about $400-450 for the OEM unit. Note that current production OEM axles require the updated axle nut (90177-A0023) — buy two if you're doing both sides.

Aftermarket options like GSP Premium Loaded or Cardone Select run $140-170 and are acceptable for stock-height 4Runners. They will not hold up as well on a lifted truck running 33s or 34s and dropping into the throttle in 4-low. The cheapest econo-brand axles ($60-90) bind at lift-height droop angles and tear boots within a year — skip them.

If you are lifted more than 2.5 inches up front, you are running the CV at angles it was never designed to operate at. The two things that prolong axle life on a lifted 4Runner are: (1) limiting down-travel with proper bumpstops so the CV never goes past its safe angle, and (2) upgrading to aftermarket upper control arms that re-locate the upper ball joint to keep the spindle more vertical. Without those changes, even an OEM CV axle is on a clock.

Common mistake: forgetting to back off the axle nut before lifting the truck. The nut is torqued to roughly 174 to 203 ft-lbs depending on which chart you trust, so it takes real force to remove and the wheel needs to be on the ground (or a helper holding the brake) so the hub doesn't spin.

Steps

  1. Loosen the front lug nuts, then loosen the axle nut while the wheel is still on the ground — staking-clip side must be unpeened first using a punch.
  2. Jack the front, set jack stands under the frame rails, and remove the wheel.
  3. Pull the brake caliper off and hang it from the coil spring with mechanic's wire. Do not unhook the brake line.
  4. Remove the rotor.
  5. Disconnect the outer tie rod from the steering knuckle (19mm castle nut, cotter pin first).
  6. Remove the two bolts that hold the lower ball joint attachment to the bottom of the knuckle (19mm). Do not disturb the LCA-to-frame bolts — those affect alignment.
  7. Pull the axle nut off completely. Swing the knuckle outward and pull the CV stub out of the hub. If it's stuck, give it a few persuasive hits with a brass drift on the threaded end.
  8. Pry the inner CV joint out of the front differential with a pry bar against the inner cup. Catch the small amount of diff fluid that leaks.
  9. Wire-brush both the hub splines and the differential output splines. Apply anti-seize to the hub splines.
  10. Slide the new axle's inner joint into the differential — it should click into the C-clip with firm hand pressure. If it won't seat, you'll need to tap it home gently with a soft mallet.
  11. Reconnect the outer stub through the hub, reinstall the ball joint attachment bolts and torque them to the service-manual figure (Trail4Runner lists 118 ft-lb, toyota-specs.com 103 ft-lbf), reconnect the tie rod (67 ft-lbs castle nut, new cotter pin).
  12. Install the NEW axle nut (PN 90177-A0023 if running new OEM axle). Torque to the service-manual figure (see the conflict above) with the wheel on the ground. Stake the nut collar into the groove on the axle.
  13. Reinstall rotor, caliper, and wheel. Torque lug nuts to 83 ft-lbs.
  14. Check front diff fluid level — top off if any was lost during the swap.

Why it works

  • Stops the clicking and prevents stranding when the joint finally lets go
  • OEM-quality axles last 100,000+ miles on a stock-height truck
  • Clean access compared to many other vehicles — no ABS sensor or vacuum disconnect to fight

Trade-offs

  • Axle nut requires impact tools or a long breaker bar to break free
  • Old axle inner joint can be stubborn out of the differential — wear safety glasses
  • C-clip on the inner stub must seat fully or the axle will work itself loose under throttle

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • 35mm socket
  • 19mm socket
  • breaker bar
  • torque wrench
  • pry bar
  • hammer
  • anti-seize
  • wire brush
