Engine Air Filter Replacement

Difficulty 1/50.1–0.25 hrs$15–502003-2009, 2010-2024

The 4Runner engine air filter is a five-minute swap with no tools required on most years — pull the four clips, lift the airbox lid, drop the new filter in, close it up.

Toyota recommends changing the engine air filter every 30,000 miles under normal driving conditions, and sooner if you spend significant time on dirt roads or trails. Phoenix-area owners, anyone who runs Forest Service roads regularly, or anyone who's done a Baja-style trip should plan on 15,000–20,000 miles instead. A clogged filter doesn't usually throw a code — it quietly costs you fuel economy and throttle response.

The 5th gen 4Runner (2010–2024) uses Toyota part number 17801-38051, later superseded by 17801-YZZ13. The two are interchangeable. Aftermarket options from FRAM, K&N, AEM, and Wix all fit and run $15–$50. Reusable oiled cotton filters like K&N flow more air but require periodic cleaning and reoiling, and over-oiling has been linked to MAF sensor contamination. For most owners, a paper OEM-style replacement is the right call.

The filter box sits on the passenger side of the engine bay, near the firewall. Open the hood and you'll see the black plastic box with metal clips on the lid. There's no airflow direction arrow that matters — the pleats need to face down so debris drops into the body of the airbox rather than the lid.

Inspect the airbox interior while you have it open. Mouse nests, leaves, and acorns are common in vehicles that sit, and any of those will get drawn through a fresh filter quickly. Vacuum or wipe out anything you find before installing the new element.

Why it works

  • No tools, no jacks, no fluids
  • Five-minute job
  • Most-skipped maintenance item with real fuel economy and throttle response payoff

Trade-offs

  • OEM filters cost more than paper alternatives but fit precisely
  • Oiled-cotton filters can contaminate MAF sensors if over-oiled

Tools required

  • flathead screwdriver (optional)
