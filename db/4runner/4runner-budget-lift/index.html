Toyota 4Runner / Suspension

Budget Lift Kit — Bilstein 5100 and Spacers (≤$600)

Difficulty 2/53–6 hrs$399–6492010-2024Jump to steps
Short answer

The Bilstein B8 5100 set is the correct budget lift for 4Runners — a direct replacement of the factory shocks that adjusts from 0 to 2.5" of front lift on snap-ring grooves and supports up to about 1.5" in the rear, with meaningfully better damping and no new arms at 2" or less. KDSS trucks need one extra step, covered below.

The 5th gen 4Runner (2010–2024) comes in two suspension configurations: non-KDSS (SR5, Limited, and base trims) and KDSS-equipped (KDSS was offered on the Trail Edition and TRD Off-Road and optional on the TRD Pro). KDSS stands for Kinetic Dynamic Suspension System — hydraulic cylinders in the sway bar linkages, tied together by lines, that let the bars stiffen on the road and free up off-road. KDSS does not call for special shock valving; the catch is geometry. As the truck is raised, the KDSS cylinder extends on one side while the rigid link on the other side cannot, which is why lifted KDSS trucks lean, and the rear panhard bar can end up touching the rear KDSS sway bar. KDSS spacer or drop kits restore the bar angle. Some lift kits are sold in separate KDSS and non-KDSS versions, so order the one the kit maker lists for your truck. Confirm which you have by looking for hydraulic cylinders and lines at the sway bar end links front and rear; non-KDSS 4Runners have conventional end links.

The Bilstein B8 5100 is the cleanest budget approach. The front 5100 is a monotube shock with snap-ring grooves for the spring seat: pick the groove for roughly 1.75" of front lift and run the rear 5100s to match. You get improved damping, the lift, and no new arms at this height. The Rough Country kit is the lowest-cost option but uses lower-quality dampers and is not recommended for builds that will see regular trail use. ICON's Stage 1 is not a budget option: it is a coilover, rear spring and rear shock kit selling for about $2,180 and belongs with the mid-tier lifts.

At 2" of lift on a non-KDSS 4Runner, the factory UCAs are operating at their outer geometry limit but don't yet require replacement. Caster changes are manageable. For anything above 2", UCAs become necessary on the 4Runner as on the Tacoma.

Steps

  1. Confirm KDSS or non-KDSS configuration before ordering; on a KDSS truck, add KDSS spacers or a drop kit to the order
  2. Lift front of vehicle, support on jack stands, remove front wheels
  3. Remove OEM front shock/strut assembly (disconnect sway bar end link, brake line bracket, the single lower shock bolt at the lower control arm, upper mount)
  4. With a spring compressor, transfer the factory coil spring and upper mount onto the Bilstein 5100, setting the snap ring in the groove for your target height per Bilstein's chart
  5. Torque the lower shock bolt (at ride height) and upper mount nuts to the service-manual spec
  6. Reconnect sway bar end link and brake line bracket; reinstall wheel
  7. Repeat opposite side
  8. Lift rear of vehicle; remove rear wheels
  9. Support rear axle under differential
  10. Disconnect rear sway bar end links
  11. Remove and replace rear shocks (two bolts each — upper and lower mount)
  12. Reinstall wheels, lower vehicle
  13. Bounce suspension to settle; torque all fasteners at ride height
  14. Verify ride height front and rear; adjust front collar if needed for level stance
  15. Get an alignment — even at 2" the alignment shifts enough to affect tire wear

Why it works

  • Clean direct-replacement install with no geometry changes required at 2" lift
  • Bilstein 5100 is a proven monotube design — significantly better than OEM damping for trail and washboard
  • Front height adjustment collar allows fine-tuning for precise level stance
  • On non-KDSS models it is a straightforward swap
  • Fits 285/75R16 (33") tires with minor fender trimming at 2" lift

Trade-offs

  • KDSS trucks need spacers or a drop kit to correct the bar angle and the lean after a lift
  • Does not address CV angle or caster above 2" — UCAs required for 2.5"+
  • Rough Country kit uses lower-quality shocks — acceptable for mild use, not recommended for regular trail driving
  • Spacer lifts retain the factory damper — if factory shocks are worn, you're adding lift without improving damping
  • Get an alignment — it's not optional after a lift, even at this height

Tools required

  • floor jack
  • jack stands
  • socket set
  • torque wrench
  • spring compressor
  • breaker bar
