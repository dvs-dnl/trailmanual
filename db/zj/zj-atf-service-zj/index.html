42RE / 46RE Transmission Fluid Service

Difficulty 3/52–4 hrs$60–1401993-1998

The 42RE (behind the 4.0L six) and the 46RE (behind the 5.2 and 5.9 Magnum V8s) are both updates to the old A500 / A518 series. They need ATF+4 — not Dexron, not Mercon, not "universal." A pan drop and filter swap takes 5 quarts and buys you 30,000 more miles of clutch life.

ATF+4 is mandatory. The older ATF+3 (and the original ATF+) are superseded by ATF+4, and Mopar 05013457AA is the genuine fluid. Any ATF+4 that meets MS-9602 spec is acceptable — Valvoline MaxLife ATF+4 and Castrol Transmax both qualify. Do not run Dexron or Mercon. The frictional properties are wrong and you'll get shudder, late shifts, and eventual band damage.

A pan drop (the only practical home service — these transmissions have no drain plug) refreshes about 30% of the total fluid. Total capacity is 8.5–11 quarts dry; a service refill is 4.5–5 quarts. Plan to do a pan drop every 30,000 miles. If the fluid is burnt-smelling or dark brown instead of red, drop the pan, change the filter, and refill — then repeat the pan drop in 1,000 miles to swap more of the converter's fluid by dilution.

Service interval: 30,000 miles for normal driving. 15,000 for trail use or towing. If the previous owner never serviced it and you bought the ZJ used, do the service immediately — but if the fluid is already burnt-black, talk to a transmission shop before you do anything; a fresh-fluid shock can flush varnish loose and clog passages on a worn 42RE.

Why it works

  • Cheap insurance for an aging transmission — clutches and bands last longer with clean fluid
  • Catches metal contamination on the pan magnet before it grinds through the planetary gears
  • A burnt-smelling drop tells you exactly what state the trans is in before you trail it

Trade-offs

  • Pan-drop service only refreshes ~30% of total fluid — for a true flush you need a shop machine
  • Aged ZJs with never-serviced fluid can react badly to fresh ATF — old varnish breaks loose and clogs valves
  • The 42RE/46RE doesn't have a drain plug, so the job is messier than a modern transmission
  • Over-torquing pan bolts warps the flange — this is the most common DIY mistake

Tools required

  • floor jack and jack stands
  • drain pan (wide, low profile, 8+ qt capacity)
  • 10mm socket and 13mm socket (pan bolts vary)
  • torque wrench
  • gasket scraper
  • fluid pump or long-neck funnel (fill is through the dipstick tube)
  • shop rags
  • brake cleaner
