12V Fridge for Power Wagon (Dometic CFX3 / ARB Zero / ICECO VL)

Difficulty 1/51–4 hrs$550–16002014-2018, 2019-2024, 2025+
Short answer

A 12V compressor fridge is the single highest-ROI overland upgrade. Set the temp, forget about it, no ice management. For a Power Wagon-sized rig with a 6.4' bed, a 45L fridge is the sweet spot — enough for a week of food for two, not so big it eats half the cargo bay. Mount on a fridge slide so you can pull it out without climbing into the bed.

A 12V compressor fridge (not thermoelectric — those are junk for camping) is the universal Power Wagon overland upgrade. Three tiers:

ICECO VL45 ProS ($580) — value pick. 45L capacity, Secop compressor (the same Danish compressor used in premium units), digital temperature display, USB charging port. Slightly noisier than Dometic/ARB but works fine for occasional use. Best $/L on the market.

Dometic CFX3 45 ($1,100) — the reference. Same compressor class as ICECO but with better insulation (lower amp draw — typically 0.5-1.0 A/hr in 90°F ambient), WiFi monitoring, dual-zone capability (fridge + freezer in one unit). The most popular overland fridge in North America. 5-year warranty.

ARB Zero 47 ($1,500) — premium. Best insulation in class (lowest amp draw of any 45L unit on the market), titanium-reinforced corners (drop-rated), removable basket. The pick if you camp 50+ days/year or live in hot climates.

Power Wagon-specific wiring: the bed has a factory 12V outlet at the rear right corner (rated 20A) — fine for any of these fridges. For serious off-grid use, wire direct from the battery through a 15A inline fuse and Anderson Powerpole. That bypasses the ignition-off cut-off (some PWs cut bed-outlet power 30 min after key-off) and gives you a clean 24/7 connection.

Dual battery consideration: a single factory battery and an ignition-off fridge will discharge to low-voltage cutoff in 8-14 hours depending on temperature. For multi-day camping with the engine off, plan on a dual-battery setup (Genesis Offroad or REDARC isolator + auxiliary battery). The Power Wagon has factory provisions for a second battery — there's a mounting tray and dedicated stud already there.

Fridge slide: if you mount the fridge in the bed (most common location), a fridge slide ($380) extends it out the tailgate for access without climbing in. The Power Wagon's tall bed makes this almost mandatory — without a slide, you're reaching down 36" to grab beer.

The factory bed outlet works for a weekend, but a direct-wired feed is what makes a fridge a true overland appliance.

  1. Decide the feed. The rear-right bed outlet (20A) runs any 45L fridge fine — until the truck cuts bed-outlet power some minutes after key-off, which kills your fridge overnight. For multi-day use, wire direct.
  2. For a direct feed, run a fused line from the battery (or the auxiliary battery) to the bed: 12-gauge wire, a 15A inline fuse within a few inches of the power source, and an Anderson Powerpole or SAE connector at the fridge end.
  3. Route the wire along an existing harness path, through the cab grommet or bed-floor pass-through, away from the exhaust and any pinch points. Loom it where it crosses sheet-metal edges.
  4. Check voltage at the fridge end with the engine off and running — you want to see battery voltage with minimal drop. A long undersized run that sags below the fridge's low-voltage cutoff will cycle the compressor off when you need it most.
  5. Set the fridge's battery-protection level to match your setup: low or medium on a single battery (so it shuts off before stranding you), low on a managed dual-battery bank.

A single starting battery and an ignition-off fridge discharge to cutoff in 8-14 hours, so plan the pw-dual-battery setup alongside the fridge if you camp without running the engine, and mount the fridge on a slide so the pw-overland-drawer and cargo plan stay accessible.

The defining mistake is buying a thermoelectric "cooler" instead of a compressor fridge — they cannot hold a safe food temperature in a hot truck bed and have no place in a build. After that, the two things that bite people are power and access. Undersized or overly long wiring causes nuisance low-voltage shutoffs that look like a broken fridge but are really a wiring problem, and a 45L unit dropped into the Power Wagon's tall bed without a slide means reaching 36 inches down every time, so the slide is functionally part of the install, not an option. Buy the fridge used if you can — Dometic and ARB units hold value, so a lightly used one saves 30-50% with no real downside.

Why it works

  • Set-and-forget cold food for a week+
  • No ice management
  • Doubles as a freezer (dual-zone models)
  • Dometic/ARB resale value is strong — buy used to save 30-50%

Trade-offs

  • $580-1,500 for the fridge alone
  • Needs dual battery for serious off-grid use (+$400-800)
  • 45L eats meaningful bed space — fridge slide is functionally required
  • Power Wagon's bed-outlet ignition cutoff may force direct-wire install

Tools required

  • wire strippers
  • fuse holder + Anderson Powerpole connector (for direct wire)
  • voltmeter
