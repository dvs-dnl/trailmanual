Side Steps and Rock Rails for the Power Wagon

Difficulty 2/51–2 hrs$329–6992016-2024

True rock rails for the Power Wagon are harder to find than on Wrangler-based builds — but this truck sits high enough from the factory that rocker contact is genuinely less frequent, and a quality step with tube protection handles most of what the trail throws at the sides.

The Power Wagon's ground clearance starts at 14.3" from the factory — higher than most lifted Wranglers. That height, combined with the truck's wider track, puts the rocker panels further from rock contact than on lower-slung rigs. It doesn't mean the rockers are immune: side slopes and tight canyon trails still put rock against the body, especially with the truck loaded and suspension compressed. But the risk profile is different from a Wrangler, and the market reflects it: most side protection options for the Power Wagon are step tubes with some degree of side protection built in, rather than full-coverage rock sliders.

Morryde's Rock Rail Steps ($549) are the closest to a true rock rail in the Power Wagon market. They use heavy-wall tubing, mount solidly to the frame, and provide a functional step without the flimsy flex common in lighter nerf bars. If you're putting this truck in situations where side impacts are likely — off-camber trails, rock gardens — these are the right choice. N-Fab's nerf steps ($499) are a step-down in rock contact capability but offer a clean look and solid factory-style mounting. Westin's HDX Drop Steps ($349) are an honest, no-frills side step that handle light trail use and daily entry/exit without any rock rail pretension.

The AMP Research BedStep2 ($329) solves a different problem — tailgate access to the bed — rather than rocker protection, but it's worth mentioning because it's the most common supplemental step on Power Wagon overland builds where the primary side steps are already covered.

Why it works

  • Improves entry/exit for a tall truck — step height without side steps on a lifted Power Wagon is a legitimate reach
  • Morryde units provide meaningful rock contact protection for moderate trail use
  • Bolt-on install without fabrication
  • Keeps the factory frame mounting points — fully reversible

Trade-offs

  • True heavy-duty rock sliders designed for this truck are limited compared to Wrangler options
  • High ground clearance reduces rocker contact risk but doesn't eliminate it on serious terrain
  • Step-style designs are lower profile protection than full rock rails
  • Heavier nerf bars at this tier (Morryde) still flex more than weld-on Wrangler sliders under hard lateral contact

Tools required

  • socket set
  • torque wrench
  • drill (some kits)
