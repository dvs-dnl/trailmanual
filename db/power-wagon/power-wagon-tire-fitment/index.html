Ram Power Wagon / Wheels & Tires

What Tires Fit a Power Wagon — and What 'Fits' Really Means on a 3/4-Ton Truck

Difficulty 1/51.0–4.0 hrs$1200–35002005-2009, 2010-2018, 2019-present
Short answer

The Power Wagon has run 33-inch Goodyear tires since the 2014 relaunch — 285/70R17 on most trucks; read your door placard for the exact size. True 35s fit with modest work. Anything larger than 35s requires real work, and the truck's weight limits how much tire capability you actually need.

The factory 285/70R17 DuraTrac is a genuine all-terrain tire, not a street tire with aggressive looks. Goodyear's DuraTrac is a capable choice for the factory setup, and many owners never swap it. If you need more grip or a specific sidewall pattern, there's room to go bigger — but understand what you're working with before spending money.

Factory Spec

285/70R17 is approximately 32.7 inches in diameter, typically described as "33 inch" in round numbers. The factory wheel is a 17-inch on the 8x165.1 pattern with a deep positive offset; read the exact width and offset off your wheel before ordering spacers or new wheels. The wheel well has been designed around this size, so fitment is clean with no rubbing.

Fitting 34s and 35s (305/70R17, 315/70R17, 35x12.50R17)

This is the first common upgrade. By the arithmetic (section width x aspect ratio x 2 / 25.4, plus the wheel diameter), a 305/70R17 is about 33.8 inches and a 315/70R17 about 34.4 inches, so neither is a true 35; a 35x12.50R17 is. Any of these on the factory 17" wheel requires attention to offset — the factory wheel's offset positions the tire so a 305/70R17 or 315/70R17 will typically rub the front sway bar link (when the bar is connected) and the inner fender at full lock.

Typical fixes for a 35-inch fitment without a lift:

  • Cycle the sway bar disconnect — the rubbing is largely at the link, not at the fender
  • Trim the front inner fender liner (plastic, not metal — reversible)
  • Use a slightly narrower 305/70R17 rather than a wider 315/70R17

With a 2-inch level, 35s usually clear with little or no trimming; check at full lock and full articulation on your wheels. This is the path most Power Wagon owners take — it's inexpensive and leaves the factory geometry mostly alone.

On 17" wheels: Most common. Plenty of tire selection at 285/70R17, 305/70R17, 315/70R17.

On 18" wheels (2019+ trucks with the optional 18" wheel package): Sizing shifts to 305/70R18 territory. The diameter equivalent stays the same, the fitment conversation is similar.

Fitting 37s — Where It Gets Honest

37-inch tires are possible on a Power Wagon. They are not plug-and-play. The challenges:

Weight: The Power Wagon is a heavy truck. Rotating mass matters at that weight — 37s are heavier tires, and the 2014-and-later truck's 4.10 axle ratio (standard behind the 6.4L HEMI; 2005-2013 trucks ran 4.56 behind the 5.7L) is already on the conservative side for 35s. With 37s and stock gears, expect noticeable performance loss and fuel economy drop.

Geometry: Big tires need the front geometry handled, not only height. AEV's Ram HD lift, for example, repositions the factory radius arms to move the axle forward 1.6" for a 40" tire and corrects the steering with a new drag link and track bar. Carli sells a complete Power Wagon system built for this truck. Either way, read each kit's Power Wagon fitment notes before buying.

Fender clearance: 37s on a stock body require significant trimming or flares. The factory fender openings are sized for 33s with clearance. Getting 37s to clear at full articulation — including through Articulink range — requires either body work or accepting contact marks on the inner liner.

Regearing: To properly support 37s on a 2014+ truck, a regear from 4.10 to 4.56 is the standard answer — see the regear guide for the ratio math. The Power Wagon runs an AAM 9.25-inch front and an AAM 11.5-inch rear (AAM 10.5-inch rear on 2005-2013 trucks), not Dana axles, and setting up an AAM carrier calls for specific procedures and careful bearing preload. This is not a casual weekend job.

The Honest Verdict

For most Power Wagon use — trail runs, overlanding, occasional rock work — 35s on a leveled truck are the right answer. They fit without compromising Articulink, they don't require regearing, and the truck's factory capability is designed around roughly that size. Jumping to 37s means a real suspension system and potentially regearing to match, on a very heavy truck. That money buys a lot of other capability improvements first.

Steps (Tire Swap, 35s on Stock or Leveled Truck)

  1. Choose tire size: 305/70R17 or 315/70R17 for 17" wheels
  2. Confirm wheel offset — stock offset works with 305/70R17; 315/70R17 may need a slight spacer or trim
  3. Check inner fender liner at full lock before final installation
  4. Torque lug nuts in a star pattern. For the number, Ram's 2022 owner's manual lists 130 ft-lb (176 N·m) for the M14x1.5 cone-seat lug nuts (22 mm socket) and 129 ft-lb (175 N·m) for flanged nuts, and says to retighten after 25 miles. Older figures differ (published charts list 140 ft-lb for flanged nuts on some years), so on an earlier truck use your own owner's manual, and on aftermarket wheels use the wheel maker's figure
  5. Recheck torque at 25 miles and 100 miles after installation

Why it works

  • 35s fit with minimal modification on a leveled truck
  • Factory DuraTrac is a legitimately capable tire — don't replace it without a reason
  • 17" wheel sizing has the broadest tire selection

Trade-offs

  • 37s require a real lift system with geometry correction, plus regearing
  • Curb weight limits the traction payoff from going larger — the truck is not light
  • Regearing a 3/4-ton AAM axle is not a quick job

Tools required

  • torque wrench
  • floor jack
  • jack stands
