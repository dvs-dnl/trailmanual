Ram Power Wagon / Suspension

Aftermarket Shocks for Power Wagon (Fox 2.5 / King 2.5 / Bilstein 5160)

Difficulty 3/54–8 hrs$800–32002014-2018, 2019-2024, 2025+
Short answer

The factory Bilsteins on the Power Wagon are good for stock height and light-duty trail. Step up to Bilstein 5160 reservoir shocks for about $900 and you get better fade resistance at desert speeds. Step up to 2.5" reservoir shocks from Fox or King and you change the truck — more control over washboard and rough high-speed terrain. Front shocks on this truck are a plain bolt-in swap: the Power Wagon front is a coil-sprung three-link solid axle with separate Bilstein monotube shocks, not struts.

The factory Power Wagon Bilstein shocks are tuned for stock 33" tires and stock weight. They're fine for daily and mild trail use but they fade fast under desert running or sustained off-road speed. Three upgrade paths:

Bilstein 5160 reservoir shocks ($900 for all four) — the value upgrade. A 46mm monotube with a reservoir for added oil capacity and fade resistance. Bolts in like a factory replacement. Best for moderate trail + occasional desert.

Fox 2.5 reservoir shocks — true 2.5" body, remote reservoir. Fox's Factory Race 2.5 front pair for 0–1.5" of lift lists the 2014–2024 Power Wagon in its fitment, at $1,299.95. Fox lists other 2.5 lines by lift height, so match the part number to your truck's height. Big step up in damping control, especially on washboard and rocky high-speed terrain.

King 2.5 OEM Performance Series — premium tier with serviceable internals. The pick if you wheel hard and want a rebuildable shock; confirm the Power Wagon fitment and lift range on the part you order.

Carli shocks come bundled in Carli's complete Power Wagon systems, valved for their coils. The choice if you're running a Carli lift.

Notes for the install: on older, rust-belt trucks the shock bolts and studs seize. Plan on penetrating oil 24 hours before, and have an impact wrench available.

For all four shock options, do front AND rear together. Mismatched damping (new fronts, tired rears) makes the truck feel weird and accelerates wear on the new shocks.

The install, step by step

All of these are a bolt-in shock swap — no fabrication, and no spring compressor, because the coils and shocks are separate parts on both axles.

  1. Soak the upper and lower shock fasteners in penetrating oil 24 hours ahead on a rusty truck.
  2. With the truck on stands and the wheels off, support the axle with a floor jack at ride height so the shock is neither compressed nor fully extended.
  3. Remove the front shock's lower bolt and upper fastener and pull the shock. Use an impact wrench on a seized fastener rather than a long hand ratchet.
  4. Install the new front shock, mount the reservoir where the hose clears the tire, the coil and the steering at full lock and full droop, and torque to the shock maker's sheet or the factory service manual.
  5. Rear shocks are the same idea: support the axle, unbolt the upper and lower mounts, swap, and torque to spec. Re-check all four after a shakedown drive.
  6. Do front AND rear in the same session. Mixing new fronts with tired rears makes the truck feel disconnected and accelerates wear on the new units.

For a step up to a taller ride height, the shock change pairs with a lift kit — see the pw-lift-aev-dualsport and Carli paths, which spec their own valving.

What to watch for

The biggest mistake is ordering reservoir shocks without checking frame clearance on an already-lifted truck — Fox and King 2.5 remote reservoirs need a clear routing path, and a 3"+ lift changes where the hose lands. Mock up the reservoir bracket before committing.

The second is valving mismatch. A non-Carli shock dropped onto a Carli or AEV spring without the matching tune will ride harsher or wallow depending on the pairing; follow the shock maker's preload and valving recommendation for your exact spring, or expect to chase ride quality afterward. And if a shock bolt does seize past the point of penetrating oil, stop before you snap it; heat on the surrounding bracket (well away from brake and fuel lines) usually wins where brute force loses.

Why it works

  • Bilstein 5160 is a true bolt-in upgrade with a meaningful improvement
  • Fox 2.5 / King 2.5 change the character of the truck for desert and washboard
  • All four options are rebuildable (Fox/King especially — long-term value)
  • Required for any 3"+ lift kit (factory shocks aren't long enough)

Trade-offs

  • Rusty hardware on older trucks is the install bottleneck
  • King 2.5 sits at premium pricing
  • All non-Carli options need Carli's tuning recommendations to match a specific lift; otherwise expect to adjust spring preload
  • Fox/King reservoirs need clearance on the frame — check fitment on lifted trucks before ordering

Tools required

  • floor jack rated to 3500 lb
  • jack stands
  • metric sockets (18mm, 21mm, 22mm)
  • torque wrench (250+ lb-ft capable)
  • PB Blaster
