Ram Power Wagon / Recovery

Recovery Points Install for Power Wagon (Front Frame Mount + Rear D-Rings)

Difficulty 3/52–5 hrs$180–4802014-2018, 2019-2024, 2025+
Short answer

The Power Wagon's front end already has two large factory tow hooks, and Ram's owner's manual says they are for emergency use to rescue a vehicle stranded off-road, recommends using both front hooks for an off-road recovery, warns never to use a chain, and says not to use them for tow-truck hookup or highway towing. The rear has no equivalent, and that is where most trucks need a rated recovery point added.

Safety-critical system. Recovery gear carries loads far past the vehicle's weight. Use rated hardware, read the working load limit, and keep everyone out of the line of pull. Torque values and sequences come from the factory service manual for your year.

The 2014 truck's bumper hides the Warn winch, with its hook clipped to one of two massive front tow hooks. Ram doesn't publish a working load limit for those hooks in the manual section above, so treat them as Ram describes them: an off-road rescue point used in pairs, with a bridle across both, and never a single-hook snatch on one side.

Three ways to set the truck up:

1. Use the factory front hooks as Ram intends — rig a bridle (two rated soft shackles or a bridle strap) across both hooks for any off-road pull of the truck, keep pulls straight and controlled, and never use chains.

2. Front bumper with integrated recovery points — if you've already swapped to an aftermarket winch bumper (see pw-front-bumper), use the rated mounts its maker publishes a WLL for.

3. Rear recovery points — the factory rear has no rated recovery points. Options:

  • A rated receiver-mount recovery point in the 2" receiver (the cheapest and most common fix)
  • An aftermarket rear bumper with integrated rated points (see pw-rear-bumper-tire-carrier), which can also solve the spare-tire problem
  • Bolt-on rear frame-mount points whose fitment line names the Power Wagon

Shackles. Use rated soft shackles or bow shackles with a marked WLL. Don't confuse a soft shackle with a winch-line shackle mount: a Factor 55 FlatLink is a folding shackle bracket for the winch line, not a shackle. WARN's rigging guide covers shackles, snatch blocks and the line damper.

Installing bolt-on recovery points

Follow the maker's printed instructions — they govern over this outline — and treat the torque values and thread-locker as non-negotiable.

  1. Chock the wheels and support the truck on stands where the mounting area is accessible.
  2. Test-fit the bracket against frame steel, not the bumper skin, and confirm every hole lines up before starting a bolt.
  3. Install the supplied grade-8 (or better) hardware; don't substitute old bolts unless the kit says to.
  4. Apply thread-locker where the instructions call for it and torque to the kit's figures.
  5. Re-torque after the first 100 miles. A recovery point that backs off is worse than none because you will trust it.

For the rear, the honest call is whether you are also doing a bumper — the pw-rear-bumper-tire-carrier integrates rated points and solves it in one move; otherwise a receiver-mount point is the cheaper path.

What to watch for

Rig the factory front hooks the way the manual says: both hooks, no chains, no single-hook snatch. Match the mount to the strap — a rated mount with a worn or under-rated shackle is only as strong as its weakest link — and inspect soft shackles for chafing every season. Never rig a recovery off the bed-mount spare cradle or a hitch pin that is not load-rated, and clear bystanders well outside the strap's reach before any kinetic pull. See pw-front-bumper for the integrated-recovery-point alternative if you are already swapping the bumper.

Why it works

  • The factory front hooks are covered for off-road recovery in Ram's manual when used in pairs
  • A receiver-mount point fixes the rear for very little money
  • Soft shackles and rated mounts are a well-documented recovery setup
  • No need to replace the factory front bumper to rig a proper front pull

Trade-offs

  • The rear still needs something — a receiver point, frame points or a rear bumper
  • Ram doesn't give the front hooks a published WLL in the owner's manual, so single-hook snatch pulls are out
  • Soft shackles have a finite life — inspect for chafing and retire them when worn
  • Drilling the rear frame for added recovery points is committing

Tools required

  • metric sockets through 22mm
  • torque wrench (250 lb-ft)
  • drill
  • thread tap (M14x1.5 if drilling)
