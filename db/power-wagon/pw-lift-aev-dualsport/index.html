AEV DualSport 4.5" Lift — Power Wagon (Full Short-Arm, L6)

Difficulty 5/514–28 hrs$5800–92002014-2018, 2019-2024, 2025+

4.5-5" of real lift on a Power Wagon, the proper way: new coils, new long-stroke Fox or AEV shocks, replacement upper control arms (factory UCAs hit the coil at this lift), correction brackets, fully aligned. AEV DualSport is the reference kit. Carli Pintop is the boutique alternative. $6-9k done — this is the kit that lets you run 37s and wheel hard.

The Power Wagon's factory upper control arm is the binding constraint at lifts above 3". Lift the truck higher than ~3.5" and the factory UCA actually contacts the coil spring at full droop. You need:

1. **New coils** sized for the lift height + loaded weight

2. **Replacement upper control arms** with revised geometry that clears the coils and restores ball joint working angle

3. **Long-stroke shocks** (Fox 2.5 or AEV-spec) with reservoirs

4. **Correction brackets** for the lower control arm mounting (if not integrated into the UCAs)

5. **Sway bar drop brackets** (factory sway bar geometry goes wrong)

6. **Pitman + idler arm** considerations for steering geometry

7. **Brake line extensions** (factory lines too short)

**AEV DualSport RT 4.5"** ($6,200 kit) — the reference kit. Includes everything above. Engineered as a system. Mile-for-mile the most popular real lift on Power Wagons. AEV is owned by AAM (the axle maker) so their geometry is dialed-in for the AAM 9.25" front axle.

**Carli Pintop 4"** ($7,400 kit) — the boutique alternative. Slightly different damping philosophy (Fox 2.5 with custom valving), tunable for desert running. The choice if you want adjustable shock valving and don't mind the premium. Both AEV and Carli are excellent — there's no wrong answer between them.

**What this lift does that a 3" lift doesn't:**

  • Clears 37" tires with minimal trim (only the inner fender liner needs trimming)
  • Adds 5-6" of effective ground clearance (vs 3" for the L4 kit)
  • Larger shock travel = better high-speed off-road performance
  • Restores proper UCA angle so ball joints last 50k+ miles

**What it costs beyond the kit itself:**

  • Installation: 14-28 hours DIY; $1,500-2,500 at a shop
  • Alignment: $150 immediate + $150 shakedown at 500 miles
  • Re-gear to 4.56 (if running 37s): $1,200-1,800 (see [[pw-regear-456]])
  • 37" tires: $2,400-3,200 set
  • Wheels or spacers: $300-2,000

**Total realistic build cost for "4.5" lift + 37s + re-gear":** $12-16k. Plan accordingly.

This is the lift for a Power Wagon you actually wheel hard or do extended overland trips with a loaded rig. For occasional weekend trail use on 33-35s, the 3" kit ([[pw-lift-mid-3in]]) is plenty.

Why it works

  • Real 4.5-5" of clearance with proper geometry
  • Clears 37s with minimal fender work
  • Engineered-as-a-system kits — no mixing-and-matching
  • Long-term reliability data from thousands of Power Wagon installs

Trade-offs

  • $6-9k for the kit alone, $12-16k for the full build with tires + re-gear
  • 14-28 hour DIY install — most owners pay a shop
  • Mandatory shakedown alignment at 500 miles (suspension settles)
  • Sway bar drop brackets affect on-road body control noticeably
  • Brake lines need replacement (extended factory lines or aftermarket Russell stainless)

Tools required

  • floor jack rated to 3500 lb
  • jack stands x4
  • spring compressor
  • torque wrench (300+ lb-ft)
  • press for control-arm bushings
  • alignment gear
  • extra hands
