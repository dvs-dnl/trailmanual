Overland Setup — Rooftop Tent and Bed Rack for the Power Wagon

Difficulty 2/53–6 hrs$599–42002014-2018, 2019-2024

Two numbers decide this build and neither one is on the tent's box. The first is the dynamic load rating of your bed rack — what it holds while the truck is moving, typically 150-300 lb on quality systems, versus the 800-1,000 lb static rating manufacturers advertise. The second is the payload on your door-jamb sticker, which on a Power Wagon is roughly 1,400-1,500 lb — the lowest in the Ram 2500 lineup, because the coil rear suspension and off-road hardware eat into it. Size the tent to the rack, then weigh the loaded truck. Everything else here is detail.

Rooftop-tent makers publish a static rating — the weight the tent's structure supports while it is open and the truck is parked, which is why an 800 lb number is plausible for two adults and bedding. That figure tells you nothing about driving.

The rack underneath is rated dynamically: the load it can carry while the truck moves, bounces, and corners. Quality bed racks land at 150-300 lb dynamic. That lower number is the ceiling on tent weight, and it is the spec to shop against.

Hard-shell tents run 120-180 lb. Soft-shell tents run 100-140 lb but pack taller and are slower to deploy. A 165 lb hard shell on a 300 lb dynamic rack is fine. The same tent on a 150 lb rack is not, no matter what the tent's own sticker says.

Do this before you buy anything.

  1. Read the yellow sticker in the driver's door jamb: "The combined weight of occupants and cargo should never exceed XXXX lb." That is your truck's payload, after its specific options.
  2. Subtract every human who rides in it. Two adults is roughly 350 lb.
  3. Subtract the build: bed rack 60-90 lb, hard-shell tent 120-180 lb, awning 60-70 lb, bedding and ladder 25 lb.
  4. Subtract the rest of the trip: water at 8.3 lb per gallon (a 20 gal tank is 166 lb), fuel cans, a fridge at 40-60 lb loaded, recovery gear at 60-100 lb, tools, food.

A representative overland Power Wagon: 350 lb of people + 240 lb of rack and tent + 65 lb awning + 165 lb of water + 90 lb of recovery gear + 60 lb fridge + 100 lb of everything else = 1,070 lb, against a payload near 1,450 lb. It fits, with less margin than most people assume, and it stops fitting the moment you add a second passenger row or a tank of extra fuel.

Then verify it. A CAT scale run costs about $15 and gives you steer axle, drive axle, and gross weights you can check against the GAWR and GVWR on the same door-jamb sticker. Front GAWR on the 2014+ Power Wagon is 6,000 lb; GVWR is around 8,510 lb. Guessing here is how people run a rear axle over its rating without knowing.

Bed rack (recommended). The tent sits over the bed, keeps the load lower, and leaves the cab roof alone. The Decked AR bed rack (~$699) is the common entry point — a rigid aluminum ladder rack that bolts to the bed rail system, usually without drilling, and carries a 300 lb dynamic rating. Front Runner's Slimline II truck bed kit (~$900-1,300) is the modular alternative if you want to hang accessories off the rack itself. The tradeoff is that the rack occupies the bed, which conflicts with drawer systems and long cargo.

Cab-over. Mounts above the cab on brackets or a cab-height crossbar and keeps the bed completely clear. It is the right call if the bed is doing real work. The costs are more visual height, more wind noise, and a more involved mounting system that must not load the cab roof skin.

Height matters. A stock Power Wagon is already around 6 ft 6 in tall. A bed rack plus a closed hard-shell tent commonly puts the truck at 8 ft or more — over most residential garage doors, at the limit of some parking structures, and firmly into "check the clearance bar" territory. Measure the finished truck and write the number on a label inside the cab. People shear tents off on parking garages every season.

Expect a real fuel economy penalty as well: 1-3 mpg is typical on a truck with this frontal area once a tent and awning are up top.

The ARB 2500 (~$299) is the highest value-per-dollar item on this list. It mounts to a bed rack or roof rail in about an hour and deploys in a couple of minutes. On dimensions, be accurate rather than optimistic: the 2500 is a 2.5 m (roughly 8 ft) wall length with about 7 ft of projection, not a square 8.5 x 8.5 ft footprint — check ARB's current spec for your model. In desert country, shade during the middle of the day is a safety item, not a comfort item.

  1. Measure the bed rail width and inside bed length to confirm rack fitment. The 2014+ Power Wagon is crew cab only with the 6'4" bed on a 149.3 in wheelbase, so there is one bed configuration to shop for.
  2. Install the bed rack per the manufacturer's instructions — most systems bolt to the factory stake pockets or bed rail mounts without drilling. Torque to spec and note which fasteners the maker wants re-torqued.
  3. Check the rack is level front-to-back with a spirit level before the tent goes on. An unlevel rack is an unlevel bed at 2 a.m.
  4. Set the tent on the crossbars with a second person. A 165 lb hard shell at chest height is a two-person lift and a common source of dented bed rails.
  5. Mount using the tent's supplied T-slot hardware or channel brackets, torqued to spec. Undertorqued tent hardware vibrates loose on washboard — this is the single most common failure on a truck that actually goes off pavement.
  6. Open the tent and verify the hinge and ladder clear the rear cab glass, the third brake light, and the tailgate through their full arc.
  7. Mount the awning to the driver-side rack rail so it opens away from the bed and does not foul the tent's opening direction.
  8. Full open-and-close rehearsal in the driveway before the first trip, then re-torque everything after the first 100 miles and again after the first washboard day.

If the numbers or the height are more than you want, the cheaper and lighter answer is a bed sleep platform — no height penalty, no dynamic load question, a fraction of the cost. If the rack is going in regardless, plan it alongside a drawer system so they do not fight for the same bed space, and read the heavy-truck weight reality guide before you finalize the load plan. A fridge and camp lighting will want a dual-battery setup so overnight loads never touch the crank battery, and the awning guide covers mounting options in more depth.

Why it works

  • The 6'4" bed is a good rooftop-tent platform and longer than most competitors' crew-cab beds
  • Hard shells deploy and pack in under three minutes — no poles, no stakes, no wet-tent wrestling
  • Bed-rack mounting keeps the sleeping platform lower and more stable than a cab roof
  • The awning integrates with the same rack for a complete camp footprint

Trade-offs

  • Dynamic rack rating (150-300 lb) governs the build, not the tent's static number
  • The Power Wagon has the lowest payload of the Ram 2500 line — roughly 1,400-1,500 lb before anything is added
  • A finished setup commonly exceeds 8 ft tall and clears fewer garages and structures than owners expect
  • Expect 1-3 mpg lost and noticeably more crosswind sensitivity
  • The rack occupies the bed and limits drawer access or long cargo without removing it

Tools required

  • socket set
  • torque wrench
  • tape measure
  • drill (some installations)
  • level
  • ladder or step stool
  • second set of hands
