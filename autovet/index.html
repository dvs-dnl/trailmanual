AutoVet — In development

Know what you're buying
before you buy it.

Most used-vehicle mistakes are predictable. AutoVet walks you through every step — from initial research to a professional inspection — so you don't inherit someone else's problems.

Most buyers look at a car, decide they like it, and then look for reasons to justify the purchase. That's backwards. The right order is: research first, then inspect, then decide.

AutoVet is being built to enforce that order — and to put professional-grade inspection knowledge in the hands of anyone buying a used vehicle, for free.

01
Step 1
Research the vehicle
Before you look at a single listing, know what you're getting into. Common failure points, best and worst model years, what owners actually complain about, and what makes a given rig worth owning long-term.
  • Known issues and failure patterns by year
  • What to look for at first glance
  • Honest verdict on long-term ownership

Step 2
Decode the VIN
Enter the VIN and get back the actual build — factory options, assembly plant, any open recalls, and known TSBs for that specific vehicle. Not just the model — this unit, this configuration.
  • Factory build confirmation vs. listed specs
  • Open NHTSA recalls (federal requirement to disclose, often isn't)
  • Technical Service Bulletins for this exact vehicle

03
Step 3
Get a model-specific inspection checklist
Generic inspection checklists are useless — they tell you to check the brakes on every vehicle regardless of whether that vehicle's known problem is the brakes. AutoVet generates a checklist built around what actually fails on this year, make, and model.
  • Inspection items ranked by failure frequency
  • Category-by-category walkthrough: exterior, engine bay, underbody, road test, docs
  • Red flags that are immediate deal-breakers vs. negotiating points
In development
04
Step 4
Book a vetted local shop for a professional PPI
If you want a professional set of eyes on the car — and for anything above $10K, you should — AutoVet connects you with a certified shop near the seller. Not anonymous contractor dispatch. Vetted, accountable shops who know what they're looking at.
  • Certified technicians, not gig-economy inspectors
  • Photo documentation per checklist item
  • Written report delivered to you by email
