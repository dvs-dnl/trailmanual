Buying a Wrangler 4XE

The market is softening from launch highs. Good examples exist. But the 4XE demands more pre-purchase diligence than almost any other SUV on the used market — the fire recall, the software complexity, and the battery replacement cost change the calculation significantly.

JL Generation·2021–Present·Updated 2026

Who This Is For

The 4XE makes sense for a specific buyer: someone who does a meaningful amount of daily driving (enough to use the EV range regularly) AND wants genuine off-road capability on weekends. The 21-mile electric range covers most commutes. The electric motors deliver real trail capability — especially on a Rubicon with Rock-Trac and locking diffs.

If you're buying a trail-only vehicle, the standard 3.6L or 3.0L diesel Wrangler is a more reliable and less complicated platform. If you're buying a commuter, a different PHEV — one without the 4XE's recall history — is a better choice. The 4XE earns its complexity premium only when you actually use both sides of the powertrain.

Battery Risk

HV battery replacement at $15,000–$30,000 is the single largest used-purchase risk factor. A 4XE priced without reflecting this potential liability is priced optimistically. This is not a hypothetical cost — it's the real figure for out-of-warranty battery replacement.

Trim Breakdown

Sport 4XE

Entry Level

Command-Trac transfer case. Chrysler 8.25" rear axle — weaker than the Dana 44 on upper trims. Cloth interior. Least desirable for off-road use. The weakest axle is the limiting factor on trail.

Sahara 4XE

Mid-Tier

Command-Trac transfer case. Better interior and comfort-focused options. Most available on the used market. No locking diffs. The right choice if trail use is moderate and comfort matters.

Rubicon 4XE

Trail-Focused

Rock-Trac transfer case (4:1 low range). Dana 44 front and rear. Front and rear locking diffs. Most capable and most expensive. Worth the premium if trail use is the actual point of the purchase.

What to Verify Before Purchase

Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before anything else. This list builds on that foundation — every item here requires independent verification, not seller confirmation.

  • Fire recall — verify the LATEST remedy, not just 95B The original 95B software fix was later confirmed ineffective in some cases. Nine fires occurred in vehicles that had already received 95B. Confirm the superseding remedy is in the service record. Ask the dealer to verify via VIN — don't rely on seller confirmation. Non-negotiable.
  • Defroster recall (February 2024) Affected ~139,000 vehicles, 2021–2024. Defrost/defog system fails under certain conditions. Verify completion via NHTSA lookup.
  • OTA software recall Affected ~24,000 2023–2025 models. A Stellantis OTA update bricked the hybrid control system. Verify dealer has reprogrammed if the VIN falls in the affected range.
  • Recall 22V-865 (loss of motive power) Affected ~94,000 vehicles, 2021–2024. Verify completion. Ask seller directly if the vehicle has ever shut off unexpectedly while driving.
  • FORM mode status Ask directly: has this vehicle ever been stuck in FORM mode (Fuel and Oil Refresh Mode)? Post-recall, some vehicles locked into FORM mode permanently, disabling EV capability. A vehicle currently in FORM mode is a red flag.
  • Oil cooler condition Ask for any oil cooler service history. Symptoms: milky coolant, oil traces in overflow tank, coolant smell. The repair is warranty-covered but has recurred after initial fix — ask for documentation.
  • Charge behavior Does the battery charge to 100%? Does it hold charge? Does EV range approach the rated ~21 miles? Any significant deviation warrants investigation.
  • 12V auxiliary battery condition Ask when it was last replaced. A recently replaced 12V on a relatively new vehicle may indicate the HV management system is stressing it abnormally.
  • OBD-II scan Request a scan tool readout or dealer diagnostic printout. Any active HV-related DTCs are a serious concern and warrant a full dealer diagnostic visit before proceeding.
  • Interior rear floor inspection Inspect the rear cargo area carefully. Water staining, dampness, or rust blooms on the battery cover panel indicate water intrusion reaching the battery tray area — a significant concern on a PHEV.
  • Differential fluids Check color and consistency. Gray or milky = water intrusion. Common after water crossings without proper servicing. Discolored fluid means the diff needs a drain and refill at minimum.
  • Transfer case operation Engage 4-Hi and 4-Lo. Both should engage cleanly. On a Rubicon, verify Rock-Trac shifts into the 4:1 low range. Any grinding, delay, or failure to engage is a flag.

Watch List

  • Fire recall not resolved with the latest remedy Walk away. This is not a negotiating point — it's a minimum condition. The original 95B fix being present is not sufficient.
  • Active HV warning lights or DTCs Do not buy without a full dealer diagnostic visit and written documentation of what's wrong and what it costs to fix.
  • HV battery replaced out of warranty Understand who paid and why. A battery replacement before 60,000 miles suggests a significant failure event. Get the full service documentation.
  • Missing service history on a PHEV PHEVs can go longer between oil changes because the ICE doesn't run every trip — but when it runs, the oil needs to be in good condition. Verify service intervals matched actual usage. A PHEV driven primarily on electricity with infrequent ICE runs still needs oil changes.
  • Salt-belt origin Vehicles from the Northeast, Midwest, or mountain states with heavy winter road treatment carry meaningfully higher battery tray and frame rust risk. Get it on a lift before committing.

Verdict

Bottom Line

The Rubicon 4XE is the right trim if trail capability is the point. A clean 2022 or 2023 example with all recalls resolved and documented service history is a viable purchase — but it requires more due diligence than almost any other vehicle in this price range. The battery replacement cost creates an asymmetric downside risk that makes thorough pre-purchase inspection more important, not less. Do not skip the NHTSA lookup, the OBD scan, or the dealer TSB check.
