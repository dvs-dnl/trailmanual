Short answer

Pinery Canyon Road, FR 42, climbs about 12 miles from SR 181 near Chiricahua National Monument to Onion Saddle, then runs east through Cave Creek Canyon to Portal or south on FR 42D to Rustler Park. The Forest Service strongly recommends high clearance; two guides report cars making the dry-weather drive. AZ Off-Road rates it 2 of 10.

Status unknown as of 2026-10-30 — source. The Forest Service page (updated 2026-04-15) gives no open status; it says the western portion, from East Turkey Creek Road to the forest boundary, can close for snow in winter. The Coronado alerts page the same day lists no order that closes FR 42. The Barfoot Snake Closure Order (April 1 to November 30 each year through 2028) closes two talus slopes near Barfoot Peak and Buena Vista Peak to entry, off the road. Confirm with Coronado National Forest — Douglas Ranger District at 520-364-3468 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

The sources split on vehicles. The Forest Service page says high clearance is strongly recommended and 4WD is also beneficial, and the Douglas Ranger District's Cave Creek Canyon guide calls FR 42 narrow, rugged and switchbacked and recommends high clearance. AZ Off-Road says high clearance is not required and calls the drive wide and smooth in most spots, and Trails Offroad says most vehicles can complete it. This record follows the land manager: a high-clearance vehicle is the working requirement, with 4WD helpful in wet or winter conditions, while the guides show cars making the drive in dry weather. No source names a tire size, lockers or skid plates; 28 inches is a conservative reading for a stock high-clearance vehicle, not a figure from a source. Vehicles over 41 feet are not allowed on FR 42.

The trail

Pinery Canyon Road, FR 42, leaves SR 181 near Chiricahua National Monument and climbs a broad canyon where grassland and riparian trees give way to oak and juniper and then mixed conifer. The Forest Service puts Onion Saddle 12 miles up the road at about 7,600 feet, with views west over the Sulphur Springs Valley toward the Dos Cabezas, Swisshelms and Dragoon mountains. AZ Off-Road finds the road wide and smooth through the lower canyon, then a fast climb with several switchbacks over the last four miles before the saddle. The district's Cave Creek Canyon guide calls FR 42 narrow and rugged and limits vehicles to 41 feet.

At Onion Saddle the choices are to continue east on FR 42, through the Paradise junction and Cave Creek Canyon to Portal, about 12 miles with pavement starting in the canyon; to climb FR 42D to Rustler Park and Barfoot Park; or to turn around. Pinery Canyon Campground sits north of the road about a mile from the saddle, and a short trail from the road at the saddle leads to an overlook.

The sources split on vehicles. The Forest Service strongly recommends high clearance and says 4WD is beneficial. AZ Off-Road says high clearance is not required, and Trails Offroad says most vehicles can complete it, with shallow low-water crossings in monsoon season. The western portion, from East Turkey Creek Road to the forest boundary, can close for snow in winter. This is Coronado National Forest, Douglas Ranger District; call 520-364-3468 for current conditions.

Named obstacles

  • Switchbacks below Onion Saddle — AZ Off-Road says the road narrows after mile 5.5, then climbs rapidly after mile 7.2 with several switchbacks over about 4 miles to the saddle at mile 12.0. The Cave Creek Canyon guide calls FR 42 a narrow, rugged dirt road with many switchbacks.
  • Onion Saddle — Junction of FR 42, running east to Cave Creek Canyon, and FR 42D, running south to Rustler Park. The Forest Service says a short trail from the road at the saddle leads to a panoramic overlook.
  • FR 42D to Rustler Park — AZ Off-Road describes this spur as narrower and still climbing, ending above 8,200 feet at a junction with a sign warning of bears. From there it is about a mile to Rustler Park Campground and about a mile to Barfoot Park.
  • Low-water crossings — Trails Offroad describes multiple shallow crossings in monsoon season that vehicles with moderate clearance usually cross.
  • Vehicle length limit — The Cave Creek Canyon guide limits vehicles to 41 feet in length on FR 42.

Trail Manual1–41 — Mild

AZ Off-Road rates the drive 2 of 10, which maps to Mild, and Trails Offroad says most vehicles can complete it. The Forest Service gives no number but strongly recommends high clearance, and its district guide calls the road narrow and rugged with many switchbacks. Those describe clearance, switchbacks and weather rather than rock obstacles, which fits Mild (a stock 4x4 with clearance), so the land manager's clearance wording is carried into the requirements instead.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Steep grades

Before:

  • Shift to 4LO before the grade, not on it.

After:

  • Check transmission fluid level and smell after a long descent in gear.

Snow and ice

Before:

  • Check the land manager's seasonal closure before you leave the pavement.

After:

  • Rinse the salt and cinders off the brake lines and frame.

Water crossings

Before:

  • Walk it first. Know where your intake and axle breathers sit.

After:

  • Check differential and transfer-case fluid for a milky look; dry the brakes before the pavement.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Coronado National Forest — Douglas Ranger District · 520-364-3468
  • Route: FR 42 (Pinery Canyon Road, Scenic Drive #42), with FR 42D from Onion Saddle up to Rustler Park. FR 42 continues east through Cave Creek Canyon to Portal. The Douglas Ranger District Motor Vehicle Use Map is the legal reference.. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Motor Vehicle Use Map: MVUM
  • Pass / permit: None.
  • Length: 23 mi — The Douglas Ranger District's Cave Creek Canyon guide gives 23 miles one way between the Cave Creek visitor center and SR 181. The Forest Service page puts Onion Saddle 12 miles up Pinery Canyon from the Chiricahua National Monument turnoff. AZ Off-Road measures 12.0 miles to Onion Saddle and 14 miles to the end of FR 42D at Rustler Park, then about 12 miles from the saddle east to the paved Portal road.
  • Season: The Forest Service says the western portion, from East Turkey Creek Road to the forest boundary, can close for snow in winter. The Cave Creek Canyon guide lists the season as year-round but says FR 42 and 42B close for snow, floods or fire danger. AZ Off-Road lists spring through fall as the best time and says the road can be impassable for much of the winter. Trails Offroad notes multiple shallow low-water crossings in monsoon season. Call the Douglas Ranger District at 520-364-3468 for current conditions.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
