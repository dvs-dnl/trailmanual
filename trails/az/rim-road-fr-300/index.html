Short answer

Rim Road (FR 300) is a graded gravel road of about 43 miles along the Mogollon Rim between State Route 87 and State Route 260, listed as open on the Coconino road-status page. The Forest Service calls its forest roads there suitable for passenger vehicles, guides rate it 1 or 2 of 10, and it carries logging and recreation traffic and closes in winter.

Open as of 2026-10-30 — source. The Mogollon Rim Ranger District table (page last updated 2026-10-22) lists FR 300 (Rim Road) as Open. The trail

Rim Road, Forest Road 300, follows the edge of the Mogollon Rim for about 43 miles between State Route 87 near Clints Well and State Route 260 near Woods Canyon Lake. The Coconino road-status page lists FR 300 as open, and the Forest Service describes it as a popular, well-traveled road that visitors use for views, camping and hiking. A Forest Service handout for the Rim Road and General Crook Trail loop calls the forest roads gravelled and suitable for passenger vehicles and says they close in winter. Arizona Highways lists no vehicle requirements, AZ Off-Road rates the road 2 of 10 and says nearly any car can make it, and onX rates it 1 of 10 and calls it a good route for beginners and two-wheel-drive vehicles.

The road runs through the Coconino National Forest for roughly its first 25 to 28 miles, then enters the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests, which is why Black Mesa Ranger District appears on the east end. AZ Off-Road puts the edge of the Rim at 5.5 miles, with overlooks and campsites along the zigzag that follows, a historical marker for the 1872 Battle of Big Dry Wash at the FR 705 junction near mile 12, and a road that grows more washboardy toward Woods Canyon Lake. Arizona Highways names Potato Lake, Kehl Springs Campground, Barbershop Canyon and Bear Canyon Lake as side trips, and the Dude Fire burn zone between about mile 7.5 and mile 10.2. Kehl Springs Campground, 6 miles east of the SR 87 end, is open early May to late October.

Expect company. AZ Off-Road rates traffic heavy, and in March 2025 the Forest Service asked drivers to pull over for log trucks on FR 300, 141 and 147. The whole road is above 7,000 feet, so snow can close it. The General Crook Trail beside the road is not a driving route: the Apache-Sitgreaves says motorized use is prohibited on it unless designated and signed. Call the Mogollon Rim Ranger District at 928-477-2255 for current conditions.

Named obstacles

  • Logging traffic — The Forest Service said in March 2025 that log trucks and heavy machinery were using FR 300, 141 and 147, asked drivers to pull over for oncoming log trucks, and asked for extra care on blind curves and steep slopes.
  • Heavy recreation traffic and blind curves — AZ Off-Road rates traffic on the road as heavy, and onX says there are blind curves with two-way traffic where drivers should slow down.
  • Washboard toward Woods Canyon Lake — AZ Off-Road says the road gets progressively more washboardy and busier past the turn onto FR 34 toward Woods Canyon Lake.
  • Snow and winter closure — AZ Off-Road says the road is entirely above 7,000 feet, where snow is likely and the road can become harder or close; the Coconino handout says forest roads there are closed in winter.

All sit in the 1 to 3 band that maps to Mild (1). Traffic, washboard and winter snow add care but not obstacles.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Washboard

Guide: Death wobble diagnostic

Before:

  • Grab the track bar and tie-rod ends and try to move them. Any play, fix it first.

After:

  • Re-torque the track bar bolts and check tie-rod ends and ball joints; washboard loosens all three. The death wobble diagnostic covers the sequence.

Shared and one-way traffic

Before:

  • Commercial tours and the uphill rig have the right of way. GMRS or CB helps at the one-way sections.

Snow and ice

Before:

  • Check the land manager's seasonal closure before you leave the pavement.

After:

  • Rinse the salt and cinders off the brake lines and frame.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Coconino National Forest — Mogollon Rim Ranger District (the east end lies in the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests, Black Mesa Ranger District) · 928-477-2255
  • Route: FR 300 (Rim Road), State Route 87 to State Route 260. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Pass / permit: None.
  • Length: 43.3 mi — 43.3 miles one way from SR 87 to SR 260 per Arizona Highways and 43.4 per onX; AZ Off-Road counts 40 miles to where the pavement starts, then about 3 paved miles to SR 260. onX lists the road east of SR 260 as a separate route, not covered here.
  • Season: AZ Off-Road lists the road as open spring through fall and closed in winter, and says it sits entirely above 7,000 feet, where snow is likely. The Coconino handout for the Rim Road and General Crook Trail loop says forest roads are closed in winter, and an onX reporter in March 2025 found a posted notice closing the road until May. Kehl Springs Campground on FR 300 lists early May to late October as its season. The Coconino road-status page (updated October 22, 2026) lists FR 300 as Open. In March 2025 the Forest Service reported log truck traffic on FR 300, 141 and 147.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
