Short answer

The Boulders OHV Staging Area, about 45 miles north of Phoenix off SR 74, is the gateway to more than 200 miles of unimproved roads and single-track in the Lake Pleasant and Hieroglyphic Mountains area. Guides describe a milder southern half and a steeper, rockier northern half, and BLM lists dispersed camping, a vault toilet and an October through May best season.

Status unknown as of 2026-10-30 — source. Confirm with Hassayampa Field Office at 602-867-5400 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

The trail

BLM runs the Boulders staging area from its Hassayampa Field Office. The turnoff is on the north side of SR 74 between mile markers 11 and 12, unmarked and easily overlooked, and the staging area sits one mile north. Facilities are a vault toilet and an information kiosk, with more than 10 acres for dispersed camping and no water, tables or shade. The area covers elevations from 1,700 to 3,500 feet, and BLM puts the best season at October through May.

AZ Off-Road describes the network in two halves. The southern loops, about 4 miles around the staging area, use routes A, B, C and D and LP 5, 6 and 8, and are mostly mild. The northern half uses LP 9, 10 and 11 and the north part of LP 5 for about 13 miles, with steeper hills, tight turns and several very rocky wash sections, and takes four hours or more. FunTreks rates its 15.5-mile Hieroglyphic Mountains Loop from the same staging area as Difficult: extremely steep and very narrow in places, with sandy wash bottoms in the second half and a trail network complex enough to get lost in. FunTreks says short-wheelbase 4x4s manage it and advises against stock SUVs and oversized vehicles.

Travel is limited to existing numbered roads, and AZ Off-Road notes closures on high-pollution advisory days. The trails are also used by ATVs, UTVs and dirt bikes, so expect mixed traffic. Unlicensed vehicles need an Arizona OHV decal under BLM's Hassayampa rules.

Named obstacles

  • Unmarked turnoff from SR 74 — On the north side of the highway between mile markers 11 and 12; BLM warns the turnoff can be overlooked. The staging area is one mile north.
  • Southern loops (routes A, B, C, D and LP 5, 6, 8) — About 4 miles around the staging area; AZ Off-Road describes them as mostly mild.
  • Northern half (LP 9, 10, 11 and the north part of LP 5) — Steeper hills, tight turns and several very rocky wash sections, about 13 miles (AZ Off-Road).
  • Hieroglyphic Mountains Loop — 15.5 miles from the staging area. FunTreks says extremely steep and very narrow in places, with wide sandy wash bottoms in the second half and a network complex enough to get lost in.
  • Training areas — Arizona State Parks lists a training lot at the staging area, and FunTreks mentions a kids' practice area.

No single number fits a network. Arizona State Parks says the area suits beginners to expert riders, AZ Off-Road splits it into a mostly mild south and a steeper, rockier north, and FunTreks rates the 15.5-mile Hieroglyphic Mountains Loop Difficult. BLM gives no rating. Moderate (2) sits between the mild southern loops and the Difficult loop, and the description says which end each route is at.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Hassayampa Field Office · 602-867-5400
  • Route: No single route number. AZ Off-Road names routes A, B, C, D and LP 5, 6, 8, 9, 10 and 11. BLM's page lists the staging area and more than 200 miles of roads and single-track but no route numbers.. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: blm.gov
  • Pass / permit: BLM's page lists the staging area as free and names no route permit. AZ Off-Road says the area is mostly BLM with some State Trust land, that a State Trust Land permit is required, and that vehicles must be licensed or carry an Arizona OHV decal. BLM's Hassayampa page requires a valid OHV decal for OHVs on public and state trust lands. The Tonto National Forest OHV permit covers four zones on national forest land elsewhere and does not apply here.
  • Length: 200 mi — BLM counts more than 200 miles of unimproved roads and single-track in the area. Guides describe loops of about 4 miles (south), 13 miles (north) and 15.5 miles (Hieroglyphic Mountains Loop).
  • Season: BLM says the best season is October through May. AZ Off-Road lists fall through spring and notes that closures can occur on high-pollution advisory days because the area is in Maricopa County. FunTreks lists spring or fall.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
