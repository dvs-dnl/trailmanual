Short answer

Senator Highway is the 37-mile Forest Service road between Prescott and Crown King, opened in 1866 as a toll road for the Bradshaw Mountains mines. Guides rate it fairly mild, with rocky sections, erosion cuts and a Pine Creek water section, but they disagree on four-wheel drive: Arizona Highways requires it, AZ Off-Road says stock SUVs with clearance can make it.

The trail

From Prescott, Mount Vernon Avenue becomes Senator Highway, Forest Road 56, and stays paved for the first several miles. Arizona Highways splits the 37 miles at the Forest Road 52 junction: 12.5 miles on FR 56, then 24.5 on FR 52 to Crown King. Pavement ends past Groom Creek, after which the road is narrow and rutted in places. The Prescott National Forest describes the drive as popular with the Jeep and OHV crowds, passing historic Palace Station before it switchbacks up to Crown King.

AZ Off-Road logs the road from the Crown King end. The Horsethief Trail junction is at 0.6 miles and the Backway to Crown King junction at 1.8, a cattle guard sits at 6.4, Palace Station at 21 (fenced, so look from outside), and the Senator Mine at 27.3. Pavement returns around mile 30 with about 7 more miles into Prescott. Obstacles are rocky sections, erosion cuts and washouts, water on the Pine Creek section, and burned ground that is still changing. Tight sections mean watching for oncoming vehicles and sometimes backing up.

The guides split on vehicle needs. Arizona Highways calls for a high-clearance four-wheel-drive vehicle and says conditions have worsened since the article ran. AZ Off-Road says most stock SUVs with good clearance can make the trip and four-wheel drive is generally needed only in bad weather. Do not drive it wet, expect snow in winter and carry water.

Named obstacles

  • Rocky sections, erosion cuts and washouts — AZ Off-Road lists these as the main surface problems, with the road narrow and twisty in places.
  • Pine Creek section — Water crosses the road on the Pine Creek section (AZ Off-Road).
  • Palace Station — Stagecoach stop built in 1875, about 21 miles from the Crown King end. It is fenced; look from outside the fence.
  • Senator Mine — Mine ruins near Maxton, about 27 miles from the Crown King end.
  • Tight sections — Watch for oncoming vehicles; backing up may be needed.
  • Fire-damaged ground — AZ Off-Road says the fire recovery area is still changing, so expect conditions to differ from older reports.

Where the sources differ on four-wheel drive we rate on the stricter reading: Moderate (2), with four-high usually enough in dry weather.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Water crossings

Before:

  • Walk it first. Know where your intake and axle breathers sit.

After:

  • Check differential and transfer-case fluid for a milky look; dry the brakes before the pavement.

Snow and ice

Before:

  • Check the land manager's seasonal closure before you leave the pavement.

After:

  • Rinse the salt and cinders off the brake lines and frame.

Steep grades

Before:

  • Shift to 4LO before the grade, not on it.

After:

  • Check transmission fluid level and smell after a long descent in gear.
