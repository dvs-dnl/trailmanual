Short answer

Redington Road (FR 371) is the 24-mile main road over Redington Pass from east Tucson to Cascabel Road in the San Pedro Valley. The first miles to the Tanque Verde Falls trailheads suit most passenger vehicles, and the rest is dirt with washboard, ruts and a muddy water crossing in the east valley. onX rates it 3 of 10, and the Forest Service allows dispersed camping beyond mile 4.6.

Status unknown as of 2026-10-30 — source. The Redington Road #371 page (updated 2026-04-15) describes the drive and gives no open or closed status. The Tanque Verde Falls Recreation Area page lists Site Open with year-round use and covers the first miles of the road. The Coronado NF alerts page lists no order closing Redington Road to vehicles; it lists the Tanque Verde Falls camping closure effective 2026-04-30. An onX report from 2026-06-08 marks the road temporarily closed with no detail, and a report from 2026-10-02 marks it open. Confirm with Coronado National Forest — Santa Catalina Ranger District at 520-749-8700 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

The Forest Service says the short dirt section up to the Tanque Verde Falls trailhead parking is bumpy but suitable for most passenger vehicles. Past that, onX says most of the western side can be driven in a low-clearance 2WD with caution, recommends airing down for a full run, and wants a high-clearance 4WD at the east-valley crossing when it holds water. Trails Offroad reviewers report a RAV4, a Bronco and a Gladiator on it, and one reviewer in July 2024 would not have taken a passenger car up the washed-out switchbacks at that time. onX reviewers in May and June 2026 describe a recently graded road that any vehicle that is not lowered could drive. No source gives a tire size, skid plate or wheelbase requirement; 27 inches is a conservative reading for a stock vehicle, not a figure from a source.

The trail

Redington Road starts as Tanque Verde Road in east Tucson and becomes Forest Road 371 as it climbs a pass between the Santa Catalina and Rincon Mountains. The Forest Service describes switchbacks through saguaros and ocotillos, then a plateau of high grassland with views of Tanque Verde Canyon, Saguaro National Park and Tucson behind you, and the Galiuro Mountains and San Pedro River valley ahead.

The first stretch is the gentlest. The Forest Service says the short dirt section up to the Tanque Verde Falls trailheads is bumpy but suitable for most passenger vehicles, and it lists trailer parking at the Middle Tanque Verde staging area, at Three Feathers a little past mile 7 and at Alambre at mile 10. From Middle Tanque Verde the road runs 24 miles to Cascabel Road. Reviewers report washboard and ruts on the Tucson side, a rough stretch between roughly mile 4.5 and mile 10, and a smoother road to the east. onX names the soft, muddy water crossing in the east valley as the hardest spot and recommends airing down for a full run.

The Forest Service allows dispersed camping beyond mile 4.6, while camping is prohibited at the Tanque Verde Falls parking areas and within 650 feet of the road in that area, under a Forest Order effective April 30, 2026 to April 29, 2029. Many forest roads branch off Redington Road. The Chimney Rock and Chiva Falls routes have their own records, and the Forest Service points drivers to the Santa Catalina Motor Vehicle Use Map for which branches are open. Call the Santa Catalina Ranger District at 520-749-8700 for conditions.

Named obstacles

  • Tucson-side climb and switchbacks — The Forest Service describes switchbacks climbing through saguaros and ocotillos to a plateau. Trails Offroad reviewers report blind, tight switchback turns, heavy washboard on the Tucson side, and in July 2024 washouts on the switchbacks.
  • Rough stretch before the plateau — A Trails Offroad reviewer in May 2023 found the road quite rough between about mile 4.5 and mile 10 and smoother beyond mile 10, and several onX reviewers say the first half heading east is roughest.
  • East-valley water crossing — onX names the water crossing in the east valley as the most difficult part of the pass: soft and muddy, and a job for a high-clearance 4WD when it holds water.
  • Staging areas and branch roads — The Forest Service lists trailer parking at Middle Tanque Verde, at Three Feathers a little past mile 7 and at Alambre at mile 10, and says numerous forest roads branch off Redington Road.
  • Weekend and night traffic — onX calls it a very popular two-lane road, and a Trails Offroad reviewer in October 2025 reported cars, trucks and side-by-sides running until late at night.

onX rates the pass 3 of 10, which maps to Mild, and the Forest Service describes the lower road as suitable for most passenger vehicles without rating the rest. Recent reports describe ruts, washboard and washouts rather than rock obstacles, and onX reviewers in May and June 2026 describe a recently graded road. The rougher side roads off Redington Road, such as Chimney Rock, have their own records and rate higher.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Washboard

Guide: Death wobble diagnostic

Before:

  • Grab the track bar and tie-rod ends and try to move them. Any play, fix it first.

After:

  • Re-torque the track bar bolts and check tie-rod ends and ball joints; washboard loosens all three. The death wobble diagnostic covers the sequence.

Water crossings

Before:

  • Walk it first. Know where your intake and axle breathers sit.

After:

  • Check differential and transfer-case fluid for a milky look; dry the brakes before the pavement.

Shared and one-way traffic

Before:

  • Commercial tours and the uphill rig have the right of way. GMRS or CB helps at the one-way sections.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Coronado National Forest — Santa Catalina Ranger District · 520-749-8700
  • Route: FR 371 (Redington Road #371), as titled on the Forest Service Redington Road #371 Scenic Drive page. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Motor Vehicle Use Map: MVUM
  • Pass / permit: None.
  • Length: 24 mi — 24 miles from the Middle Tanque Verde staging area to Cascabel Road per the Forest Service; its drive page counts about 12 miles to Redington; onX lists 24.1 miles and Trails Offroad 27.64 miles for the whole pass
  • Season: The Forest Service lists the Tanque Verde Falls area on Redington Road as year-round use, and Trails Offroad lists the pass as typically open all year. onX lists all four seasons. Reviewers report washboard on the Tucson side after monsoon and heavy weekend traffic, and onX says the east-valley water crossing turns soft and muddy when it holds water.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
  • Water crossings: 1
