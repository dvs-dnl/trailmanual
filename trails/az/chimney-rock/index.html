Short answer

Chimney Rock is a 10-mile run of Forest Service roads through rocky washes in the Redington Pass area east of the Catalinas. A gentler south entrance exists, but the full route has deep washouts, boulder crawls, close brush and about half a dozen gates, and sources call for skid plates and a capable driver.

The trail

Chimney Rock is a chain of Forest Service roads in the eastern foothills of the Santa Catalina Mountains, reached from Redington Road, which Arizona State Parks lists as the only access to the Redington Pass area. Trails Offroad gives the road numbers as FR 4430, 4438, 4431, 36 and 371, and describes a lesser-known series known for its scenery and geology, running west to east through high desert oak scrub and woodland with waterholes along the way.

FunTreks lists 10.2 miles and about three hours. It describes two ways in: a gentler entrance from the south side, and a less-used back way that is more demanding. Most of the route follows a rocky wash. Brush crowds the wash enough that Trails Offroad warns of pinstripes, and the washouts get deep in places. Trails Offroad lists deep V washouts, rocky climbs and boulder crawls, and onX reviewers add steep climbs near the middle and about half a dozen gates, some of them closed. After heavy rain the dry washes can fill with deep water, and an October 2026 onX reviewer found the stretch past Chimney Rock harder after the monsoon.

Part of the route can be driven in a two-wheel-drive vehicle with good clearance, but the full route asks for more. Trails Offroad says to count on rock rails, skid plates and capable driving, and FunTreks says aggressive stock vehicles with skid plates can finish but may get scratched. Navigation is confusing enough that FunTreks and onX both recommend a GPS or offline map.

Arizona State Parks says motorized travel in this area is limited to designated routes. The Forest Service pages found do not list these roads by number, so check the Motor Vehicle Use Map for your roads and call the Santa Catalina Ranger District at 520-749-8700 for current conditions.

Named obstacles

  • Rocky wash with tight brush — Both FunTreks and Trails Offroad describe a rocky wash with brush close enough to scratch paint.
  • Deep V washouts and boulder crawls — Trails Offroad lists deep washes, rocky climbs, deep V washouts and boulder crawls on the full route.
  • Steep climbs near the middle — An onX reviewer reports rock ledges, sandy wash crossings and steep climbs near the middle of the route.
  • Gates — An onX reviewer counts about half a dozen gates, and a February 2025 report found two closed. The Forest Service asks drivers to leave gates as they find them.
  • Water after heavy rain — FunTreks and onX both warn that heavy rain can create deep water in the washes.
  • Route finding — FunTreks and onX recommend a GPS or offline map because navigation can be confusing.

FunTreks rates it Moderate, which maps to 2, while onX gives a 6 of 10, which maps to 3. The guides split, so the land-manager and gear wording decides it. Arizona State Parks calls the secondary roads in the Redington Pass area very rugged and strongly recommends a high-clearance 4x4, and Trails Offroad says the full route calls for rock rails and skid plates. Reviewers in 2026 report a stock Wrangler finishing it, which is why the record says capable stock rigs only rather than built rigs only.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Rock garden

Guide: Lift & tire fitment — the clearance you can measure

Before:

  • A spotter beats a locker. Pick the line on foot.

After:

  • Sight down the tie rod and drag link for a bend; check toe by eye against a straight edge.

Ledges and step-ups

Guide: Lift & tire fitment — the clearance you can measure

Before:

  • Know your lowest point before you need to: oil pan, transfer-case skid, rear diff.
  • Air down. Low-20s psi puts more tread on the step.

After:

  • Look under every skid plate for fresh scrapes and rolled edges.
  • Check rocker panels and sliders; check the rear diff cover for dents.

Tight brush

Before:

  • Fold the mirrors. Tape the paint edges if you care about them.

After:

  • Pull branches out of the suspension and check the brake lines and breather hoses.

Steep grades

Before:

  • Shift to 4LO before the grade, not on it.

After:

  • Check transmission fluid level and smell after a long descent in gear.

The Forest Service Redington Road #371 page and the Arizona State Parks Redington Pass Backcountry Touring Area entry do not list the Chimney Rock roads individually.. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Motor Vehicle Use Map: MVUM
  • Pass / permit: None.
  • Length: 10.2 mi — 10.2 miles per FunTreks and onX; FunTreks describes a gentler entry from the south side and a less-used, more demanding back way
  • Season: Trails Offroad lists the route as typically open all year, and FunTreks and onX name spring and fall as the best seasons. Heavy rain can turn the dry washes into deep water crossings, and an onX reviewer in October 2026 blamed the recent monsoon for a harder stretch past Chimney Rock.
