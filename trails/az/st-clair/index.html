Short answer

St. Clair is a Cave Creek Ranger District permit zone of about 65 miles of routes, best known for the one-way Telegraph Line Trail, MT-42, which the Forest Service says suits modified full-size 4WD vehicles with a lift, oversize tires and a locker. Horseshoe Dam Road, where MT-42 exits, is closed to vehicles until November 2, 2026.

Restricted as of 2026-10-30 — source. The Forest Service closed Horseshoe Dam Road (NFSR 205) to motor vehicles from 2026-09-29 to 2026-11-02 at 08:00 unless rescinded. The Telegraph Line Trail exit, the Kentuck staging area and the Corral access are on that road, and the Corral trailhead is also listed temporarily closed. Zone page (updated 2026-08-21) otherwise lists the Telegraph Line East, Sunrise Ridge, Indian Spring and Kentuck sites and Camp Creek Wash access as open. A Tonto NF OHV Permit is required. Confirm with Tonto National Forest — Cave Creek Ranger District at 480-595-3300 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

For MT-42 the Forest Service says modified full-size 4WD vehicles with a lift, oversize tires and at least one axle locking device, and its map note adds high clearance, extended suspension travel and tall tires and says it is not recommended for inexperienced drivers. No source gives a tire size or lift height, so 33 inches and 2 inches are conservative readings of that wording. Other routes in the zone are milder: onX scores two St. Clair loops 3 of 10. The handout says road conditions are primitive in some areas and require four-wheel drive.

The trail

St. Clair is a 24,500-acre zone on the Cave Creek Ranger District with roughly 65 miles of motorized routes. The area was closed after the 2005 Bart Fire, and it reopened after volunteers and Forest Service crews rebuilt more than 45 miles of trail between 2022 and 2024. Its best-known route is the Telegraph Line Trail, MT-42, which the Forest Service calls high difficulty and best suited to modified full-size 4WD vehicles with a lift, oversize tires and at least one axle locking device. The map note adds that it is not recommended for inexperienced drivers or for vehicles without high clearance, extended suspension travel, tall tires and differential lockers.

MT-42 runs one way, east to west. You enter from North Lake Road about two miles northeast of Bartlett Dam Road, past a gate keeper obstacle that shows what the trail is like, and leave on Horseshoe Dam Road about 6.5 miles north of Bartlett Dam Road. The trail is too narrow for passing. Other routes in the zone include MT-1117 at Sunrise Ridge, MT-1104 at Corral, MT-532 to the Devil's Hole day-use site, and Camp Creek Wash, MT-413.

Check the closure first. Horseshoe Dam Road, NFSR 205, is closed to motor vehicles for flood-damage repairs from September 29, 2026 to November 2, 2026, unless the Forest Service lifts the order early. The MT-42 exit and the Kentuck and Corral accesses are on that road. Camping is off limits at staging areas and Devil's Hole is day use only. A Tonto NF OHV Permit is required.

Named obstacles

  • Telegraph Line Trail (MT-42) — One way, east to west, from North Lake Road to Horseshoe Dam Road. The Forest Service calls it highly technical and too narrow for passing or turning around.
  • Gate keeper obstacle — Placed right before the MT-42 entrance to show the trail's typical conditions. Limited parking at the entrance; do not block the trail.
  • Horseshoe Dam Road closure — NFSR 205 is closed to motor vehicles from 2026-09-29 to 2026-11-02, repair work after flood damage. The MT-42 exit, Kentuck staging and Corral access are on that road.
  • Camp Creek Wash (MT-413) — A popular wash access reached from Bartlett Dam Road about 2.75 miles east of the Cave Creek district office.
  • Devil's Hole (end of MT-532) — A primitive day-use site on the Verde River. The Forest Service bars camping there because water levels change quickly.

The Forest Service wording for MT-42 (highly technical, modified rig with lift, tall tires and a locker) outranks the guides and puts the zone's headline route at Difficult (3). The zone's other routes are milder, and onX scores two St. Clair loops 3 of 10, which is Mild. The rating follows the trail the zone is known for.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

Guide: Tire pressure & air-down

  • Air down further than you think. Carry a board or traction mat to jack against.

After:

  • Pull the air filter and look. Check brake dust shields and rotors for packed sand.

Water crossings

Before:

  • Walk it first. Know where your intake and axle breathers sit.

After:

  • Check differential and transfer-case fluid for a milky look; dry the brakes before the pavement.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Tonto National Forest — Cave Creek Ranger District · 480-595-3300
  • Route: St. Clair OHV Permit Zone; Telegraph Line Trail MT-42; routes marked open by brown markers include MT-1104, 1117, 1058, 1065, 1069, 2063, 2068, 2069, 2070, 2073, 2076, 42 and 532; Camp Creek Wash is MT-413. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Pass / permit: Tonto NF OHV Permit, sold only on Recreation.gov (3-day $10, 6-month $60, annual $80); not sold at district offices. One permit covers all four permit zones (Bulldog Canyon, The Rolls, St. Clair, Desert Vista). Every motorized vehicle in a zone needs at least one rider holding a permit in their driver-license name; vehicles parked at staging areas do not. Qualifying off-highway vehicles also need an Arizona OHV decal. Required since 2025-01-01.
  • Length: 65 mi — about 65 miles of motorized routes in a 24,500-acre zone per the Forest Service; no source here gives a verified length for MT-42 itself
  • Season: The Forest Service handout lists the season of use as all year. Watch for flash flooding in the washes. Check the closure on Horseshoe Dam Road before planning a Telegraph Line run, since that is where the trail exits.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
