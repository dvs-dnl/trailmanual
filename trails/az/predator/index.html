Short answer

Predator is a short rock wash of about three-quarters of a mile in the Table Mesa Recreation Area north of Phoenix, with a water crossing, a V-notch and off-camber sections. A 2020 event write-up calls strong rockers essential and says the trail is known for body damage. BLM's pages do not list it by name, so its status is unconfirmed.

Status unknown as of 2026-10-30 — source. BLM's OHV designation data lists the Table Mesa area as Limited to designated roads and trails under a signed travel management plan (decision June 2010). BLM's public route data identifies routes by number or TV label only, and Predator could not be matched to one. BLM's Little Pan Staging Area page covers the recreation area but does not name this route. Confirm with Phoenix District, Hassayampa Field Office (Table Mesa Recreation Area) at 602-867-5400 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

The trail

Predator is a rock wash in the Table Mesa Recreation Area, described in a March 2020 write-up of a Jeep Jamboree at Bumble Bee Ranch as about three-quarters of a mile long. The group drove Table Mesa Road to the Little Pan staging area, aired down there, and spent several hours on the trail. It opens with a water crossing that rain had changed since the author's January visit, then a small run-off and a wash where the rocks zig and then zag. A rock formation needs a hard turn to avoid see-sawing or rolling over, and a hard-left step-up follows. Off-camber sections include one hugging a large rock. Chunky rocks cost one driver a sidewall, and loose boulders can high-center a differential. The V-notch, a solid wall with rock sticking out on one side and a cluster on the other, is called the trail favorite.

The write-up calls strong rockers essential and stresses articulation, tire placement and spotters. It says the trail has claimed taillights and fenders in the past and is known for paint damage.

An enthusiast route page says Predator is not one of the TV routes BLM labels, runs in the same wash as Annihilator and ends where Annihilator begins. BLM's pages and public route data do not name Predator or Annihilator, and we could not confirm either is open. The BLM Hassayampa Field Office manages the land under a signed travel plan that limits vehicles to designated roads and trails, so check the kiosk maps and the field office before you drive it.

Named obstacles

  • Water crossing near the start — Present in the 2020 write-up and changed by rain since the author's January visit.
  • Zig-zag rock wash — After a small run-off the wash turns in a zig, then a zag, of rocks.
  • Hard-turn rock formation — A formation that needs a hard turn to avoid see-sawing or rolling the rig.
  • Hard-left step-up — A step-up to the left. It was shorter than usual on the report day because sand had collected.
  • Off-camber sections — Include one that hugs a large rock and a dip that tilts the rig to the right.
  • Chunky rocks — One driver in the group suffered a sidewall-slicing blowout here. Loose boulders can high-center a rig on a differential.
  • V-notch — A solid wall with rock sticking out on one side and a cluster of rock on the other. The write-up calls it the trail favorite and a test of skill.

No numeric rating for Predator turned up in the sources read. A 2020 event write-up describes obstacles from mild to spotter-required, a V-notch and a record of body damage, and an enthusiast page says many drivers who can run Predator cannot run Annihilator, which 4x4 Trail Info lists with club ratings of 5.0 and 4.5. That places Predator below the Extreme tier, so Difficult (3). It is a judgment from wording alone and should be revisited when a numeric rating is found.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Water crossings

Before:

  • Walk it first. Know where your intake and axle breathers sit.

After:

  • Check differential and transfer-case fluid for a milky look; dry the brakes before the pavement.

Rock garden

Guide: Lift & tire fitment — the clearance you can measure

Before:

  • A spotter beats a locker. Pick the line on foot.

After:

  • Sight down the tie rod and drag link for a bend; check toe by eye against a straight edge.

Ledges and step-ups

Guide: Lift & tire fitment — the clearance you can measure

Before:

  • Know your lowest point before you need to: oil pan, transfer-case skid, rear diff.
  • Air down. Low-20s psi puts more tread on the step.

After:

  • Look under every skid plate for fresh scrapes and rolled edges.
  • Check rocker panels and sliders; check the rear diff cover for dents.

Off-camber

Before:

  • Load low and strapped. Fuel and water on the floor, not the roof.

After:

  • Check that the roof rack and recovery gear mounts did not shift.
