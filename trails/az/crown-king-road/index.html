Short answer

Crown King Road is the graded way up to Crown King from I-17, running 26 miles through Bumble Bee and Cleator on a former railroad grade. Passenger cars drive it, though washboard, blind corners, single-lane bridges and heavy traffic slow the climb to about 5,800 feet.

AZ Offroad says high clearance is not needed and passenger cars make the trip often. onX calls it rough and mostly unmaintained, while reports from 2025 and 2026 range from well maintained to really rough. Expect washboard and slow the pace on the narrow upper miles.

The trail

A 2025 onX report calls this the only maintained road to Crown King and mentions passing a UPS truck on it. From I-17 exit 248, pavement lasts 1.9 miles, then the road turns to wide dirt with washboard. AZ Offroad crosses a single-lane bridge over Bumble Bee Creek at mile 4 and passes through Bumble Bee a mile later.

The road crosses into the Prescott National Forest beyond the Golden Turkey Mine, tops out briefly, and reaches Cleator at about mile 14, with a general store and bar. West of Cleator it roughly follows the bed of the Bradshaw Mountain Railroad, which operated from the 1890s to 1926. Road cuts and switchbacks mark the old grade. The DeSoto Mine Trail leaves on the right at mile 15.2.

The last miles climb in switchbacks and narrow to one lane, per onX and AZ Offroad. Traffic is the main hazard, and AZ Offroad says to expect frequent stops to let others by. Reports on the surface vary from well maintained to really rough, depending on when it was last graded.

From Crown King, the Senator Highway continues to Prescott, and other routes lead to Horsethief Lake and the Back Way.

Named obstacles

  • Washboard — AZ Offroad and onX both report washboard for long stretches.
  • Single-lane bridges — AZ Offroad notes single-lane bridges over Bumble Bee Creek and past the Golden Turkey Mine.
  • Switchbacks and blind corners — onX notes several sharp switchbacks and blind corners, and says the last several miles before Crown King are one vehicle wide.
  • Heavy traffic — AZ Offroad calls it a very high-traffic road and says to expect to stop often to let others pass.

AZ Offroad rates it 1 and onX rates it 2, both of which map to Mild. Neither names an obstacle beyond washboard, blind corners and narrow upper miles. The Forest Service gives no rating on the pages read, so Mild (1) stands.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Washboard

Guide: Death wobble diagnostic

Before:

  • Grab the track bar and tie-rod ends and try to move them. Any play, fix it first.

After:

  • Re-torque the track bar bolts and check tie-rod ends and ball joints; washboard loosens all three. The death wobble diagnostic covers the sequence.

Shared and one-way traffic

Before:

  • Commercial tours and the uphill rig have the right of way. GMRS or CB helps at the one-way sections.

Shelf road

Before:

  • Brakes: firm pedal, full reservoir, pads with life in them. Downhill traffic yields.

After:

  • Feel the rotors for heat checking; smell for cooked pads.
