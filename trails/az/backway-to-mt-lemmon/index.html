Short answer

The Backway to Mt. Lemmon is Forest Road 38, the Control Road, a gravel climb of about 28 miles from Oracle through desert, oak and pine to the top of the mountain. The lower half is wide and graded, the upper half is rocky and narrow with drop-offs, and the Forest Service gates it every year from December 15 to February 28.

Open as of 2026-10-30 — source. The Control Road #38 page lists site status Open (page updated 2026-02-27) and the annual gate closure from December 15 to February 28, which has not started. The Coronado NF alerts page checked the same day lists no order titled for Control Road or FR 38; the Bear Wallow Riparian Area order covers land beside FR 833, the General Hitchcock Highway. Confirm with Coronado National Forest — Santa Catalina Ranger District at 520-749-8700 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

The trail

The Control Road is the dirt route up the northeast side of Mt. Lemmon, and drivers call it the backway because the paved Catalina Highway carries most of the traffic from Tucson. From Oracle you follow Old Mt. Lemmon Road to the forest boundary, where it becomes Forest Road 38. The climb starts near 4,300 feet in desert scrub and saguaro and ends near 7,900 feet, passing juniper and oak and then ponderosa pine.

The first half is wide gravel and dirt, and the road climbs gradually. The last six miles are different: FunTreks says the road steepens and twists, and the Forest Service describes the whole road as rough and winding. Expect rocks, narrow spots with drop-offs, little room to pass, and washouts after monsoon and winter damage. The slopes carry scars from several fires, and onX reviewers mention trees across the road in the burn area. Dust is heavy when it is dry, and the road turns slick and muddy in snow or monsoon rain.

Most drivers finish at the top and come back down the paved Catalina Highway. The Forest Service also describes starting from the top: from Tucson, take the Catalina Highway to milepost 24.5, beyond the Loma Linda Picnic Area, and turn northeast onto Forest Road 38.

Plan around the gate. The Forest Service closes the road every year from December 15 to February 28, and Arizona State Parks lists the same winter closure for the higher sections. Check the Coronado National Forest alerts page and call the Santa Catalina Ranger District at 520-749-8700 before you go.

Named obstacles

  • Upper road above the fork — The Forest Service and Arizona State Parks both call it a rough, winding road. FunTreks says the upper section has rocks, narrow spots, drop-offs and little room to pass, and onX reports rock gardens near the upper fork, sharp rocks, washouts and small ledges.
  • Last six miles to the top — The climb is gradual until the final six miles, where FunTreks says the road steepens and twists.
  • Washouts and erosion — Trails Offroad reviewers report washed-out sections near the top and more exposed rock after monsoon and winter damage. One 2023 report describes a new obstacle where a 4Runner driver used 4Lo.
  • Other traffic — Trails Offroad says side-by-sides, UTVs, motorcycles and passenger vehicles share the road, and some of them travel fast.
  • Burn area — onX reviewers mention trees in the road within the fire-scarred stretch.

The Forest Service and Arizona State Parks give no number but call the road rough and winding, and reviewers describe the upper half as rocky and narrow, with one 4Runner driver using 4Lo on a newer obstacle. That wording fits the top of Mild: the lower half is graded gravel and the last miles ask more of a stock SUV.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

Steep grades

Before:

  • Shift to 4LO before the grade, not on it.

After:

  • Check transmission fluid level and smell after a long descent in gear.

Shelf road

Before:

  • Brakes: firm pedal, full reservoir, pads with life in them. Downhill traffic yields.

After:

  • Feel the rotors for heat checking; smell for cooked pads.

Snow and ice

Before:

  • Check the land manager's seasonal closure before you leave the pavement.

After:

  • Rinse the salt and cinders off the brake lines and frame.

Shared and one-way traffic

Before:

  • Commercial tours and the uphill rig have the right of way. GMRS or CB helps at the one-way sections.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Coronado National Forest — Santa Catalina Ranger District · 520-749-8700
  • Route: FR 38 (Control Road, also called Old Mt. Lemmon Road or Old Mt. Lemmon Highway). Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Motor Vehicle Use Map: MVUM
  • Pass / permit: None.
  • Length: 28.2 mi — about 28 miles one way from Oracle to the top of Mt. Lemmon; sources list 28.1 to 29.7 miles, and the paved Catalina Highway return is extra
  • Season: The Forest Service closes the Control Road gate every year from December 15 to February 28, and the road can be snow-covered and impassable in winter. Trails Offroad says snow commonly closes the gate around mile 22 in winter. A FunTreks reviewer who drove through an open gate in January 2025 was met by a sheriff's deputy at the top and given a warning. Dust is heavy when the road is dry, and onX says the upper sections turn slick and muddy in winter snow or summer monsoon rain.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
