Short answer

Charouleau Gap is FR 736, a 4WD-only road that crosses Samaniego Ridge and follows the Canada del Oro wash for about 19 miles between the Catalina side and Oracle. Both guides rate it Difficult and report boulders, an off-camber ledge called The Step and deep wash crossings, with FunTreks estimating seven hours.

Status unknown as of 2026-10-30 — source. The Forest Service page for FR 736 (updated 2025-12-29) gives year-round use and 4WD only but no site-specific open or closed status, and lists no site alerts. The Forest Service says the road is for 4-wheel-drive vehicles only and calls for good ground clearance and excellent traction. onX recommends a high-clearance 4WD or capable side-by-side with at least 33-inch tires, lockers and skid plates, and FunTreks says it is not suitable for stock SUVs. onX reviewers in 2024 to 2026 report a stock 2WD Tacoma turning back after 2 to 3 miles, a stock Rubicon going 2.7 miles with a spotter, a group needing a winch and recovery gear, and one obstacle with no bypass. Both guides advise traveling in a group. No source states 4LO as a requirement; it is set to true because the Forest Service limits the road to 4WD and the climbs are steep and loose.

The trail

Charouleau Gap Road, Forest Road 736, is the Forest Service's own name for this route, and its page is blunt about it: 4-wheel-drive vehicles only, a rough and rocky dirt road that needs good ground clearance and excellent traction. To reach it, drive north from Tucson on AZ 89 about 23 miles to Golder Ranch Road, turn east about a mile to Lago del Oro Road, then go north about two miles to the dirt road and bear left at the private property sign. The road crosses an ephemeral riverbed and climbs over Samaniego Ridge to Charouleau Gap at about 5,400 feet. The page lists Samaniego Ridge Trail #7 and Canada del Oro Trail #4 for hikers and horseback riders.

FunTreks and onX both describe the full run as about 19 miles between the Catalina side and Oracle, following the Canada del Oro wash. FunTreks estimates seven hours; onX reviewers report 9 to 11 hours with groups. The named obstacles are The Step, an off-camber ledge with an optional bypass, Kiss Rock, and several steep loose climbs with deep ruts and tight shelf road. The wash can run heavily after monsoon storms or snowmelt, and the crossings get deep. Fire scars from the Bighorn Fire line parts of the route, and reviewers say the trail has deteriorated.

Recent onX reviews show the range of outcomes: a stock Rubicon covered 2.7 miles with a spotter, a group of seven rigs took about nine hours, and another group needed a winch and recovery gear. FunTreks calls the route long and remote with difficult route-finding and advises traveling in a group.

Named obstacles

  • The Step — FunTreks calls it the most difficult obstacle, on an optional side loop. onX describes a large off-camber ledge with an optional bypass.
  • Kiss Rock — Named by reviewers on both FunTreks and onX. An onX reviewer also describes an unbypassable off-camber loose-rock climb with a high rollover chance and turned around.
  • Samaniego Ridge climb — The Forest Service says the road crosses an ephemeral riverbed and climbs over Samaniego Ridge to Charouleau Gap, and calls it rough and rocky.
  • Canada del Oro wash crossings — FunTreks and onX both warn of deep water crossings when the wash flows.
  • Steep, loose climbs and deep ruts — FunTreks reports steep sections with loose rock and erosion, and onX adds deep ruts and tight shelf roads.
  • Burn scars and stumps — FunTreks reviewers mention stumps and burn areas from the Bighorn Fire, and a trail more deteriorated than in past years.

FunTreks rates it Difficult and onX gives it a 7 of 10; both map to 3. The Forest Service and Arizona State Parks give no number but limit it to 4WD and require good clearance and excellent traction, which agrees. FunTreks does not say modified rigs only, the test for 4, but it says the route is not suitable for stock SUVs, and onX reviewers report winch use and an obstacle with no bypass. That places it at the top of 3, and the requirements describe a built rig.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Ledges and step-ups

Guide: Lift & tire fitment — the clearance you can measure

Before:

  • Know your lowest point before you need to: oil pan, transfer-case skid, rear diff.
  • Air down. Low-20s psi puts more tread on the step.

After:

  • Look under every skid plate for fresh scrapes and rolled edges.
  • Check rocker panels and sliders; check the rear diff cover for dents.

Rock garden

Guide: Lift & tire fitment — the clearance you can measure

Before:

  • A spotter beats a locker. Pick the line on foot.

After:

  • Sight down the tie rod and drag link for a bend; check toe by eye against a straight edge.

Off-camber

Before:

  • Load low and strapped. Fuel and water on the floor, not the roof.

After:

  • Check that the roof rack and recovery gear mounts did not shift.

Steep grades

Before:

  • Shift to 4LO before the grade, not on it.

After:

  • Check transmission fluid level and smell after a long descent in gear.

Water crossings

Before:

  • Walk it first. Know where your intake and axle breathers sit.

After:

  • Check differential and transfer-case fluid for a milky look; dry the brakes before the pavement.

Shelf road

Before:

  • Brakes: firm pedal, full reservoir, pads with life in them. Downhill traffic yields.

After:

  • Feel the rotors for heat checking; smell for cooked pads.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Coronado National Forest — Santa Catalina Ranger District · 520-749-8700
  • Route: FR 736 (Charouleau Gap Road #736). Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Motor Vehicle Use Map: MVUM
  • Pass / permit: None.
  • Length: 18.7 mi — FunTreks lists 18.7 miles and onX 19 miles for the full run between the Catalina side and Oracle; the Forest Service page gives no length
  • Season: The Forest Service lists FR 736 for year-round use. FunTreks and onX name spring and fall as the best seasons, and both say the Canada del Oro wash can run heavily after monsoon storms or snowmelt, which deepens the crossings.
  • Cell coverage: spotty
