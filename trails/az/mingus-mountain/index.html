Short answer

Mingus Mountain is a 17 to 19 mile route from Mingus Avenue at Cottonwood up FR 493 and FR 413 to Highway 89A near the Mingus summit, a gravel climb of more than 3,000 feet that Arizona State Parks lists as a bypass around Jerome. FunTreks rates it Moderate, with a narrow shelf road and no room to pass. Route status is unconfirmed: one guide reports a gated section of FR 413, so check with the Verde Ranger District first.

Status unknown as of 2026-10-30 — source. The Verde Ranger District page covers the Mingus Mountain area but lists only non-motorized trails there, and says the last mile of FR 493 toward the Blowout Wash trailhead is graded dirt. It does not list the Cottonwood-to-summit OHV route or give road status. Arizona State Parks lists the route without a status. The Prescott NF alerts page shows no FR 413 or FR 493 closure. Confirm with Prescott National Forest — Verde Ranger District at 928-567-4121 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

The trail

The Mingus Mountain route starts on the edge of Cottonwood, where Mingus Avenue turns into Forest Road 493 at a cattle guard, and climbs the Prescott National Forest side of the mountain on a narrow gravel road that gains more than 3,000 feet. Arizona State Parks lists it as an OHV route and describes it as a bypass to Highway 89A through Jerome. Guides measure it at 17 to 19 miles to the highway near the summit and give 3 to 4 hours.

FunTreks rates the drive Moderate and says much of the road is mild, with several steep, narrow and rocky spots. The one to plan for is a narrow shelf road with no room to pass. AZ Offroad adds many tight turns, switchbacks, dry washes and a few rocky sections, and a 2023 FunTreks update calls the road barely wide enough for a Wrangler or midsize truck. The route passes the Copper Chief Mine, which FunTreks says is closed to the public. Summer and fall are the best months. Snow makes the road much harder, and FunTreks does not recommend it in winter.

Status needs a call. The Verde Ranger District page lists only non-motorized trails at Mingus Mountain and does not list this route, and the alerts page shows no closure. Trails Offroad says a section of FR 413 toward Jerome has been gated and closed, and maps may still show it open. Carry the Prescott National Forest motor vehicle use map for the East Zone and call the Verde Ranger District at 928-567-4121 before you drive it.

Named obstacles

  • Narrow shelf road — FunTreks reports a narrow shelf road with no room to pass after its second waypoint, and AZ Offroad lists narrow shelf roads and many tight turns.
  • Copper Chief Mine area — The junction of FR 413 and FR 493 sits near the mine. FunTreks says the mine itself is closed to the public, and AZ Offroad asks drivers to obey the posted signs around it.
  • Rocky sections — AZ Offroad says several rocky sections call for high clearance and four-wheel drive; FunTreks describes steep, narrow and rocky spots.
  • Weather-dependent surface — A FunTreks reviewer reports mud and ledges with drop-offs after rain, and AZ Offroad says snow makes the road much harder.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Shelf road

Before:

  • Brakes: firm pedal, full reservoir, pads with life in them. Downhill traffic yields.

After:

  • Feel the rotors for heat checking; smell for cooked pads.

Steep grades

Before:

  • Shift to 4LO before the grade, not on it.

After:

  • Check transmission fluid level and smell after a long descent in gear.

Snow and ice

Before:

  • Check the land manager's seasonal closure before you leave the pavement.

After:

  • Rinse the salt and cinders off the brake lines and frame.
