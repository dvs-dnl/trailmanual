Short answer

FR 203 follows Cherry Creek beside the Sierra Ancha Wilderness between stretches of State Route 288. onX rates it 3, but its write-up warns of a shelf undercut to about UTV width that is unsafe for heavier rigs, and reports describe deep washouts. It rewards small, capable rigs only.

The Forest Service recommends a high clearance 4x4 on FR 203. onX rates it 3 and says a stock 4x4 can pick lines, but then says the undercut shelf section is only as wide as a large UTV and is unsafe for heavier SUVs and pickups, and that it could give way. Field reports call it very gnarly in a long-bed Tacoma and barely wide enough for a four-door Jeep. Treat this as a road for a short, narrow, capable rig with an experienced driver, and scout on foot. A Grand Cherokee Trailhawk and a four-door Jeep did get through in 2025, which is why this reads capable-stock-only and not built-only, but neither driver called it comfortable. The tire and skid plate entries are conservative calls; no source states them.

The trail

FR 203 runs along Cherry Creek beside the Sierra Ancha Wilderness. The Forest Service gives directions from State Route 288 and recommends a high clearance 4x4 to reach the Raccoon Trailhead, 10 miles up the road and 6 more on FR 203A. Wilderness trails that start from this road are closed to motor vehicles. Arizona Highways says the south end features tall saguaros, big sycamores along the creek and a view of Roosevelt Lake.

The condition of the road is the story. Coming from the north, onX describes descending switchbacks with bowling ball-sized rocks and washouts that require weaving through displaced rock. Several miles down, a section it says was always skinny and tippy is now undercut to the width of a large UTV, and the undercut shelf could give way suddenly. The write-up says this stretch is unsafe for heavier SUVs and pickup 4x4s.

Field reports agree. In February 2025, one driver found a washout almost 10 feet deep and slid 18 degrees sideways on a shelf in a four-door Jeep. In March 2025, a Grand Cherokee driver saw track barely wider than the wheels. A May 2024 Tacoma driver said he would not bring a long wheelbase again.

Fire closures in 2025 covered this road, so call Payson first.

Named obstacles

  • Undercut shelf road — onX says a section several miles down to about the halfway point is now undercut and only the width of a large UTV, could give way suddenly, and is unsafe for heavier SUVs and pickups. A 2025 report had a Jeep sliding 18 degrees sideways on a shelf there.
  • Deep washouts — A February 2025 report describes washouts, one almost 10 feet deep and very steep.
  • Loose rock on switchbacks — onX calls bowling ball-sized rocks the main challenge on the descending switchbacks.
  • Creek crossings — Reports describe several flowing creeks and degraded drainage crossings.

onX rates it only 3 but its own text says the undercut shelf is unsafe for heavier SUVs and pickups. Reports describe a washout almost 10 feet deep and a Jeep sliding toward the drop. The land manager recommends a high clearance 4x4. The hazard outranks the number, so Difficult (3) is the conservative call.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Shelf road

Before:

  • Brakes: firm pedal, full reservoir, pads with life in them. Downhill traffic yields.

After:

  • Feel the rotors for heat checking; smell for cooked pads.

Off-camber

Before:

  • Load low and strapped. Fuel and water on the floor, not the roof.

After:

  • Check that the roof rack and recovery gear mounts did not shift.

Rock garden

Guide: Lift & tire fitment — the clearance you can measure

Before:

  • A spotter beats a locker. Pick the line on foot.

After:

  • Sight down the tie rod and drag link for a bend; check toe by eye against a straight edge.

Water crossings

Before:

  • Walk it first. Know where your intake and axle breathers sit.

After:

  • Check differential and transfer-case fluid for a milky look; dry the brakes before the pavement.

Steep grades

Before:

  • Shift to 4LO before the grade, not on it.

After:

  • Check transmission fluid level and smell after a long descent in gear.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Tonto National Forest — Pleasant Valley and Tonto Basin Ranger Districts · 928-474-7900
  • Route: Forest Road 203, as named by the Forest Service; onX and Arizona Highways call it Cherry Creek Road.. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Pass / permit: None.
  • Length: 11.2 mi — 11.2 miles per onX from SR 288 in the north. Arizona Highways gives 17.2 miles on FR 203 from FR 202 to SR 288 in the south.
  • Season: Reports from early 2024 and 2025 describe rain making sand and rocks loose with water running. A Forest Service trail page warns Cherry Creek may be impossible to cross at high water.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
  • Water crossings: 3
