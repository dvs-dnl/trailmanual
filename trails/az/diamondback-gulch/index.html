Short answer

Diamondback Gulch, also called Greasy Spoon, is a 6-mile Forest Service loop of FR 152A and FR 9513 in the Red Rock Ranger District, rated Moderate by onX and suited to most high-clearance 4x4s by the land manager. A steep, rocky pipe line and a deep gulch with a 4-low climb are the hard parts. On October 22, 2026 the Forest Service status page listed both roads as closed.

Closed as of 2026-10-30 — source. Red Rock Ranger District table (page last updated 2026-10-22) lists FR 152A & FR 9513 (Diamondback Gulch OHV) as CLOSED; the page says a Closed road is closed to motorized vehicles unless noted. No reason is given on the page. Confirm with Coconino National Forest — Red Rock Ranger District at 928-203-2900 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

The trail

Diamondback Gulch is a 6-mile loop of Forest Road 152A and FR 9513 in the Red Rock Ranger District. Guides use two names for it: Trails Offroad titles its page Diamondback Gulch aka Greasy Spoon, and onX lists it as Diamondback Gulch (Greasy Spoon), after the old corral at Greasy Spoon Tank. The Forest Service and Arizona State Parks call it a popular route suitable for most high-clearance 4x4s, with two staging areas.

The hard parts are specific. State Parks names a steep, rocky pipe line on the west end, FR 9513. onX says the route gets harder after the corral, with steep rocky hills, ruts up to 18 inches deep, and a steep descent into a deep gulch. It says a high-clearance 4WD with 4-low capability is required. Trails Offroad reviewers agree: one used 4-low from about waypoint 8, most call it Moderate for stock SUVs with good tires, and one says an armored stock vehicle can finish with the right driver. The north section, FR 152A, turns very slick in rain and may be impassable, so pick a dry day. onX lists views of Doe Mountain and the Cockscomb.

Check status before you go. On October 30, 2026 the Forest Roads Status page, last updated October 22, listed FR 152A and FR 9513 as closed to motor vehicles and gave no reason. Trails Offroad shows user reports of the route open earlier in 2026, but those are not Forest Service notices. State Parks lists no camping on the route. Call the Red Rock Ranger District at 928-203-2900 for current conditions.

Named obstacles

  • Steep, rocky pipe line — The Forest Service and State Parks single out a steep, rocky pipe line on the west end, FR 9513.
  • Deep gulch descent and climb — onX describes a steep descent into a deep gulch and says 4-low capability is required on the route; a Trails Offroad reviewer used 4-low from about waypoint 8.
  • Slick north section — FR 152A turns slick in rain and may be impassable, per State Parks.
  • Ruts — onX reports ruts up to 18 inches deep along the route.
  • Greasy Spoon Tank corral — A historic corral that onX uses as the landmark where the route gets harder.

The deep gulch with its 4-low climb is the hardest part, and no source places the route above Moderate.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Coconino National Forest — Red Rock Ranger District · 928-203-2900
  • Route: FR 152A / FR 9513 (Diamondback Gulch OHV); also called Greasy Spoon. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Pass / permit: None.
  • Length: 6 mi — 6-mile loop per the Forest Service and Arizona State Parks. onX measures 6.1 miles point to point and Trails Offroad 6.24 miles, so the numbers depend on how the staging areas and connecting roads are counted.
  • Season: Arizona State Parks says the north section, FR 152A, can be very slick and may be impassable when it rains, and onX says dry conditions are essential and the route is impassable when wet. Trails Offroad lists the route as open year-round. On 2026-10-22 the Forest Service status page listed FR 152A and FR 9513 as closed to motor vehicles and gave no reason.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
