Short answer

FR 52 runs about 6 miles southeast of Crown King into Horsethief Basin, a CCC-built recreation area with a small lake, camping and a 1934 lookout. The Forest Service recommends high clearance on this unimproved dirt road, and it turns slick and muddy after monsoon rain.

Status unknown as of 2026-10-30 — source. The Forest Service recommends a high-clearance vehicle for all visitors to Horsethief Basin and calls FR 52 an unimproved dirt road. A Trails Offroad reviewer called it a 1 after grading. Plan for high clearance, and carry 4WD for wet conditions. No source gives a tire size.

The trail

Horsethief Basin sits southeast of Crown King. From town, FR 259A runs south half a mile to FR 52, then the road continues about 6 miles to the recreation area, according to the Forest Service. Trails Offroad measures the trip at 10.19 miles out and back and lists the location as Crown King.

The Forest Service calls FR 52 an unimproved dirt road and, until further notice, recommends a high-clearance vehicle for anyone visiting the basin. Trails Offroad reviewers split by weather. One called it a 1 after grading. Another, driving in monsoon season, found narrow shelf road and a slick, muddy rock garden.

The road has a story. Trails Offroad says the basin was a hideout for horse thieves in the late 1800s, that the Civilian Conservation Corps built the road, a dam and campgrounds, and that Horsethief Lookout went up in 1934. A July 2025 reviewer found the road open only up to a gate before the lookout, so expect that the last stretch may be shut.

Horsethief Lake is not stocked but holds bluegill, bass and catfish, under a fish consumption advisory for bass. Castle Creek Wilderness lies nearby. There is no potable water at the recreation area.

Named obstacles

  • Narrow shelf road — Trails Offroad mentions narrow shelf road on the way in.
  • Rock garden when wet — A monsoon-season review on Trails Offroad found a muddy, slick rock garden after rain.
  • Gate before the lookout — A July 2025 Trails Offroad review said the road was open only up to the gate before Horsethief Lookout.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Shelf road

Before:

  • Brakes: firm pedal, full reservoir, pads with life in them. Downhill traffic yields.

After:

  • Feel the rotors for heat checking; smell for cooked pads.

Mud

Before:

  • Recovery points front and rear, rated, with a strap you can reach.

After:

  • Wash the undercarriage and radiator fins; mud holds heat and moisture against steel.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Prescott National Forest — Bradshaw Ranger District · 928-443-8000
  • Route: FR 52, reached from Crown King on FR 259A, per the Forest Service campground directions; Trails Offroad lists the official trail name as 52.. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Motor Vehicle Use Map: MVUM
  • Pass / permit: None.
  • Length: 10.2 mi — 10.19 miles out and back per Trails Offroad. The Forest Service gives about 6 miles from FR 259A to the Horsethief Basin Recreation Area, one way.
  • Season: The Forest Service says day use sites and trails are open year-round but overnight accommodations are not available in winter. Turney Gulch Group Campground runs May 1 to October 31. Trails Offroad reviewers found it mild when dry and muddy and slick in the monsoon.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
