Soldier Pass

Sedona — Red Rock Country · nearest town Sedona, AZ

Short answer

Soldier Pass is a short, permit-only rock road above Sedona, listed by the Forest Service at 2.5 miles round trip, with ledges, slickrock steps and a narrow line to the Seven Sacred Pools and Devil's Kitchen. It needs a Recreation.gov permit, with the Forest Service stating at least 72 hours ahead, and the road is open 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. only. Guides call for very high ground clearance and rate it Difficult.

Open as of 2026-10-30 — source. Red Rock Ranger District table (page last updated 2026-10-22) lists FR 9904 (Soldier Pass Road) as Open, by permit only. FR 9904B is not listed on the page. Confirm with Coconino National Forest — Red Rock Ranger District at 928-203-2900 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

Recreation.gov requires a high-clearance 4x4 vehicle. The Forest Service calls the road suitable for most high-clearance 4x4s, while FunTreks and onX say stock SUVs need very high ground clearance and onX recommends low range. Trails Offroad reviewers report a stock Rubicon finishing it, a Gladiator Rubicon and a Wrangler Sahara scraping, and say an AWD vehicle likely cannot manage the waypoint 4 ledge. The road is narrow, brush reaches the paint, and no trailer parking is available. The trail

Soldier Pass is Forest Road 9904, a roughly one-mile rock road that climbs out of Sedona's residential streets toward Seven Sacred Pools and the Devil's Kitchen sinkhole. The Forest Service lists the route at 2.5 miles round trip, calls it scenic with challenging rock ledges and obstacles, and says it suits most high-clearance 4x4s. FunTreks and onX are blunter: stock SUVs need very high ground clearance, and both rate the road Difficult.

The obstacles are ledges and slickrock steps on a narrow road shared with hikers and mountain bikers. Trails Offroad reviewers report scraping at the entrance and say a washed-out ledge at waypoint 4 now rates harder than the rest, though a stock Rubicon got through without trouble. The Devil's Kitchen spur adds a steep rock climb, and brush reaches the paint in tight spots. Reviewers mostly finish in about an hour.

The permit is the part to plan around. The Soldier Pass Motorized Use Permit on Recreation.gov is limited to 12 a day, one vehicle per permit, with a $6 non-refundable fee, and it covers road access only, not parking at the trailhead. The Forest Service and State Parks both say to request it at least 72 hours ahead; the Recreation.gov listing states no minimum and allows booking up to 90 days out, and one 2025 reader got approval the night before. The road is open daily 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., the HOA gate is locked Thursday through Sunday and opens with the permit combination, and you lock both gates behind you. State Parks lists no trailer parking and no camping.

Named obstacles

  • Entrance ledges — A Trails Offroad reviewer reports a Wrangler Sahara scraping on the entrance challenge, and another says some obstacles scrape a stock Gladiator Rubicon.
  • Ledge at waypoint 4 — Trails Offroad reviewers say this ledge has washed out and now rates harder than the rest of the road; one doubts an AWD vehicle can climb it.
  • Devil's Kitchen sinkhole spur — Optional side trip with a steep rock climb, per Trails Offroad. Reviewers say the approach can cause clearance problems.
  • Seven Sacred Pools — FunTreks reviewers mention a narrow section and a 270-degree turn down to the pools; the road is shared with hikers.
  • Locked gates and neighborhood streets — Access runs through residential streets and two locked gates; Arizona State Parks asks drivers to observe speed limits and minimize noise.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Ledges and step-ups

Guide: Lift & tire fitment — the clearance you can measure

Before:

  • Know your lowest point before you need to: oil pan, transfer-case skid, rear diff.
  • Air down. Low-20s psi puts more tread on the step.

After:

  • Look under every skid plate for fresh scrapes and rolled edges.
  • Check rocker panels and sliders; check the rear diff cover for dents.

Slickrock

Guide: Tire pressure & air-down

Before:

  • Air down for grip; slickrock rewards contact patch, not speed.

After:

  • Check sidewalls for cuts and chunking — sandstone edges eat them.

Tight brush

Before:

  • Fold the mirrors. Tape the paint edges if you care about them.

After:

  • Pull branches out of the suspension and check the brake lines and breather hoses.

Shared and one-way traffic

Before:

  • Commercial tours and the uphill rig have the right of way. GMRS or CB helps at the one-way sections.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Coconino National Forest — Red Rock Ranger District · 928-203-2900
  • Route: FR 9904 / 9904B (Soldier Pass Road), by permit only. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Pass / permit: Soldier Pass Motorized Use Permit through Recreation.gov: a maximum of 12 permits a day, one vehicle per permit, a $6 non-refundable reservation fee, and applications up to 90 days ahead. The Forest Service OHV page and Arizona State Parks both say the permit must be requested at least 72 hours in advance. The Recreation.gov listing states no minimum lead time, and one 2025 reader report describes approval the night before, so plan on the 72-hour rule. The permit covers road access only and does not authorize parking at the Soldier Pass Trailhead. Individuals only; businesses, clubs and organizations may not apply. Carry the permit and lock both gates behind you.
  • Length: 2.5 mi — 2.5 miles round trip per the Forest Service. Recreation.gov describes FR 9904 itself as a one-mile road, and onX and Trails Offroad measure 0.9 and 1.49 miles, so the 2.5 figure likely counts the neighborhood access streets.
  • Season: Permits are available year-round and the road is open daily from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. only. The HOA gate is open Monday through Wednesday and locked Thursday through Sunday, when the permit's combination opens it; during spring break, mid-February through April, it is closed seven days a week. The road may be closed in wet weather. Trails Offroad logged temporary closures for the Pocket fire in June 2026 and a gated parking lot in August 2026.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
