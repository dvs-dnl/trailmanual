Short answer

Montana Mountain is a 29-mile loop on Tonto National Forest roads around the Hewitt Station OHV Area, through a creek canyon, tight switchbacks and a long descent. Guides rate it mild to moderate, with conditions that swing between graded and rutted. Check the status of Hewitt Station Road before you commit to a loop.

Status unknown as of 2026-10-30 — source. The Tonto National Forest alerts page lists 31 notices and none names Hewitt Station, Queen Valley, Montana Mountain or FR 357, but it is not a route status list and the Forest Service says no Motor Vehicle Use Map is available. The page shows no closure here and does not show the route as open, so status stays unknown. Confirm with Tonto National Forest — Mesa Ranger District at 480-610-3300 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

FunTreks says a stock 4x4 with high ground clearance can manage the route when dry with an experienced driver, and AZ Off-Road says most SUVs with good clearance will make it and recommends 4WD. Some reviewers turned back in unmodified vehicles, and others recommend a lifted 4WD with larger tires and possibly lockers; a Jeep Rubicon and an F250 finished it. No source calls for low range. Skid plates are recommended here because of the reported ruts and rocks. No source states a wheelbase limit.

The trail

Montana Mountain is a loop of about 29 miles in the Tonto National Forest's Mesa Ranger District, starting on Hewitt Canyon Road near Queen Valley and circling the Hewitt Station OHV Area. AZ Off-Road's mileage log heads north across a wide wash, follows a usually dry creek into a scenic canyon with several crossings, passes a natural rock arch and a small stone cabin, then climbs past cattle tanks to a tight switchback and into the forest. Camping spots appear near mile 13.5, an overlook near mile 14, and the high point near mile 14.7. A long switchback descent follows, dropping more than 1,100 feet over about two miles, and the route crosses the Arizona Trail before reaching Hewitt Station Road.

Conditions have varied widely. FunTreks says the lower section is graded road with embedded rock and the upper sections are steeper, narrower, and muddy and rutted when wet. Reviewers report deep erosion ruts, washouts, tight off-camber switchbacks, drop-offs and heavy traffic. In May 2025 a passenger reported rocks over two feet and advised against the loop; in August 2025 another report said maintenance graded and widened the trail and removed obstacles. Snow has turned drivers around, and the creek crossings can be impassable after rain. A stock 4x4 with high clearance can manage it when dry, according to FunTreks.

Check the exit before you start. Guides report Hewitt Station Road (FR 357) closed to through traffic since 2017, with a bypass on FR 252 and FR 1904 for OHVs and conflicting later reports. The Forest Service says no Motor Vehicle Use Map is available for the Tonto, and its alerts page names no closure for this area today, though that page is not a route status list. Call the Mesa Ranger District before you go.

Named obstacles

  • Creek crossings — The route follows a usually dry creek into a canyon and crosses it several times after about 3.4 miles; it may be impassable after rain.
  • Roger's Trough — A rocky spot where FunTreks reviewers turned back in 2020 and 2022 because of washed-out rock.
  • Upper switchbacks — Tight, off-camber switchbacks with drop-offs near the high point. A 2025 report cites rocks over two feet and sections about six feet wide; an August 2025 report says maintenance graded and widened the trail.
  • Long switchback descent — AZ Off-Road logs the steepest descent near mile 18.4, with numerous switchbacks over about two miles and more than 1,100 feet of drop.
  • Arizona Trail crossing — The route crosses the Arizona Trail near mile 22.8; stay off the trail.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Steep grades

Before:

  • Shift to 4LO before the grade, not on it.

After:

  • Check transmission fluid level and smell after a long descent in gear.

Off-camber

Before:

  • Load low and strapped. Fuel and water on the floor, not the roof.

After:

  • Check that the roof rack and recovery gear mounts did not shift.

Shelf road

Before:

  • Brakes: firm pedal, full reservoir, pads with life in them. Downhill traffic yields.

After:

  • Feel the rotors for heat checking; smell for cooked pads.

Water crossings

Before:

  • Walk it first. Know where your intake and axle breathers sit.

After:

  • Check differential and transfer-case fluid for a milky look; dry the brakes before the pavement.

Mud

Before:

  • Recovery points front and rear, rated, with a strap you can reach.

After:

  • Wash the undercarriage and radiator fins; mud holds heat and moisture against steel.

Snow and ice

Before:

  • Check the land manager's seasonal closure before you leave the pavement.

After:

  • Rinse the salt and cinders off the brake lines and frame.

Shared and one-way traffic

Before:

  • Commercial tours and the uphill rig have the right of way. GMRS or CB helps at the one-way sections.
