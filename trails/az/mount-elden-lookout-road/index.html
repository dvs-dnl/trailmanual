Short answer

Mount Elden Lookout Road (FR 557) climbs about 2,000 feet up the west side of Mount Elden above Flagstaff on graded gravel, with switchbacks, hairpins and a gate below the summit. As of October 30, 2026 the Forest Service status page lists the road as closed to motor vehicles, and Arizona State Parks says snow usually closes it in winter.

Closed as of 2026-10-30 — source. Flagstaff section of the Forest Roads Status table lists FR 557 (Elden Lookout Road) as CLOSED with no note; the page says closed means closed to motorized vehicles unless noted and was last updated 2026-10-22. The Coconino alerts page retrieved today has no alert for FR 557, so the reason and any reopening date are not stated. Confirm with Coconino National Forest — Flagstaff Ranger District at 928-526-0866 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

The trail

Elden Lookout Road is the motorized way up Mount Elden, the ridge that rises north of Flagstaff. The route starts on paved Schultz Pass Road east of Highway 180, where Arizona State Parks lists a staging area. A guide says the pavement runs for about the first 1.3 miles before the gravel begins. From there the road climbs steadily past 9,100 feet with switchbacks and hairpin turns, occasional ruts and washboarding, and a series of gullies. A gate blocks the last stretch to the summit, so drivers park and walk the rest. Expect to share the road with other vehicles and mountain bikers.

Distances differ by source. Arizona State Parks puts the experience at about 10 miles round trip, while another guide measures about 7 miles each way. The sources agree that the grade is the main demand, not the surface. One guide advises against passenger cars and says a high-clearance vehicle is needed, though two-wheel drive is acceptable. A second guide says unlicensed vehicles are allowed past the end of the pavement.

Access is the bigger question right now. The Forest Service road status page, last updated October 22, 2026, lists FR 557 as closed and explains that closed means closed to motorized vehicles. It gives no reason and no reopening date, and the Coconino alerts page shows no separate notice. Arizona State Parks says snow usually closes the road in winter, and guides list a season of roughly May through November. This is Coconino National Forest, Flagstaff Ranger District. Call 928-526-0866 for the current word before you drive.

Named obstacles

  • Switchbacks and hairpins — Arizona State Parks describes a graded road with switchbacks and hairpin turns above 9,100 feet. A guide adds a sharp right turn near mile 4.
  • Gullies, ruts and washboard — A guide reports a series of gullies and occasional ruts and washboarding on the gravel, with a steep grade throughout.
  • Gate below the summit — A guide reports that a gate blocks the final approach, so vehicles park and the last stretch is on foot.
  • Shared road — Arizona State Parks says drivers share the road with other vehicles and mountain bikers.

AZ Off-Road scores the road 3 of 10, which falls in the 1 to 3 band that maps to Mild, and FunTreks puts the Elden Mountain and Schultz Pass trail in its lowest tier. Arizona State Parks describes a graded road and the Forest Service gives no rating. The 2,000-foot climb and hairpins ask for care and some clearance, but no rock obstacles are described, so Mild (1).

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Steep grades

Before:

  • Shift to 4LO before the grade, not on it.

After:

  • Check transmission fluid level and smell after a long descent in gear.

Washboard

Guide: Death wobble diagnostic

Before:

  • Grab the track bar and tie-rod ends and try to move them. Any play, fix it first.

After:

  • Re-torque the track bar bolts and check tie-rod ends and ball joints; washboard loosens all three. The death wobble diagnostic covers the sequence.

Snow and ice

Before:

  • Check the land manager's seasonal closure before you leave the pavement.

After:

  • Rinse the salt and cinders off the brake lines and frame.

Shared and one-way traffic

Before:

  • Commercial tours and the uphill rig have the right of way. GMRS or CB helps at the one-way sections.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Coconino National Forest — Flagstaff Ranger District · 928-526-0866
  • Route: FR 557 (Elden Lookout Road). Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Pass / permit: None.
  • Length: 10 mi — about 10 miles round trip per Arizona State Parks; another guide measures about 7 miles each way (14 round trip), so the total depends on where you start counting
  • Season: Arizona State Parks says the road is usually closed in winter because of snow, and a guide lists a typical season of May 1 to November 30. As of 2026-10-30 the Forest Service road status page lists FR 557 as closed, defines closed as closed to motorized vehicles, and gives no reason or reopening date. Call the Flagstaff Ranger Station at 928-526-0866 before planning a drive.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
