The Rolls OHV Permit Zone

Short answer

The Rolls is a Mesa Ranger District permit zone of about 150 miles of routes near Saguaro Lake, from a graded seven-mile road to steep rutted ridge runs that end at Salt River coves. A Tonto NF OHV Permit applies in the zone but not on Four Peaks Road beside it.

Open as of 2026-10-30 — source. Zone page lists the Pobrecito, Four Peaks, Palo Fierro and Cottonwood staging areas as Site Open, and the Tonto NF alerts page lists no closure covering the zone. A Tonto NF OHV Permit is required for motorized use in the zone, not on Four Peaks Road. Arizona State Parks still shows no permit for this area; the Forest Service pages and permit FAQ govern. Confirm with Tonto National Forest — Mesa Ranger District at 480-610-3300 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

The zone mixes graded roads with steep, rutted routes. In September 2026 a stock Bronco Badlands drove Roller Coaster both ways in low range, with one skid-plate touchdown and a rear locker near the top of the climb. onX text from late 2022 says lockers or a heavily modified vehicle are likely needed there. Rolls Road suits side-by-sides and high-clearance 4x4s. No source gives a tire size; the value is a conservative reading of that guidance.

The trail

The Rolls is a 24,000-acre zone on the Mesa Ranger District with about 150 miles of motorized routes that vary in width and challenge, near Saguaro Lake. Four staging areas open onto it. Three sit on Four Peaks Road (Forest Road 143): Four Peaks, Palo Fierro and Cottonwood. Pobrecito is reached from Bush Highway and is the usual starting point for the trails that run down to the Salt River coves.

The Forest Service now draws a line between the zone and the road beside it. Four Peaks Road and the Great Western Trail section on the zone's north edge lie outside the boundary and need no Tonto NF OHV Permit. Riding into the zone from a staging area does need one, and the permit has applied since January 1, 2025. An older Arizona State Parks listing still says no permit is required, so go by the Forest Service.

Difficulty depends on the route you pick. onX rates Rolls Road, a seven-mile point-to-point, 1 of 10, the 12-mile Rolls East loop 6, and Roller Coaster, a ridge route that ends at a Salt River cove, 5. Reports on Roller Coaster describe two-foot ruts, steep nose-down descents on loose soil, deep sand in the washes and thick mud near the water. A stock Bronco Badlands drove it in low range with a rear locker near the top. onX suggests the central corridor to avoid crowds on the east and west sections.

Named obstacles

  • Roller Coaster descents — onX describes steep nose-down descents on loose soil with two-foot ruts on this ridge route to a Salt River cove; approach drops head on.
  • Sticky mud and deep sand at the water — onX warns of deep sand in the washes and thick mud near the Salt River on the last third of Roller Coaster.
  • Rolls East crossing — Cutting across to The Rolls East brings some rock crawling, per onX.
  • Staging area split — Pobrecito, off Bush Highway, is the usual start for the cove trails. The three staging areas on Four Peaks Road sit beside the zone, and the permit applies once you ride south into it.

The Forest Service gives no rating beyond saying routes vary. onX scores the named Rolls routes 1, 5 and 6, which map to 1, 2 and 2 to 3, so the zone is rated Moderate (2) for its central routes. The steep, rutted Roller Coaster and Rolls East routes sit at the top of that range.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Steep grades

Before:

  • Shift to 4LO before the grade, not on it.

After:

  • Check transmission fluid level and smell after a long descent in gear.

Deep sand

Guide: Tire pressure & air-down

Before:

  • Air down further than you think. Carry a board or traction mat to jack against.

After:

  • Pull the air filter and look. Check brake dust shields and rotors for packed sand.

Mud

Before:

  • Recovery points front and rear, rated, with a strap you can reach.

After:

  • Wash the undercarriage and radiator fins; mud holds heat and moisture against steel.

Shared and one-way traffic

Before:

  • Commercial tours and the uphill rig have the right of way. GMRS or CB helps at the one-way sections.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Tonto National Forest — Mesa Ranger District · 480-610-3300
  • Route: The Rolls OHV Permit Zone; the Forest Service zone page lists no route numbers. Four Peaks Road (FR 143) and the Great Western Trail lie outside the zone.. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Pass / permit: Tonto NF OHV Permit, sold only on Recreation.gov (3-day $10, 6-month $60, annual $80); not sold at district offices. One permit covers all four permit zones (Bulldog Canyon, The Rolls, St. Clair, Desert Vista). Every motorized vehicle in a zone needs at least one rider holding a permit in their driver-license name; vehicles parked at staging areas do not. Qualifying off-highway vehicles also need an Arizona OHV decal. Required since 2025-01-01. Four Peaks Road (FR 143) and the Great Western Trail section on the zone's north edge are outside the zone and need no Tonto permit.
  • Length: 150 mi — about 150 miles of motorized routes in a 24,000-acre zone per the Forest Service; an older Arizona State Parks listing gives 65 miles and 27,000 acres, so the Forest Service figure is used
  • Season: The Forest Service rules call the Tonto NF OHV trails open year-round, subject to severe weather or special closures. onX lists winter, fall and spring for its Rolls routes.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
