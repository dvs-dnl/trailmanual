Short answer

Charlie Bell Road is the refuge's route west from Ajo into Cabeza Prieta National Wildlife Refuge: 17.3 miles from the visitor center to a hiking-only parking area at Charlie Bell Pass in the Growler Mountains. The refuge allows high-clearance two-wheel-drive vehicles and recommends four-wheel drive. It needs a free online permit, and the refuge has no services and no cell coverage.

Open as of 2026-10-30 — source. The refuge Visit Us page lists lands, roads and trails as open daily ('Always open'), and says a 1,500-foot strip of wilderness along the refuge's southern boundary is temporarily closed while all refuge trails and facilities remain open. It shows no alert for Charlie Bell Road. Refuge notices from 2002 and 2006 closed this road from March 15 to July 15 for pronghorn fawning; the pages opened today give no dates for this year. Confirm with Cabeza Prieta National Wildlife Refuge at 520-843-6867 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

The trail

Charlie Bell Road is the usual way into Cabeza Prieta National Wildlife Refuge from Ajo. The refuge road guide starts the log at the Cabeza Prieta Visitor Center, turns onto Rasmussen Road off Highway 85, passes several junctions with BLM dirt roads, and reaches the refuge entrance at mile 4.9. From there the road crosses open desert with teddy bear cholla and saguaros, passes a windmill and Little Tule Well at mile 8.5, and ends at the Charlie Bell public use parking area at mile 17.3. Beyond that point the old road is for hikers only. The guide describes stunning views of the Growler Mountains along the way.

The refuge says two-wheel-drive vehicles with high clearance may use the road, but four-wheel drive is recommended. A 2011 field report from a 4WD Jeep found washboard, many dry sandy washes, a few partly washed-out stretches and a very rocky last third, and took 1 hour 40 minutes each way. Every visitor needs a free permit obtained online, vehicles must be street legal, registered and insured, the speed limit is 25 mph, and the refuge asks for 15 mph near the pronghorn recovery pen. The rules page does not allow groups of more than four vehicles.

There is no fuel, drinking water, restroom or cell service on the refuge, and the refuge recommends two spare tires and two gallons of water per person per day in summer. A 2006 refuge notice closed this road from March 15 to July 15 for pronghorn fawning, so confirm spring access with the refuge.

Named obstacles

  • Early junctions — The refuge road guide lists a Y at Rosedale Road (mile 0.8), BLM 8112F (stay right, mile 1.9), Price Ranch Road (stay left, mile 2.7) and BLM 8112E (stay left, mile 2.9) before the refuge entrance at mile 4.9.
  • Washboard and washes — A 2011 field report describes washboard, many dry sandy washes and a few partly washed-out stretches that were still passable.
  • Rocky last third — The same 2011 report calls the first two thirds the better part and the last third very rocky, taking 1 hour 40 minutes for the 17 miles.
  • Pronghorn recovery pen stretch — The refuge asks for 15 mph here. Its rules page puts the zone approximately 11.5 miles west of the 'Cabeza entrance' and says it runs one mile.
  • Daniel's Arroyo — Named on the refuge road guide at mile 15.3.
  • Charlie Bell public use parking area — Mile 17.3. Hiking only beyond this point. The guide lists Childs Valley and Jack's Well administrative roads (126 and 125) shortly before it, and the refuge says administrative roads are for government use only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Washboard

Guide: Death wobble diagnostic

Before:

  • Grab the track bar and tie-rod ends and try to move them. Any play, fix it first.

After:

  • Re-torque the track bar bolts and check tie-rod ends and ball joints; washboard loosens all three. The death wobble diagnostic covers the sequence.

Rock garden

Guide: Lift & tire fitment — the clearance you can measure

Before:

  • A spotter beats a locker. Pick the line on foot.

After:

  • Sight down the tie rod and drag link for a bend; check toe by eye against a straight edge.

Desert heat

Guide: Fuel range planner

Before:

  • A gallon of water per person per day is the Forest Service minimum. Check the coolant level and fan clutch before summer runs.

After:

  • Watch the temperature gauge on the drive home; a weak fan clutch shows up in traffic, not on the trail.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Cabeza Prieta National Wildlife Refuge · 520-843-6867
  • Route: No route number found. The refuge manages Charlie Bell Road by name as a public use road; its road guide logs the administrative and side roads off it as 125, 126 and 127.. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fws.gov
  • Pass / permit: Free refuge permit for every visitor, obtained online: create a Recaccess account and list each vehicle, and keep a copy with you at all times. The refuge home page calls it a Barry M. Goldwater Range access permit. The road guide starts Charlie Bell Road at the refuge visitor center in Ajo and does not route it through the range; only its two I-8 entrances to El Camino del Diablo do. The refuge rules page does not allow groups of more than 4 vehicles.
  • Length: 17.3 mi — 17.3 miles one way from the Cabeza Prieta Visitor Center in Ajo to the Charlie Bell public use parking area (refuge road guide), about 35 miles round trip. The refuge entrance is at mile 4.9, so about 12 miles are on the refuge. Beyond the parking area the old road is open to hikers only.
  • Season: The refuge lists lands, roads and trails as open daily and calls late fall, early spring and winter the best time to visit. A 2006 refuge notice closed Charlie Bell Road from March 15 to July 15 for Sonoran pronghorn fawning, and a 2002 notice called that closure annual. The pages opened now give no closure dates, so ask the refuge before a spring trip. Summer heat runs well above 100 degrees. The refuge asks drivers to slow to 15 mph through the stretch near the pronghorn recovery pen.
  • Cell coverage: none
