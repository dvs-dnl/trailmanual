Short answer

Log Corral is MT-3456, a challenging wash-and-canyon route that runs west from SR 87 past a rock garden and springs to a ridge above the eastern shore of Bartlett Lake. Guides rate it Difficult at about five hours, lockers are the usual recommendation, and water or mud after rain can stop vehicles early.

Open as of 2026-10-30 — source. Staging area page lists the Log Corral OHV Staging Area as Site Open with year-round use, and the Tonto NF alerts page lists no closure covering MT-3456. The page asks drivers to check the weather and never cross flowing creeks. Field reports from October 2025 describe an impassable muddy crossing about a mile in after rain. Confirm with Tonto National Forest — Mesa Ranger District at 480-610-3300 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

The trail

Log Corral is MT-3456, a route that starts at the Log Corral OHV Staging Area off SR 87, about 24 miles past Fountain Hills, and runs west through washes and canyons toward the eastern shore of Bartlett Lake. The Forest Service calls it a unique and challenging route and notes that the surface changes every time the wash flows. The route also connects to trail systems north and south.

Drivers describe a long run in. The start crosses and follows the Sycamore Creek wash, then turns north onto a narrow track lined with brush. About a mile in, the trail enters a rock garden where most obstacles have two lines and there is no room to turn around, and two small springs keep parts of it wet. Beyond it the track twists through boulders and trees, then climbs for several miles on open dirt to a ridge with a gate at the old log corral and a view of the lake. A crossroads near the shore leads to a cove. FunTreks rates the route Difficult and logs 25 miles and five hours. Trails Offroad also calls it difficult, out and back, about five hours, and says the end of the trail offers remote camping.

Water changes everything here. In October 2025 two reports described an impassable mud crossing about a mile from the staging area, and a May 2025 report found a two-step ledge with no bypass. Lockers and a lifted rig are the usual recommendation, and several reports say not to run it alone.

Named obstacles

  • Sycamore Creek wash start — The route begins by crossing and following the wash, which onX says can run for a mile or two. Reports in October 2025 describe a thick, swampy mud crossing about a mile in that stopped many vehicles.
  • Rock garden — About a mile past the turn north. onX says most obstacles have two lines and there is no room to turn around, and two springs keep parts of it wet.
  • Two-step rock ledge — A May 2025 onX report found one ledge with no bypass and loose stacked rock that made traction poor; a locked Samurai on 33s used a winch.
  • Log corral and gates — After a climb of a few miles you reach the old log corral and a gate, with a view of Bartlett Lake and another gate before the crossroads near the shore.

The Forest Service calls MT-3456 challenging, Trails Offroad and FunTreks both rate it Difficult, and onX scores a section 6 of 10, which maps to Difficult (3). Reports of lockers, winch use at one ledge and water-dependent mud keep it at 3 rather than a built-rig-only rating.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Rock garden

Guide: Lift & tire fitment — the clearance you can measure

Before:

  • A spotter beats a locker. Pick the line on foot.

After:

  • Sight down the tie rod and drag link for a bend; check toe by eye against a straight edge.

Water crossings

Before:

  • Walk it first. Know where your intake and axle breathers sit.

After:

  • Check differential and transfer-case fluid for a milky look; dry the brakes before the pavement.

Steep grades

Before:

  • Shift to 4LO before the grade, not on it.

After:

  • Check transmission fluid level and smell after a long descent in gear.

Deep sand

Guide: Tire pressure & air-down

Before:

  • Air down further than you think. Carry a board or traction mat to jack against.

After:

  • Pull the air filter and look. Check brake dust shields and rotors for packed sand.

Mud

Before:

  • Recovery points front and rear, rated, with a strap you can reach.

After:

  • Wash the undercarriage and radiator fins; mud holds heat and moisture against steel.

Tight brush

Before:

  • Fold the mirrors. Tape the paint edges if you care about them.

After:

  • Pull branches out of the suspension and check the brake lines and breather hoses.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Tonto National Forest — Mesa Ranger District · 480-610-3300
  • Route: MT-3456 (Log Corral Trail), entered from the Log Corral OHV Staging Area off SR 87 in the Lower Sycamore OHV Recreation Area. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Pass / permit: None.
  • Length: 25 mi — FunTreks logs 25 miles for Log Corral to Bartlett Lake; Trails Offroad lists an out-and-back route and no readable distance, so treat the figure as approximate
  • Season: The Forest Service lists year-round use. The surface changes whenever the wash flows, and water changes the difficulty. FunTreks prefers January through May; onX lists spring and fall. Do not cross flowing creeks.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
