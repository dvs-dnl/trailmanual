Short answer

Sleeping Princess is a BLM OHV trail that climbs about 1,600 feet up the Black Mountains above Lake Mohave in 3.2 miles of steep, off-camber, narrow and loose track with ledges and drop-offs. onX records a 27.9-mile through version from Golden Valley and rates it 8 of 10. BLM recommends non-stock four-wheel drives with short wheelbases.

Status unknown as of 2026-10-30 — source. BLM's Sleeping Princess OHV Trail page describes the trail and its hazards and lists no closure notice, but it states neither open nor closed and gives no route number. It covers the 3.2-mile OHV trail, not the longer Golden Valley approach recorded by onX. Confirm with Kingman Field Office — Sleeping Princess OHV Trail at 928-718-3700 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

BLM recommends non-stock four-wheel drives and four-wheel drives with short wheelbases, and calls the trail a ride for experienced drivers. onX recommends lockers on both axles for the climb, and one onX driver recommends at least 33 inch tires and one locker, though some groups with open differentials finished. onX warns that vehicle damage and rollovers are very possible, and Trails Offroad advises a spare tire or patch kit and careful tire placement to avoid sidewall cuts. No source states 4LO, skid plates, a lift height or a wheelbase number; those entries follow the ledge and rock reports and BLM's non-stock wording.

The trail

Sleeping Princess climbs the Black Mountains above Lake Mohave, west of Kingman. BLM describes the OHV trail itself as 3.2 miles with about 1,600 feet of gain: it reaches a wash at 1.4 miles, then follows the wash another 1.8 miles to the top of the range. The BLM trailhead is on Old Kingman Highway, off State Route 68 about 8 miles east of Bullhead City. onX records a longer point-to-point version, 27.9 miles from Golden Valley in the north to Highway 68 in the south. Its first 5 miles are paved and the next 15 are graded, so the technical part is the last stretch.

BLM lists steep grades, off-camber sections, narrow passages, loose surfaces, sharp turns and ledges or drop-offs, and says there is no shade or drinking water. Trails Offroad describes a wash start that gives way to rock climbs, small boulder fields, a pinch point that is tight for full-size trucks and a shelf road across a scree field. onX adds a hill that drops about 1,000 feet, a mile of narrow off-camber shelf road, ledges up to 6 feet, and two optional loops, Hell's Half Mile and the Bob Miller Extension, that it rates 8.

BLM recommends non-stock four-wheel drives and short-wheelbase four-wheel drives and says experienced ATV, UTV and motorcycle riders may also use the trail. One onX driver recommends at least 33 inch tires and one locker, though some groups with open differentials finished. BLM says weather is most comfortable from September through May.

Named obstacles

  • Wash start and rock climbs — Trails Offroad says the trail starts in a wide sandy wash, then becomes a series of solid and loose rock climbs and small boulder fields as it winds up the valley.
  • Major hill — onX describes a hill that drops about 1,000 feet with few places to level out, approached by a ridge of about 2 miles with views of Lake Mohave and Laughlin.
  • Narrow off-camber shelf road — About a mile, described by onX as the most nerve-wracking stretch. onX advises sending a radio-equipped person ahead to find a wide spot where a group can pull over. Trails Offroad mentions a shelf road across a scree field.
  • Pinch point — Trails Offroad reports a pinch point that is tight for full-size trucks.
  • Hell's Half Mile (optional) — An optional left-side loop under a quarter mile, rated 8 by onX, with a boulder area and an optional climb-over that onX says is not suited to 4-door Jeeps.
  • Bob Miller Extension (optional) — An optional right-side loop rated 8 by onX, with a steep drop, several ledges and boulder fields on its south leg.

onX rates the trail 8 of 10, which maps to Extreme (4). BLM calls it challenging with treacherous attributes and recommends non-stock, short-wheelbase four-wheel drives, which points the same way. onX user reports put the main line near 6 to 7 and the optional loops at 8 to 9, so a capable rig that skips the loops sits closer to Difficult. The rule and the land manager's non-stock wording decide it: 4.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

What this trail stresses — and what to check

Pack against the list in the Trail tool kit builder.

Ledges and step-ups

Guide: Lift & tire fitment — the clearance you can measure

Before:

  • Know your lowest point before you need to: oil pan, transfer-case skid, rear diff.
  • Air down. Low-20s psi puts more tread on the step.

After:

  • Look under every skid plate for fresh scrapes and rolled edges.
  • Check rocker panels and sliders; check the rear diff cover for dents.

Off-camber

Before:

  • Load low and strapped. Fuel and water on the floor, not the roof.

After:

  • Check that the roof rack and recovery gear mounts did not shift.

Shelf road

Before:

  • Brakes: firm pedal, full reservoir, pads with life in them. Downhill traffic yields.

After:

  • Feel the rotors for heat checking; smell for cooked pads.

Steep grades

Before:

  • Shift to 4LO before the grade, not on it.

After:

  • Check transmission fluid level and smell after a long descent in gear.

Rock garden

Guide: Lift & tire fitment — the clearance you can measure

Before:

  • A spotter beats a locker. Pick the line on foot.

After:

  • Sight down the tie rod and drag link for a bend; check toe by eye against a straight edge.

Desert heat

Guide: Fuel range planner

Before:

  • A gallon of water per person per day is the Forest Service minimum. Check the coolant level and fan clutch before summer runs.

After:

  • Watch the temperature gauge on the drive home; a weak fan clutch shows up in traffic, not on the trail.
