Short answer

Kelly Point is the end of NPS 1203, a 26-mile road from Mt. Dellenbaugh to a Grand Canyon rim that the NPS calls one of the roughest and slowest in the monument. It plans 4 to 5 hours one way in a Jeep, limits the road to UTVs and Rubicon or Bronco class rigs, and gates it to motor vehicles from December to early March.

The trail

Kelly Point is the end of NPS 1203, which the NPS calls one of the roughest and slowest roads in the monument, leading to a rim view over the western Grand Canyon. The road begins where Mohave County Road 103 meets the BLM and NPS boundary by Mt. Dellenbaugh and runs 26 miles one way to the rim. From the NPS gate to Waring Ranch it is usually appropriate for high-clearance 4x4s, but the meadow before the ranch holds deep mud and ruts after rain. At Waring Ranch the hard part starts with 100 yards of bedrock called Dragon's Teeth, and a few miles later the road crosses volcanic boulders the size of watermelons. Speeds run 5 to 15 mph for much of the way. The NPS plans 4 to 5 hours one way in a Jeep and 3 to 3.5 hours in a UTV.

The NPS strongly recommends only UTVs, Jeep Rubicons, Ford Broncos or similar, light dirt bikes, or heavily modified and lifted short-wheelbase 4x4s, and says stock 4x4 pickups and SUVs will not make it for lack of clearance. The road is gated and closed to motor vehicles from December to early March, and it is impassable when wet. A 2018 BLM and NPS release said winter permits may be available from the Chief Ranger when conditions are dry. At the end there are many flat sandy places to camp near the rim, with no permit or fee as long as you stay on the rim. The ruin of the Shanley ranch cabin is part of the story of cattle grazing here since the 1800s. Verizon service reaches the rim itself but not far from it, and the NPS says not to count on it for rescue.

Named obstacles

  • Mud and ruts in the meadow — Between Mt. Dellenbaugh and Waring Ranch the NPS says the road can have deep mud and ruts after rain, and that the whole road is impassable when wet.
  • Dragon's Teeth — A 100-yard stretch of bedrock at Waring Ranch where the difficult conditions begin, per the NPS. An onX reporter describes a 1 to 5 mph crossing of abrupt rock fins 6 to 12 inches tall.
  • Watermelon boulders — The NPS says there are multiple stretches over volcanic boulders the size of watermelons, starting a few miles past Waring Ranch, with speeds of 5 to 15 mph for much of the drive.
  • Winter gate — The NPS says the road is gated and closed to motorized vehicles from December through early March.
  • Waring Ranch and Shanley cabin — The NPS preserves the ruin of the Shanley ranch cabin as part of the Arizona Strip ranching story; onX places it at Waring Ranch, about 16.8 miles along the track.

The NPS calls it one of the roughest and slowest roads in the monument, limits it to UTVs, Rubicon and Bronco class rigs or modified short-wheelbase 4x4s, and says stock pickups and SUVs lack the clearance. Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

  • Wash the undercarriage and radiator fins; mud holds heat and moisture against steel.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Grand Canyon-Parashant National Monument (co-managed by the NPS and the BLM Arizona Strip District) · 435-688-3200
  • Route: NPS 1203 (Kelly Point Road), which starts where Mohave County Road 103 (CR 103) reaches the BLM and NPS boundary by Mt. Dellenbaugh, and runs to the rim.. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: nps.gov
  • Pass / permit: None.
  • Length: 52 mi — about 52 miles round trip from the NPS boundary at Mt. Dellenbaugh: the NPS gives 26 miles one way from there to Kelly Point. onX records a longer 31.3-mile one-way track that starts on CR 103 about 4.8 miles before the Mt. Dellenbaugh trailhead. Reaching CR 103 takes dirt roads from St. George (BLM 1069 and CR 5), which this figure does not include.
  • Season: The NPS scenic drives page says the road is gated and closed to motorized vehicles from December through early March. A 2018 BLM and NPS release said permits for winter motorized access may be available from the Chief Ranger when conditions are dry, and that the road normally reopens in March. The NPS says the road is impassable when wet, and that in the meadow between Mt. Dellenbaugh and Waring Ranch deep mud and ruts follow rain. Its safety page says roads at Mt. Dellenbaugh and Twin Point, above 4,000 feet, are mostly impassable from December through March.
  • Cell coverage: spotty
