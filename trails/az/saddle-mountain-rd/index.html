Short answer

Saddle Mountain Road is the pair of Kaibab Plateau forest roads, FR 611 and FR 610, that run about 13 to 14 miles east from AZ 67 to Saddle Mountain Overlook and the wilderness trailhead. The Forest Service calls both roads suitable for passenger cars, and Arizona Highways recommends high clearance, with a rougher final descent toward the rim.

Status unknown as of 2026-10-30 — source. The trail

Saddle Mountain Road is the way to the Saddle Mountain trailhead and overlook on the Kaibab Plateau, on the North Kaibab Ranger District of the Kaibab National Forest. The Forest Service trail sheet gives the route: about 27.5 miles south of the Kaibab Plateau Visitor Center on AZ 67, turn left onto FDR 611, go about 1.4 miles, turn right onto FDR 610, and follow it 12.3 miles to the end of the road and the trailhead. It calls both roads suitable for passenger cars, though the sheet is undated. Arizona Highways measures the same drive at about 13.4 miles one way, calls FR 611 rough in spots, and says FR 610 gets rougher as it descends toward the canyon rim around mile 10. It recommends a high-clearance SUV or truck, notes most cars can pass in good weather, and warns that fallen trees sometimes block the road.

The road ends at Saddle Mountain Overlook, with views into Marble Canyon and a short, steep hike to a better view of the summit. Arizona Highways reports several dispersed campsites near the overlook. The Saddle Mountain Wilderness begins at the road's end, and no motor vehicles may be operated inside it. onX rates the route 2 of 10, as gravel that starts wide enough for two vehicles and narrows to one.

Season matters here. AZ 67 is closed to the public from December to mid-May, and ADOT plans to close it at Jacob Lake on December 7, 2026. The Forest Service district map warns that roads become impassable when wet and that cell service is unreliable. Call the North Kaibab Ranger District at 928-643-7395 before you go.

Named obstacles

  • Rougher descent toward the rim — Arizona Highways says FR 611 is rough in spots and that FR 610 gets rougher as it descends toward the canyon rim around mile 10.
  • Fallen trees — Arizona Highways says fallen trees sometimes block the road.
  • Saddle Mountain Overlook and trailhead — The road ends at the overlook, with views into Marble Canyon and a short, steep hike to a better view of the summit. The Forest Service sheet says no motor vehicles may be operated in the Saddle Mountain Wilderness.
  • Width narrows to one vehicle — onX says the gravel starts wide enough for two vehicles and narrows to one vehicle length.

onX rates the route 2 of 10, which maps to Mild, and the Forest Service trail sheet calls both roads suitable for passenger cars. Arizona Highways recommends high clearance and describes a rougher descent near the rim, which fits a stock 4x4 with clearance. Land-manager wording and the guides agree, so Mild (1).

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Kaibab National Forest — North Kaibab Ranger District · 928-643-7395
  • Route: FR 611 to FR 610 (Forest Development Roads 611 and 610), ending at the Saddle Mountain trailhead and overlook. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: fs.usda.gov
  • Pass / permit: None.
  • Length: 27.4 mi — about 27 miles round trip from AZ 67: the Forest Service trail sheet gives about 1.4 miles on FDR 611 plus 12.3 miles on FDR 610 each way, Arizona Highways measures about 13.4 miles one way, and onX records 12.3 miles
  • Season: The Forest Service district map (5/30/2025) shows AZ-67 closed December to May 14 and warns that roads become impassable when wet, and the Kaibab alerts page says ADOT plans to close SR 67 at Jacob Lake on December 7, 2026. onX lists spring, summer and fall as the best time. The alerts page retrieved today lists only three staging sites as still closed under the fire order updated for April 17, 2026 and does not mention FR 610 or FR 611.
  • Cell coverage: spotty
