Short answer

Walnut Canyon is a roughly 20-mile loop off Battle Axe Road south of Superior, through sandy washes, steep rutted climbs and a Gila River ford. Arizona State Parks calls it moderate to difficult and says to plan 5 to 6 hours. A State Land permit is needed, and much surrounding land is private.

Status unknown as of 2026-10-30 — source. The BLM Tucson Field Office page shows no closure for this area (its one active closure notice is a border strip in Santa Cruz County) but does not list Walnut Canyon. Arizona State Parks lists Walnut Canyon as a BLM Tucson Field Office staging area without giving a status, and the BLM map page warns that some access routes cross private land. Status stays unknown until BLM confirms the route. Confirm with Tucson Field Office — Middle Gila Canyons Area at 520-258-7200 before you go; status changes with weather, fire restrictions and land-manager decisions.

Your lift, tires and lockers change the answer, so check your own rig.

FunTreks recommends modified vehicles; its reviewers ran 33 to 37 inch tires with 2 to 3 inch lifts and said lockers helped in places, and a December 2022 group needed lockers and hit skid plates several times. onX reviewers had 4-low and lockers helpful, a 2.5 inch lift on 33s still catching on rocks, and a Polaris RZR that could not finish because of a washout. A stock Jeep JK and a TJ and JT have also completed it, so a capable stock rig with a careful driver can pass. Reviewers suggest airing down. No source states a wheelbase limit.

The trail

Walnut Canyon is a loop of roughly 20 miles that starts on Battle Axe Road, off SR 177 about 10 miles south of Superior. Arizona State Parks describes a winding road with steep climbs and descents, mostly through low sandy washes, rated moderate to difficult, and says to plan on 5 to 6 hours. FunTreks adds red buttes, the White Canyon Wilderness boundary to the north and the Gila River to the south. The route is on BLM land with a short stretch of State Trust Land, where a State Land permit is needed.

The toughest section is the long, steep downhill between waypoints 5 and 6, which FunTreks reports as deeper and steeper after rain in 2022 and especially bad near the river bottom in 2018. The wash near the river is tricky, and the route is hard to keep track of there. Walnut Ford on the Gila River is passable when the flow is not too high. Reports through 2024 describe head-sized loose rocks, deep ruts, washouts and brush that scratches paint, and an onX reviewer in October 2026 could not finish the loop because of a washout. FunTreks suggests modified vehicles; reviewers ran 33 to 37 inch tires with lifts and said lockers helped in places. A stock Jeep JK has completed the loop in about 5 hours, so a capable stock rig can pass with care.

Access needs care. onX says much of the land around the route belongs to a mining company, that access is allowed for now, and that it can be revoked at any time; FunTreks adds that a spring at its fourth waypoint is reportedly in private use. The BLM Middle Gila Canyons map warns that some access routes cross private land without confirmed public access. The BLM page does not list this route or its status, so confirm with the Tucson Field Office before you go.

Named obstacles

  • Steep downhill between waypoints 5 and 6 — The toughest section per both FunTreks and onX. FunTreks reports deeper, steeper ruts here in 2022 and a bad stretch near the river bottom in 2018.
  • Walnut Ford on the Gila River — A river crossing that onX calls good when the flow is not too high.
  • Wash near the river — A tricky section where reviewers say the route is hard to keep track of.
  • Loose rock, ruts and washouts — FunTreks updates through 2024 report head-sized loose rocks, deep ruts and washouts; an October 2026 onX report did not finish the loop because of a washout.
  • Overgrown brush — Brush and low trees close to the track scratch paint, per FunTreks and onX.

FunTreks (Moderate) and onX (5 of 10) both map to 2, but the state agency page, which describes the BLM staging area, says moderate to difficult, a FunTreks reviewer would rate it Difficult, and onX reviewers put it at 6 or 7. Wording that reaches difficult outranks a guidebook, so the route is 3. Recent reports of deep ruts, washouts and a steep downhill support the higher value.

Trail Manual scale: 1 Mild — graded dirt or mild two-track. 2 Moderate — rocks, ruts, mild ledges; stock 4x4 with clearance and 4LO. 3 Difficult — ledges, slickrock steps, off-camber; clearance, tires and skid protection matter. 4 Extreme — built rigs only.

Before:

  • Check transmission fluid level and smell after a long descent in gear.

Tight brush

Before:

  • Fold the mirrors. Tape the paint edges if you care about them.

After:

  • Pull branches out of the suspension and check the brake lines and breather hoses.

Water crossings

Before:

  • Walk it first. Know where your intake and axle breathers sit.

  • Check differential and transfer-case fluid for a milky look; dry the brakes before the pavement.

Before you go

  • Land manager: Tucson Field Office — Middle Gila Canyons Area · 520-258-7200
  • Route: No route number found; known locally as Walnut Canyon, reached by Battle Axe Road off SR 177 about 10 miles south of Superior. Arizona State Parks lists it as a BLM Tucson Field Office staging area; a short stretch crosses State Trust Land, and onX reports that much of the surrounding land belongs to a mining company.. Stay on the numbered route; everything else is closed to motor vehicles.
  • Status page: blm.gov
  • Pass / permit: Arizona State Parks says to pick up a State Land permit before the trip because a short stretch crosses State Trust Land. Confirm current fees at land.az.gov. Non-street-legal OHVs also need the Arizona OHV decal (BLM Tucson Field Office page). onX adds that access across surrounding private land is allowed for now and can be revoked at any time.
  • Length: 19.5 mi — about 19.5 miles by onX and 19.4 by FunTreks; Arizona State Parks says roughly 20. Trails Offroad measures only a 4.47-mile connector section of the canyon.
  • Season: Trails Offroad lists the route as typically open year-round, with an open report in September 2026. FunTreks and onX favor spring and fall, and onX notes abundant wildflowers in spring. Heavy rain rutted and washed out the route in reports from 2022 through October 2026, and the Gila River crossing at Walnut Ford depends on river flow.
  • Cell coverage: unknown
