Common issues after lifting a JK

Each item below is one source's report, with its label. Owner reports are single experiences, not failure rates.

CV joints and front geometry

An independent front end changes CV and control arm angles as it lifts, so these findings matter more than the lift height.

  • ExtremeTerrain ranks likely causes from most to least probable: front track bar, ball joints, drag link and tie rod ends, upper and lower control arms, bushings, steering stabilizer, knuckles. Inspect the track bar anchors first; a new stabilizer may mask the real fault. Lifting reduces caster, which adds strain. The frame-mount track bar bolt needs about 125 ft-lb and a loose one can wear the frame hole. Track bar first Reviewer opinion ExtremeTerrain death wobble article, 2019-06-12
  • Buyer's guide says around 4 inches and up the rear driveshaft angle gets too steep for the stock slip yoke, so an SYE with CV driveshaft is needed; regearing (typically 4.56 or 4.88 with 35in+ tires) is listed from 4in. SYE/CV from about 4in Reviewer opinion ExtremeTerrain lift kit buyer's guide, 2026-06-24
  • Just For Jeeps says exhaust spacers are needed on 2012-18 JKs when shocks are longer than 24.5in. TeraFlex's 3in page repeats the 24.5in limit and lists exhaust spacers part 2610000 and a Rzeppa CV kit (1744014) for driveshaft work. Shock length over 24.5in needs spacers Manufacturer claim TeraFlex 3in kit page, 2026-10-30

Track bar and axle offset

A taller spring moves the axle sideways on its track bar. Some kits include an adjustable bar to re-center it.

  • Lifting moves the axle off-center; the retailer guide says lifted Jeeps need a longer track bar and, without a relocation bracket, an adjustable track bar to recenter the axle. Symptoms listed: drift when driving straight, vague steering, visible lateral offset, death wobble. Adjustable or longer track bar Reviewer opinion ExtremeTerrain track bar guide
  • Guide says JK track bar bolts shipped slightly undersized for the collar, wearing bolts and collars over time, and recommends correctly sized replacements. Replace undersized bolt and collar Reviewer opinion ExtremeTerrain track bar guide
  • Four of ten reviewers added geometry correction or drop brackets; two bought adjustable rear track bars; two drilled new rear sway link holes. One says the rear track bar and sway bars are not in the kit and caster adjustment was tricky. Extras reported: geometry brackets, rear track bar Owner report Quadratec reviews, 2022-06-04
  • Reviewers report 6-10 hour installs; one recommends TeraFlex lower front and upper rear control arms to fix alignment issues; one had a cross-threaded rear track bar bracket replaced free by TeraFlex and says instructions may be outdated. 6-10 h install Owner report Quadratec reviews and Q&A, 2021-04-17

Death wobble, caster and steering

Lift changes the angle of the control arms. These findings cover what that does to steering feel.

  • Retailer guide suggests about 4 to 6 degrees of positive caster for stable highway driving on lifted Wranglers and adjustable front control arms to get there; alignment required after any lift. Factory caster is about 4.2 degrees per the death wobble article. 4-6 deg caster target Reviewer opinion ExtremeTerrain lift kit buyer's guide, 2026-06-24
  • Clayton's Ride Right+ page says factory caster is about 4 degrees, that reaching 5.5 degrees may need upgraded front lower arms, and that much less caster can make steering light or vague. 5.5 deg may need lower arms Retailer claim Ratchets Off Road (Clayton listing), 2026-10-30
  • One owner says severe death wobble was fixed after the lift and the Jeep drives better than stock; another with prior AEV builds had front sag from a heavy bumper and winch, and was still testing the JKS setup. Death wobble reported fixed Owner report Quadratec reviews, 2022-06-04

Driveshafts, vibration and the transfer case

Lift steepens the driveshaft angle. On short wheelbases that is often the first problem to show up.

  • Rough Country's 2.5in kit page says 2012 and newer JKs need its exhaust spacer kit (part 1030) for front driveshaft clearance. Exhaust spacer part 1030 Retailer claim ExtremeTerrain (Rough Country 67930), 2026-10-30

Sway bar links

  • Owner reports the JKS rear brake line extension put the hard-line junction too close to the sway bar and a hard line broke on a trail; replaced with extended braided lines. Same owner estimated about 3in of lift (not measured) and fits 35s. Brake line broke on trail Owner report Quadratec reviews, 2020-07-18

Brake lines and bump stops

Droop stretches brake lines, and a lift changes where the bump stops land.

  • Buyer's guide lists extended brake lines at 3 inches and up because stock lines can stretch or bind at full droop, and suggests stainless lines. Needed at 3in+ Reviewer opinion ExtremeTerrain lift kit buyer's guide, 2026-06-24
  • TeraFlex's 3in coil kit page requires extended-length front brake lines (part 4353300) on 2007-2011 models. Part 4353300 on 2007-2011 Manufacturer claim TeraFlex 3in kit page, 2026-10-30
  • Buyer's guide says stock bump stops may be too short after lifting and extended stops prevent over-compression; it lists this need from the 2in band. Extend bump stops from about 2in Reviewer opinion ExtremeTerrain lift kit buyer's guide, 2026-06-24

Tires, fenders and gearing

  • Just For Jeeps says the TeraFlex 2.5in kit needs aftermarket wheels with 4.75in backspacing or less to run up to 35in tires. Backspacing 4.75in or less Retailer claim Just For Jeeps (TeraFlex 1351000), 2026-10-30

Articulation numbers

RTI scores depend on ramp angle, and CTI is MetalCloak's own method, so compare only numbers from the same test.

  • RTI is distance up a 20-degree ramp at the wheel center divided by wheelbase, times 1000. Driving Line says stock SUVs typically score 400-600. Scores depend on ramp angle, tire pressure and sway bar state, so compare only like with like. Stock SUV 400-600 Reviewer opinion Driving Line, 2015-07-01
  • MetalCloak claims its Duroflex joint allows up to 34 degrees of misalignment and 28 degrees more articulation than typical control arm joints; a diagram contrasts 68 degrees against 40 degrees for a Clevite-style arm. Manufacturer figures, not independently measured. 68 deg vs 40 deg (diagram) Manufacturer claim MetalCloak, 2026-10-30
